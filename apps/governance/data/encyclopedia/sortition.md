# Sortition

## What It Is

Sortition is the practice of selecting citizens for public office or civic duty through random selection, similar to how juries are selected in modern legal systems. Rather than election or appointment, sortition uses a lottery to choose who will serve in governmental roles.

The fundamental principle is that any citizen meeting basic eligibility requirements has an equal chance of being selected to serve. This contrasts with electoral systems where candidates compete for votes, or appointive systems where officials select their subordinates.

## History

Sortition was fundamental to Athenian democracy (508-322 BCE), where most public offices were filled by lot rather than election. The Athenians used a device called a *kleroterion* - a stone slab with slots for citizen tokens - to conduct random selections.

Key features of Athenian sortition:
- The *boule* (Council of 500) was selected annually by lot, with 50 members from each of the ten tribes
- Most magistracies and the jurors for the people's courts (*dikasteria*) were also chosen by lot
- Only military commanders (*strategoi*) were elected, as the Athenians believed military leadership required specialized expertise
- Citizens could serve multiple terms, but not consecutively

The practice largely disappeared after the fall of Athens, though elements persisted in medieval Italian city-states like Venice and Florence. Some modern experiments with citizens' assemblies (Ireland's Constitutional Convention, France's Citizens' Climate Convention) have revived interest in random selection for specific deliberative tasks.

## In the Benjamin Franklin Society

The BFS uses sortition as the primary method for constituting deliberative bodies, particularly the General Assembly.

### General Assembly

The General Assembly consists of 12 randomly-selected citizens serving year-long terms. Selection occurs from the pool of all eligible members.

**Eligibility requirements:**
- Must be a member in good standing
- Must have completed any required civic education
- Cannot currently hold another sortition seat
- Must not be serving a suspension

**Selection process:**
1. All eligible members are entered into the lottery pool
2. Random selection occurs at the designated time
3. Selected members are notified
4. Terms begin immediately

### Committees and Colleges

Sortition can also be used for committee selection, drawing from:
- The entire membership (general lottery)
- A specific college (e.g., Agriculture Committee from Agriculture College)
- A combination approach

### Rationale

The BFS employs sortition as a deliberate response to problems observed in contemporary American electoral democracy:

**Against career politicians**: By limiting terms and using random selection, the BFS ensures governance remains a temporary civic duty rather than a professional career. Assembly members return to ordinary life after their service, maintaining connection to the concerns of fellow citizens.

**Money-independent**: Random selection eliminates the need for campaign fundraising. Members owe nothing to donors or special interests and face no pressure to prioritize electoral advantage over sound policy.

**Reduces factionalism**: Without competitive elections, there are fewer incentives for coalition-building around personalities or partisan loyalty. Deliberation can focus on policy merits rather than political calculation.

**Difficult to corrupt**: While elections can be influenced through media spending, propaganda, and voter manipulation, random selection is far harder to corrupt. The only way to influence a sortition-based body is through rational persuasion during deliberation.

**Equality of opportunity**: Every member has an equal chance to participate in governance, regardless of wealth, charisma, or political connections. This stands in contrast to electoral systems where candidacy is effectively limited to those with substantial resources.

**Descriptive representation**: Over time, sortition produces governing bodies that statistically represent the membership's actual diversity of backgrounds, experiences, and perspectives - not just the subset wealthy or connected enough to run for office.

**Civic education incentive**: Knowing they might be selected at any time motivates all members to understand governance, not just those seeking office. This encourages broader civic literacy.
