# Eudaimonia

## What It Is

Eudaimonia (εὐδαιμονία) is the ancient Greek concept of human flourishing - living well and doing well in the fullest sense. The term combines *eu* (good, well) and *daimon* (spirit, deity), suggesting a life favored by good fortune or divine blessing, though philosophers understood it more as the result of virtuous living than divine intervention.

Eudaimonia is often translated as "happiness," but this translation is misleading. Modern "happiness" typically means subjective feeling - pleasure, contentment, positive emotion. Eudaimonia was something more: the actualization of human potential, the fulfillment of our nature as rational and social beings, living excellently over a complete life.

It was not a fleeting feeling but an objective state of affairs - a life well-lived according to virtue (*arete*), engaged in worthwhile activities, in community with others, across the full span of years. You couldn't be eudaimon from feeling good momentarily; you had to actually be living well, exercising your capacities excellently, over time.

Eudaimonia was the ultimate goal of human life - the *telos* (end/purpose) toward which all other goods were means. We seek wealth, health, friendship, honor, and knowledge not for their own sake ultimately, but because they contribute to eudaimonia. A flourishing life was what everything else was for.

## History

**Aristotle's philosophy:**

Aristotle (*Nicomachean Ethics*, circa 350 BCE) provided the most influential account of eudaimonia:

**Eudaimonia as virtuous activity:**
- Eudaimonia consists in activity of the soul in accordance with virtue
- Not a passive state of pleasure, but active engagement in excellent pursuits
- Requires both moral virtues (courage, temperance, justice, generosity) and intellectual virtues (wisdom, understanding, practical judgment)
- Must extend over a complete lifetime - one cannot judge someone eudaimon based on a single day or year

**Material preconditions:**
- Eudaimonia requires certain external goods: health, material security, friends, political participation
- Poverty, slavery, or isolation makes eudaimonia difficult or impossible
- But wealth beyond sufficiency doesn't increase eudaimonia - more money doesn't make you flourish more
- The goal was having "enough" - secure provision of needs, not unlimited accumulation

**Social nature:**
- Humans are *zoon politikon* (political animals) - we flourish in community, not isolation
- Friendship (philia) was essential to the good life - not mere acquaintance but deep mutual regard
- Political participation was part of flourishing - humans actualize potential through engagement in common affairs
- The city (polis) existed to enable citizens to live well, not merely to survive

**Practical wisdom (phronesis):**
- Eudaimonia required practical wisdom - the ability to deliberate well about what conduces to the good life
- Not following rigid rules, but exercising judgment about how to live in particular circumstances
- Developed through practice, experience, and habituation in virtue

**Key principle**: The good life is activity in accordance with excellence, in community, with sufficient provision of material needs, over a complete life.

**Other Greek schools:**

Different philosophical schools offered varying accounts:

**Stoics**: Eudaimonia consists in virtue alone; external circumstances (wealth, health, political status) are "indifferent." The sage is eudaimon even in poverty or exile because virtue is self-sufficient.

**Epicureans**: Eudaimonia is pleasure, but pleasure understood as tranquility (ataraxia) and freedom from pain, not sensual indulgence. Achieved through friendship, simple living, and philosophical contemplation.

**Cynics**: Eudaimonia requires freedom from social convention and material desire. Live simply, according to nature, rejecting artificial needs society imposes.

All agreed that eudaimonia was the goal, differing on what it required and how to achieve it.

**Medieval Christian transformation:**

Medieval Christianity absorbed Greek philosophy but transformed eudaimonia:

**Thomas Aquinas** (13th century):
- Accepted Aristotelian framework but reoriented toward supernatural end
- Ultimate eudaimonia was beatific vision of God in afterlife, not achievable in this life
- Natural virtues (Aristotelian) were insufficient; required theological virtues (faith, hope, charity)
- Earthly life was preparation for eternal happiness, not complete in itself
- Contemplation of divine truth became the highest human activity

This preserved the concept of human flourishing but made it ultimately otherworldly and dependent on grace rather than human effort and excellent activity in community.

**Modern dissolution:**

The Enlightenment and modernity gradually dissolved eudaimonia as a coherent concept:

**Utilitarianism** (Bentham, Mill, 19th century):
- Reduced good life to "utility" - pleasure and absence of pain
- Treated humans as pleasure-maximizing machines
- Lost the distinction between higher and lower pleasures, between flourishing and mere feeling good
- "Happiness" became subjective satisfaction, not objective flourishing

**Liberal individualism**:
- Rejected any shared conception of the good life as tyrannical imposition
- Each individual decides for themselves what makes them happy
- State should be neutral about conceptions of good life - just protect rights and freedom
- Lost the idea that humans have a nature that could flourish or fail to flourish

**Consumer capitalism**:
- Happiness became equivalent to consumption and preference satisfaction
- Advertising sold products by promising happiness/fulfillment
- GDP growth became proxy for social wellbeing
- Accumulation replaced flourishing as life's aim

**Subjective wellbeing research**:
- Psychology studied "happiness" as self-reported life satisfaction and positive affect
- Focused on feelings, not virtuous activity or excellent functioning
- Could be manipulated by drugs, delusion, or ignorance
- Lost connection between feeling happy and living well

**Contemporary condition:**

By the early 21st century, eudaimonia had largely disappeared from public consciousness:

- People pursued "happiness" understood as pleasure, comfort, excitement
- Success was measured by wealth, status, consumption
- Work was means to income, not expression of virtue or contribution to community
- Leisure was passive entertainment, not active engagement
- Social media addiction, substance abuse, and anxiety epidemics despite rising material wealth
- No shared language for "living well" beyond subjective preference

Studies showed rising rates of depression, anxiety, loneliness, and meaninglessness despite increasing GDP. Material prosperity didn't translate to flourishing because the culture had lost the concept of what flourishing meant beyond feeling good and having stuff.

## In the Benjamin Franklin Society

The BFS explicitly organizes to enable member eudaimonia - human flourishing understood in the Aristotelian sense. This is the fundamental purpose from which everything else derives.

### "Human Flourishing Is The Point"

The society's motto expresses this clearly: the point is not profit, not growth, not power, not pleasure - the point is that members flourish as human beings.

This means:
- Economic activity serves flourishing, not the reverse
- Institutions are judged by whether they enable members to live well
- Success is member flourishing, not organizational metrics
- The society exists for its members' good life, not for its own perpetuation

### Material Preconditions

Following Aristotle, BFS recognizes that flourishing requires certain material conditions:

**Basic provision**: Every member household needs reliable access to:
- Nutritious food
- Secure housing
- Healthcare
- Education
- Essential tools and equipment

The [[Oikonomia]] economy exists to provision these needs. Without material security, flourishing is difficult or impossible.

**Sufficiency, not unlimited wealth**: More wealth beyond sufficiency doesn't increase flourishing. BFS aims to provision everyone adequately, not to enable unlimited accumulation. The billionaire is not flourishing more than the person with secure sufficiency.

### Virtuous Activity

Eudaimonia consists in activity according to virtue - doing worthwhile things excellently:

**Meaningful work**: Labor should be contribution to community wellbeing, not just income generation. The farmer feeding neighbors, the teacher educating children, the builder constructing homes - these are virtuous activities that constitute flourishing, not merely means to paycheck.

**Craftsmanship and excellence**: Work should allow people to develop skills and exercise them well. The society values doing things excellently, not merely efficiently or profitably.

**Service to community**: Contributing to shared wellbeing through governance participation, mutual aid, cultural contribution. These activities are part of flourishing, not sacrifices of personal happiness.

### Social Nature

Humans flourish in community, not isolation:

**Friendship (philia)**: BFS recognizes that deep relationships are essential to good life. The society creates contexts for genuine friendship to develop - working together, deliberating together, celebrating together.

**Political participation**: Members engage in self-governance through [[Democracy]], [[Sortition]], [[Referendum]], and [[Petition]]. This participation is not a burden but part of flourishing - actualizing our nature as political animals.

**Shared life**: The society creates opportunities for collective activity - meals, festivals, assemblies, projects. The good life is lived together, not in private isolation.

### Practical Wisdom

The society supports development of practical wisdom (phronesis):

**Civic education**: Members learn to deliberate well about common good, not just follow rules or maximize private interest.

**Experience and habituation**: Participation in governance and community life develops judgment about how to live well together.

**Deliberation**: Structured processes for thinking through decisions collectively, considering multiple perspectives, arriving at wise courses of action.

The goal is not to produce rule-followers but to develop citizens capable of excellent judgment about how to live.

### Colleges as Communities of Excellence

BFS [[College]]s organize around domains of knowledge and practice, creating communities where members develop and exercise excellence:

- Agriculture College: excellent farming, deep knowledge of food systems, stewardship of land
- Healthcare College: excellent healing, medical knowledge, care for fellow members
- Education College: excellent teaching, understanding of learning, transmission of knowledge

These are communities of virtue - groups pursuing excellence together in their domains, contributing to shared flourishing.

### Time and Leisure

Eudaimonia requires time for worthwhile activities:

**Not overwork**: Flourishing requires leisure (scholē) - time for contemplation, learning, friendship, civic participation, cultural engagement. Overwork prevents flourishing even if it generates wealth.

**Active leisure**: True leisure is not passive consumption (watching TV, scrolling social media) but engaged activity - reading, discussing, creating, learning, participating. BFS creates opportunities for genuine leisure.

**Life balance**: Work provisions needs; leisure actualizes potential. Both are necessary for flourishing.

### Community over Competition

Eudaimonia is not a competitive good - my flourishing doesn't require your non-flourishing:

**Non-zero-sum**: Everyone can flourish together. The society enables collective flourishing, not competitive advantage.

**Mutual support**: Members support each other's flourishing through teaching, friendship, mutual aid, shared resources.

**Cooperative excellence**: Excellence is pursued together, not in rivalry. The Agriculture College sharing knowledge helps everyone farm better.

### Limitations and Failures

BFS recognizes that flourishing is fragile:

**Misfortune**: Illness, injury, loss can damage flourishing despite virtue. The society provides mutual aid to help members weather misfortune.

**Character development takes time**: People develop virtue gradually through practice and habituation. The society supports this development but can't force it.

**Incomplete realization**: Not everyone will achieve full eudaimonia even in good conditions. The goal is to enable flourishing, not to guarantee it.

**Honest about constraints**: The society operates within larger systems (capitalism, state power) that constrain what's possible. It does not promise utopia, but better conditions for flourishing than prevailing alternatives.

### Rationale

Organizing around eudaimonia as fundamental purpose addresses contemporary problems:

**Against consumerism**: Flourishing is not consuming more. The society provides sufficiency and focuses on virtuous activity, not accumulation or consumption.

**Against alienation**: Work as meaningful contribution, not just wage labor. People actualize themselves through excellent activity, not just earn income.

**Against isolation**: Flourishing requires community. The society creates thick social bonds, not just market transactions.

**Against meaninglessness**: Life has an objective purpose - to flourish, to live well, to actualize human potential. Not just subjective preference satisfaction.

**Against optimization**: Flourishing is not maximization. It's living excellently, in balance, in community, over time. Not grinding yourself to maximize any single metric.

The BFS returns to an ancient question that modernity abandoned: What is the good life for humans, and how can we organize to enable people to live it?

The answer is eudaimonia - flourishing through virtuous activity, in community, with material sufficiency, across a complete life. Everything else is either a means to this or a distraction from it.
