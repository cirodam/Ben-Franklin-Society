# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Deathcare Service
*Article XVII of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Death is the one experience every member of the community will face. How a community handles its dead says something about what it values. The Deathcare Service exists first as a public health obligation — the proper handling of human remains is essential to community safety — and second as a community expression of respect for the dead and care for the living who grieve them. For most of human history these functions were carried by the community itself, not by a commercial industry. The Ben Franklin Society recovers that community responsibility, strips it of exploitation, and grounds it in both competent practice and genuine human care.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Public Health Foundation.** The Deathcare Service's foundational mandate is public health. The proper handling, storage, and disposition of human remains is essential to preventing disease spread within the community. This obligation is not conditional on cultural preference, religious tradition, or family circumstance — it applies to every death and must be fulfilled regardless of other considerations. Everything else the Deathcare Service does flows from and around this foundational obligation.

**§2. Community Function.** The care of the dead and the support of the grieving are community functions that predate professional deathcare by millennia. The Deathcare Service recovers these functions for the community — providing competent, dignified care for the dead and practical support for the living without the exploitation that has characterized the commercial funeral industry. The Service exists to serve families and the community, not to extract value from grief.

**§3. Functions.** The Deathcare Service carries out its mandate through the following functions: immediate response and remains handling; preparation and storage; disposition; death records; grief support coordination; land stewardship; and inter-society coordination.

**§4. Oversight.** The Deathcare Service is overseen by the College of Mortuary Practice — a peer body of practitioners with competence in the handling of remains, grief support, and the community's relationship with death. The College sets technical and ethical standards for the Service and credentials its practitioners.

**§5. No Exploitation.** The Deathcare Service shall provide its functions to all members without exploitation of grief. Costs shall be transparent, services shall be clearly explained, and families shall never be pressured into choices that exceed their needs or wishes. The Service exists to fulfill a community obligation, not to generate revenue from the most vulnerable moments in members' lives.

---

## Article II — Immediate Response and Remains Handling

**§6. Response Obligation.** The Deathcare Service shall respond to every member death in a timely manner. Timely response is both a public health requirement — remains must be handled promptly to prevent disease — and a human obligation to the family and community. The Service shall maintain response capacity at all times including nights, weekends, and periods of community disruption.

**§7. Safe Handling.** All remains shall be handled in accordance with public health standards established by the College of Mortuary Practice in coordination with the Health Service. Safe handling protects the community from disease transmission and protects the practitioners who perform this work. Standards shall be maintained without exception.

**§8. Family Coordination.** The Deathcare Service shall coordinate with the deceased member's family or designated contacts from the moment of response. Families shall be informed of the process, their options, and their rights at every stage. No decision shall be made about a member's remains without family knowledge and, wherever possible, family participation.

**§9. Health Service Coordination.** The Deathcare Service shall coordinate with the Health Service on deaths that occur within Health Service facilities or that involve public health concerns. When a death raises questions about communicable disease, the Health Service and Deathcare Service shall work together to ensure appropriate handling and community protection.

---

## Article III — Preparation and Storage

**§10. Preparation.** The Deathcare Service shall prepare remains in accordance with public health requirements and the family's wishes within those requirements. Preparation methods shall be explained clearly to families. The Service shall support family participation in preparation where families wish it — the tradition of family members washing and dressing their own dead is honored where it is desired.

**§11. Storage.** The Deathcare Service shall maintain storage facilities adequate to hold remains safely between death and disposition. Storage facilities shall meet public health standards established by the College of Mortuary Practice. The Service shall maintain sufficient storage capacity for normal community death rates and shall have contingency plans for periods of elevated mortality.

**§12. Dignity.** Remains shall be treated with dignity at every stage of preparation and storage. The dead are members of the community and shall be handled as such. The Deathcare Service shall maintain standards of care that reflect the community's respect for its members even after death.

---

## Article IV — Disposition

**§13. Methods.** The Deathcare Service shall provide disposition options appropriate to the community's capacity, values, and available land. Burial and cremation are the baseline disposition capabilities the Service shall maintain. Natural burial — interment without embalming in biodegradable materials — shall be supported where land is available and community capacity allows. The Assembly shall determine what disposition methods the community supports and shall ensure the land and infrastructure necessary for those methods are available.

**§14. Family Choice.** Families shall have genuine choice among the disposition methods the community supports. The Deathcare Service shall present options clearly and honestly, without steering families toward any particular method for reasons of Service convenience or cost. The family's wishes, within the community's available capacity, shall govern disposition.

**§15. Religious and Cultural Practice.** The Deathcare Service shall accommodate religious and cultural practices around death and disposition to the greatest degree consistent with public health requirements. The community contains members with diverse traditions around death. The Service shall approach this diversity with respect and shall develop the capacity to serve it.

**§16. Community Gatherings.** The Deathcare Service shall support community gatherings around death — viewings, wakes, memorial services, and such other gatherings as families and communities wish to hold. These gatherings are not incidental to the deathcare function — they are how communities process loss and affirm their bonds. The Service shall facilitate them practically without imposing form or cost.

---

## Article V — Land Stewardship

**§17. Community Land.** Burial requires land. The Assembly is responsible for ensuring that the community has access to sufficient land for the disposition methods it supports. Land set aside for burial and disposition is held in perpetuity for that purpose — it is not subject to redevelopment or reallocation without extraordinary community process.

**§18. Stewardship.** The Deathcare Service is responsible for the stewardship of community land used for burial and disposition. Stewardship includes maintenance, record-keeping of interment locations, and ensuring that the land remains accessible and dignified for families who wish to visit. The dead remain members of the community's story and the land that holds them shall reflect that.

**§19. Natural Burial.** Where the community supports natural burial the Deathcare Service shall manage natural burial ground with attention to ecological health. Natural burial returns remains to the earth without chemical intervention — it is both the most ancient human disposition practice and the most ecologically sound. The Service shall develop natural burial capacity as a priority where land allows.

---

## Article VI — Death Records

**§20. Record Obligation.** The Deathcare Service shall maintain accurate records of every member death — date, cause where known, and disposition. Death records are community infrastructure. They inform the Central Bank's issuance function, the Social Insurance Fund's obligations, and the community's understanding of its own demographic health.

**§21. Central Bank Notification.** The Deathcare Service shall notify the Central Bank of every member death promptly. This notification triggers the cessation of birthday issuance for the deceased member and initiates such processes as the standing rules establish for the disposition of Frank holdings in deceased member accounts. Accurate and timely notification is essential to the integrity of the Frank.

**§22. Privacy.** Death records are held with appropriate privacy protections. Cause of death and personal health information about deceased members shall not be disclosed without family consent except as required for public health purposes or community statistical reporting in aggregate.

**§23. External Records.** The Deathcare Service shall coordinate with external record-keeping requirements — death certificates and such other legal documentation as external systems require. The Service shall assist families in obtaining necessary documentation without requiring families to navigate external bureaucracies alone in their grief.

---

## Article VII — Grief Support

**§24. Immediate Support.** The Deathcare Service shall provide immediate practical support to grieving families — helping with the immediate decisions that death requires, connecting families with community resources, and ensuring that families are not left alone to navigate the practical demands of death while in acute grief. This immediate support is a core service function, not an add-on.

**§25. Coordination.** Deeper and longer-term grief support is provided through the Health Service's mental health function, the College of Conciliation's community navigation role, and the Community Care Service where practical support is needed. The Deathcare Service shall actively connect grieving families with these resources rather than leaving connection to chance.

**§26. Community Grief.** When a death affects the broader community — the loss of a widely known member, a tragic or sudden death, a death that leaves many people grieving — the Deathcare Service shall coordinate with the College of Conciliation and other services to support community-wide grief response. Communities grieve as communities. The Service shall recognize and facilitate that.

**§27. Practitioner Support.** Deathcare work is emotionally demanding. Practitioners who handle remains, support grieving families, and sit with death regularly are exposed to sustained emotional strain. The Deathcare Service shall actively support its practitioners — through peer support, access to the Health Service's mental health function, and recognition of the weight of the work they carry. Practitioner wellbeing is not separate from service quality — it is essential to it.

---

## Article VIII — Contingency Capacity

**§28. Mass Casualty Planning.** The Deathcare Service shall maintain contingency plans for periods of elevated mortality — pandemic, disaster, acute crisis — in which deaths significantly exceed normal community rates. Mass casualty situations place extraordinary demands on deathcare capacity. The Service shall plan for these situations before they arise rather than improvising during them.

**§29. Surge Capacity.** Contingency plans shall include surge capacity for remains handling and storage, expedited disposition protocols that maintain public health standards, and coordination with the Health Service and Energy Service on the resources contingency deathcare requires. The community shall not be caught without deathcare capacity when it is most needed.

**§30. Inter-Society Coordination.** The Deathcare Service shall maintain relationships with the deathcare services of other Ben Franklin Societies for mutual support during mass casualty events, for the handling of members who die away from their home community, and for the transport of remains when families wish it. Death does not always happen at home. The federation network shall ensure that no member's remains are stranded without care.

---

## Article IX — Transparency and Reporting

**§31. Annual Report.** The Deathcare Service shall publish an annual report covering: total member deaths; disposition methods used in aggregate; land capacity and utilization; grief support referrals; inter-society coordination activity; contingency planning status; and any significant developments in deathcare capacity or practice. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§32. Cost Transparency.** The Deathcare Service shall publish clear and complete information about the costs of its services. No family shall face unexpected costs or pressure to spend beyond their needs. Cost transparency is both a practical protection for grieving families and an expression of the Service's commitment to operating without exploitation.

**§33. Member Privacy.** Individual death records and family information are private. Aggregate mortality data is reported publicly as community health information. Individual circumstances of death and family decisions about disposition shall not be disclosed without family consent.
