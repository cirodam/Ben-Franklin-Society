# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of Franklin County, Georgia
### Constitution
*Adopted by the Community on [Date]*

---

## Preamble

We are the people of Franklin County, Georgia. We are farmers and tradespeople, nurses and teachers, parents and neighbors. We have watched the institutions that were supposed to serve us fail — slowly at first, then faster. We have watched the distance between what the powerful promised and what ordinary people received grow wider with each passing year. We have watched our county hollowed out, our healthcare evaporate, our children leave, our neighbors struggle in isolation.

We decided to stop watching. We decided to build.

We take our name from Benjamin Franklin — not the myth but the man. Franklin did not wait for someone else to solve his community's problems. He organized his neighbors. He built a fire company, a library, a hospital, a philosophical society. He understood something that has been systematically forgotten: that ordinary people, working together, can build the institutions a community needs to survive and flourish.

We found the Ben Franklin Society of Franklin County in that spirit. We are not a political party. We are not an ideology. We are not a protest. We are a community that has decided to take care of itself — to build the institutions that make genuine self-sufficiency possible, to develop the knowledge and skills that keep a community functioning when external systems fail, and to do it together because none of us can do it alone.

This Constitution is the foundational law of the Ben Franklin Society of Franklin County. It governs how we govern ourselves. It is adopted by the community and can only be changed by the community. It answers to the Ben Franklin Society Charter, which we hold in common with all Ben Franklin Societies and which defines what we are.

We do not take any of this lightly. We resolve to meet it together.

## Article I — The Community

**§1. Origin of Authority.** All authority within the Ben Franklin Society of Franklin County originates with and belongs to the community in its entirety. No office, institution, or individual possesses authority by right of their own. Authority is held by the community, delegated for practical purposes, and may be recalled at any time. This is the foundational principle from which everything else in this Constitution derives.

**§2. The Community Defined.** The community comprises all members of the Ben Franklin Society of Franklin County in good standing. Membership is governed by the Society's Membership bylaws. Every member is the community. The community is every member.

**§3. Final Authority.** The community is the final authority over all institutions, bodies, decisions, and actions of the Society. The General Assembly, the Colleges, and the Services all operate at the community's pleasure and are subject to community override at all times. When the community speaks, all other bodies must yield.

**§4. Community Override.** Any member may petition for community review of any decision made by any body of the Society. Petition thresholds, referendum procedures, and community vote requirements shall be established in the standing rules. The right to petition and the right to community override are constitutional rights that no institution may abridge.

**§5. Delegation.** The community delegates the practical exercise of its authority to the General Assembly, and through the General Assembly to committees, Colleges, and Services. Such delegation is made for practical purposes only. It does not transfer ownership of authority. The community delegates. It does not abdicate.

## Article II — The General Assembly

**§6. Establishment.** The community delegates its primary legislative authority to the General Assembly. The General Assembly acts on behalf of the community and at the community's pleasure. It is the Society's primary deliberative and legislative body.

**§7. Composition.** The General Assembly shall consist of members drawn by sortition from the eligible adult membership of the Society. Assembly size shall be established in the standing rules as a proportion of the total eligible membership, with minimum and maximum bounds ensuring the Assembly is large enough to be representative and small enough to deliberate effectively. *[Placeholder: Assembly size formula to be established in standing rules.]*

**§8. Sortition.** Members of the General Assembly are selected by lot from the pool of all eligible adult members in good standing. Sortition is not merely a procedural choice — it is a constitutional commitment. The randomness of selection is the mechanism by which the General Assembly remains the community's body rather than any faction's instrument. No election, appointment, or hereditary process may substitute for sortition in selecting General Assembly members.

**§9. Terms.** General Assembly members shall serve terms of one year. No member shall serve consecutive terms without an intervening period of at least one year. Term limits prevent entrenchment. Rotation ensures that the General Assembly continuously draws from the full community rather than developing a permanent governing class.

**§10. Civic Obligation.** Service on the General Assembly is a civic obligation of membership. A member drawn by sortition is expected to serve. Grounds for declining shall be established in standing rules and shall be limited to genuine incapacity and material conflict of interest.

**§11. Legislative Authority.** The General Assembly holds the primary legislative authority of the Society, acting within the bounds of this Constitution and the Ben Franklin Society Charter. The General Assembly may establish, amend, or revoke ordinances of general application. It may establish Services, create and dissolve Colleges, appropriate funds, and delegate legislative authority to committees within defined domains.

**§12. Deliberation.** The General Assembly shall deliberate openly. Its proceedings shall be accessible to all members. Decisions shall be made through a deliberative process that gives all Assembly members genuine opportunity to contribute. The specific rules of procedure shall be established in the standing rules.

**§13. Limits.** The General Assembly may not amend this Constitution. It may not act contrary to the Ben Franklin Society Charter. It may not abridge the rights established in this Constitution. It may not concentrate authority in itself beyond what the community has delegated. All General Assembly actions are subject to community override.

## Article III — Committees

**§14. Establishment.** The General Assembly may establish committees to exercise delegated legislative authority within defined domains. Committees are how the General Assembly governs in detail — through smaller bodies with defined mandates drawn from appropriate pools.

**§15. General Sortition Committees.** Where a committee's function requires democratic legitimacy and community perspective more than domain expertise, the General Assembly shall draw from the general eligible membership by sortition. Accountability oversight, budget review, and policy questions affecting the whole community are examples of functions appropriate for general sortition committees.

**§16. Qualified Sortition Committees.** Where a committee's function genuinely requires domain expertise, the General Assembly shall draw from the membership of the relevant College or Colleges by sortition. Clinical standard-setting, agricultural governance, engineering oversight, and safety management are examples of functions appropriate for qualified sortition committees. The General Assembly determines which functions require qualified sortition and from which College pool members shall be drawn.

**§17. Mixed Committees.** The General Assembly may establish committees drawing from both College pools and the general membership, combining domain expertise with community accountability.

**§18. Committee Authority.** A committee's authority derives from the General Assembly's delegation, not from any College that supplied its members. Committees exercise real legislative authority within their defined domain. That authority is always bounded by the General Assembly's delegation and subject to General Assembly and community override.

**§19. Permanent Review Committee.** The General Assembly shall maintain a Permanent Review Committee drawn by pure sortition from the general eligible membership, excluding members of the College of Conciliation and College of the Charge. The Permanent Review Committee exercises independent binding oversight of the Constabulary Service and the College of Conciliation. Its mandate, authority, and procedures are established in the relevant bylaws.

## Article IV — The Colleges

**§20. Nature.** The Colleges are self-governing peer bodies through which the community acknowledges domain expertise and maintains qualified sortition pools for governance requiring specialized knowledge. The Colleges are subordinate to the General Assembly. They advise, credential, and supply qualified practitioners. They do not govern independently. The full framework governing the Colleges is established in the Colleges bylaws.

**§21. Establishment.** Colleges are established by the General Assembly by resolution. The General Assembly defines each College's domain, approves its charter, and holds it accountable. No College exists without General Assembly creation. The General Assembly may dissolve Colleges that have ceased to fulfill their mandate.

**§22. Community Acknowledgment.** The community, through the General Assembly, acknowledges the expertise of each College within its defined domain. This acknowledgment is an act of community sovereignty — the community choosing to recognize expertise it does not universally share and to draw upon it through qualified sortition. The acknowledgment can be withdrawn. The community retains authority over all College functions.

**§23. Accountability.** Every College is accountable to the General Assembly and ultimately to the community. Colleges shall report annually to the General Assembly. The community retains override authority over all College decisions and functions.

## Article V — The Services

**§24. Nature.** The Services are operational bodies through which the community fulfills its obligations to its members. Services are established by the General Assembly, funded through member dues, and accountable to the General Assembly and community. The framework governing Services is established in the Services bylaws. Individual Service mandates are established in their own foundational documents.

**§25. Foundational Services.** The following Services are foundational — they must be established at the Society's founding and maintained without interruption: the Central Bank, the Community Bank, the Treasury, and the Social Insurance Fund. These four Services constitute the Society's financial infrastructure and are prerequisite to all other community functions.

**§26. Essential Services.** The Society is obligated to establish and maintain Services providing healthcare, food security, and shelter as early as practicable. These obligations flow directly from the Ben Franklin Society Charter and may not be indefinitely deferred. The Society shall be continuously working toward fulfilling them.

**§27. Budget Process.** Services operate on annual budgets submitted to the General Assembly. The General Assembly appropriates funds from member dues. Services shall operate within their appropriated budgets and account to the General Assembly for all expenditures.

**§28. Open Participation.** Any member in good standing may work within any Service. Participation in Service work is a right of membership and shall not be gatekept. Named staff roles within Services are filled through nomination processes established in the Services bylaws.

## Article VI — The Frank

**§29. The Community Currency.** The Frank is the official currency of the Ben Franklin Society of Franklin County. It is the material expression of the community's economic sovereignty. The Frank is backed by the community's collective capacity — its labor, its services, its productive infrastructure, and its mutual obligations.

**§30. Issuance.** The Frank is issued on the basis of the person-year — two thousand Franks per member per year, deposited into the Treasury on each member's birthday. The issuance rate is equal for every member regardless of age, occupation, or capacity. The principle that every person's presence in the community generates equal value is expressed materially in Frank issuance.

**§31. Constitutional Status.** The Frank is a constitutional institution. The General Assembly may not abolish the Frank or substitute another currency without community ratification. The Central Bank's mandate to issue Franks faithfully per the membership roll is constitutionally protected from political interference. The full framework governing the Frank is established in the Frank bylaws.

**§32. Integrity.** The integrity of the Frank — the honesty of the count, the faithfulness of the issuance, the accuracy of the ledger — is among the Society's highest obligations. Manipulation of Frank issuance or the membership roll for political purposes is a fundamental breach of community trust and is constitutionally prohibited.

## Article VII — Membership

**§33. Open Membership.** Membership in the Ben Franklin Society of Franklin County is open to any person sixteen years of age or older who is willing to abide by the rules and expectations of the Society and to act in its spirit. The Society does not select its members. It receives all who genuinely commit.

**§34. Mutual Commitment.** Membership carries both rights and obligations. The member commits to the community — in dues, in conduct, in participation. The community commits to the member — in services, in governance, in care. This mutual commitment is the nature of mutual aid. The full framework governing membership is established in the Membership bylaws.

**§35. Constitutional Rights.** Every member holds the following constitutional rights which no institution of the Society may abridge: the right to participate in community governance; the right to petition any body of the Society; the right to due process before any deprivation of membership rights or standing; the right to access their own Community Bank account records; and the right to inspect the records of the Society's public institutions. Additional member rights are established in the Membership bylaws.

**§36. Human Dignity.** The unconditional human dignity established in Principle I of the Ben Franklin Society Charter is a constitutional constraint on every institution of this Society. No member shall be treated as less than fully human by any body, officer, or institution of the Ben Franklin Society. This constraint admits no exceptions.

**§37. Due Process.** No member shall be removed from membership, suspended from good standing, or deprived of constitutional rights without due process. Due process requires at minimum: clear notice of the matter at issue, a reasonable opportunity to respond, and a fair hearing before a body with appropriate authority. The community retains the right to review any such determination.

## Article VIII — The Ben Franklin Society Charter

**§38. Supremacy of the Charter.** The Ben Franklin Society Charter is supreme over this Constitution. Where this Constitution conflicts with the Charter, the Charter prevails. This Society is founded in accordance with the Charter and remains bound by it.

**§39. Charter Obligations.** This Society accepts and fulfills the obligations the Charter establishes — the eleven founding principles, the institutional minimums, and the commitment to hold the Charter collectively with all Ben Franklin Societies. These obligations are constitutional. The General Assembly may not act contrary to them.

**§40. Charter Amendments.** The Charter may only be amended by the consensus of all Ben Franklin Societies that hold it. This Society may propose Charter amendments through the federation process but may not unilaterally amend the Charter.

---

## Article IX — Supremacy and Document Hierarchy

**§41. Hierarchy.** The governing documents of the Ben Franklin Society of Franklin County operate in the following order of supremacy, from highest to lowest: the Ben Franklin Society Charter; this Constitution; the Society's foundational bylaws; standing rules adopted by the General Assembly. Each document must conform to those above it. In case of conflict, the higher document prevails.

**§42. Bylaws.** The Society's foundational bylaws establish the detailed framework for governance, currency, membership, services, and colleges. Bylaws are adopted by the General Assembly and must conform to this Constitution. Where this Constitution supersedes earlier bylaws, the Constitution governs.

**§43. Standing Rules.** Standing rules govern operational details — schedules, thresholds, procedures, and such other specifics as the General Assembly determines. Standing rules must conform to the Constitution and bylaws. They may be amended by General Assembly majority unless this Constitution or the bylaws require otherwise.

**§44. Conflicts.** Any member may petition the General Assembly to resolve a conflict between documents. The General Assembly shall resolve such conflicts in accordance with the hierarchy established in this Article. Community override is available for any General Assembly resolution of a document conflict.

## Article X — Amendment

**§45. Community Amendment.** This Constitution belongs to the community and may only be amended by the community. The General Assembly may not amend this Constitution. It operates under this Constitution — it cannot alter the document that defines its own authority and limits.

**§46. Proposal.** Any member may propose an amendment to this Constitution. Proposed amendments shall be submitted in writing to the General Assembly, which shall publish them to the full membership and schedule a community deliberation. The General Assembly may recommend for or against an amendment but may not block a community vote. *[Placeholder: petition threshold and deliberation period to be established in standing rules.]*

**§47. Ratification.** An amendment to this Constitution is ratified when approved by a supermajority of members participating in a community vote, provided a minimum quorum of the membership participates. *[Placeholder: supermajority threshold and quorum requirement to be established in standing rules.]*

**§48. Limits on Amendment.** No amendment may make this Constitution conform less fully to the Ben Franklin Society Charter. No amendment may remove the community's status as the origin and final authority of all power within the Society. No amendment may eliminate the right to due process or the unconditional human dignity established in Article VII. These provisions are the constitutional floor below which no community vote may go.

**§49. Effective Date.** A ratified amendment takes effect on the date specified in the amendment or, if no date is specified, thirty days after ratification. Amendments do not apply retroactively.
