# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on Membership
*Article III of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Membership in the Ben Franklin Society is the foundation of everything the Society does. Members are not customers of the Society's services, nor subjects of its governance. They are the Society itself — its origin, its purpose, and its final authority. Membership carries both rights and obligations. It is an act of mutual commitment: the member commits to the community, and the community commits to the member. This is the nature of mutual aid.

---

## Article I — Eligibility and Admission

**§1. Open Membership.** Membership in the Ben Franklin Society is open to all persons who meet the eligibility requirements of this Article and who are willing to abide by the rules and expectations of the Society and to act in its spirit. The Society does not select its members. It receives all who genuinely commit.

**§2. Age.** Any person sixteen years of age or older is eligible for membership. The Society recognizes that the capacity for civic participation, mutual obligation, and meaningful contribution to community life is present at sixteen. Full membership rights and obligations apply from the moment of admission regardless of age, subject only to such standing rules as the Assembly may establish for the welfare of younger members.

**§3. Geographic Expectation.** Members are generally expected to live within proximity of the community sufficient to render and receive physical aid. This is not a hard boundary but a relational expectation rooted in the nature of mutual aid. A community whose members cannot reach one another is a community in name only. Members living at significant distance should be understood as exceptions rather than the norm.

**§4. Admission.** A person becomes a member upon affirming their commitment to the rules, expectations, and spirit of the Society and upon registration with the Community Bank for establishment of their account. *[Placeholder: admission process and affirmation form to be established in standing rules.]*

**§5. Dependents.** Children under the age of sixteen residing with a member household are recognized as dependents of the Society and are entitled to the protections and care of the community. They are not full members but are not strangers. Upon reaching sixteen they may choose full membership on their own affirmation.

## Article II — The Spirit of the Society

Rules can be written. Spirit cannot. The following is offered not as a legal standard but as a statement of what membership means — what a member is committing to when they join, and what the community is committing to in return.

**§6. Mutual Commitment.** A member of the Ben Franklin Society is someone who has chosen to be part of something larger than themselves. They have committed not just to receive the community's support but to be part of the community's capacity to support. Membership is a two-way bond.

**§7. Good Faith.** Members are expected to act in good faith toward one another, toward the Society's institutions, and toward the community as a whole. Good faith means honesty, transparency, and genuine regard for the wellbeing of others. It means engaging with the community's processes earnestly rather than manipulating them.

**§8. Give and Receive.** Mutual aid is reciprocal. The Society does not compel participation, but it recognizes that the capacity to receive flows from the willingness to give. A member who contributes to the community builds the community's capacity to support them in return. This is not a transaction — it is the nature of community itself.

**§9. Human Presence.** The Society holds that human presence in the community generates value. A member need not be productive by the measures of the outside world to be a full and valued member of this one. The elderly, the disabled, the young, the recovering — all are full members of the community whose presence matters and whose needs the community is obligated to meet.

## Article III — Dues

**§10. Nature of Dues.** Membership dues are the community's primary means of funding its Services and fulfilling its obligations to members. Dues are not a fee for services rendered. They are a contribution to the common capacity of the community — akin in nature to taxation, and understood as such. The Society runs on the collective contribution of its members.

**§11. Denomination.** Dues are denominated and paid in Franks, the Society's currency. This ensures that the community's financial life circulates within the community.

**§12. Collection.** Dues are collected monthly by automatic deduction from the member's Community Bank account. The deduction is administered by the Treasury in coordination with the Community Bank. Members shall be notified of each deduction.

**§13. Rates.** Dues rates are established by the Assembly and reviewed annually. Rates shall be set at a level sufficient to fund the Society's Services and obligations while remaining within the reasonable means of members. *[Placeholder: dues rates and any age or circumstance adjustments to be established in standing rules.]*

**§14. Hardship.** No member shall lose standing or access to essential services solely because they lack sufficient Franks to cover their dues. The Social Insurance Fund and such hardship provisions as the Assembly establishes shall ensure that poverty is not a barrier to membership in good standing. *[Placeholder: hardship dues provisions to be established in standing rules.]*

## Article IV — Good Standing

**§15. Definition.** A member is in good standing when they are meeting their obligations to the Society and conducting themselves in accordance with its rules and spirit. Good standing is the normal condition of membership. It is not an achievement but a baseline.

**§16. Conduct.** Good standing is primarily a matter of conduct. A member who acts in good faith, meets their dues obligations, and does not act in ways seriously harmful to other members or to the Society's institutions is a member in good standing. The Society does not police participation levels — mutual aid flows naturally from genuine community membership and cannot be compelled.

**§17. Significance.** Good standing is referenced throughout the Society's foundational documents as a condition for participation in governance, service in Colleges and Committees, and access to certain community resources. A member not in good standing retains their basic rights as a member but may have certain participations suspended pending resolution of the matter affecting their standing.

## Article V — Rights of Members

The following rights belong to every member of the Society. They may not be suspended, abridged, or conditioned by the Assembly, any College, any Committee, or any Service except through due process as established in these bylaws. They represent the floor below which the community will not go.

**§19. Right to Participate.** Every member has the right to participate in the community's governance, to attend Assembly proceedings, to work within Services, and to bring matters before the community through petition.

**§20. Right to Services.** Every member in good standing has the right to access the Society's Services. Essential services — particularly healthcare and food — shall not be withheld from any member in circumstances of genuine need regardless of standing, as the community's commitment to human dignity supersedes administrative status.

**§21. Right to Information.** Every member has the right to inspect the records of the Society's institutions, attend meetings of the Assembly and Committees, and be informed of decisions that affect them. The Society's business is the members' business.

**§22. Right to Petition.** Every member has the right to petition the Assembly, any College, any Committee, or the community as a whole. Petitions shall be received and considered in good faith. *[Placeholder: petition thresholds and response requirements to be established in standing rules.]*

**§23. Right to Due Process.** No member shall be removed from membership, suspended from good standing, or deprived of their rights without due process. Due process requires at minimum: clear notice of the matter at issue, a reasonable opportunity to respond, and a fair hearing before a body with appropriate authority. The community retains the right to review any such determination.

**§24. Right to Account.** Every member has the right to a Community Bank account, to full access to their own account records, and to accurate accounting of their Frank holdings and transactions. This right may not be suspended without due process.
