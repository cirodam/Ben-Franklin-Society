# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Health Service
*Article VIII of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

The Health Service exists to provide the best healthcare the community can offer with the resources and personnel it has. This document is written with clear eyes about the realities of rural healthcare — physician shortages, fragile pharmaceutical supply chains, limited specialist access, and the likelihood that conditions will sometimes fall far short of the ideal. The Health Service does not wait for ideal conditions. It builds what it can, operates at whatever level of capacity is available, and plans explicitly for circumstances in which conventional medical resources are scarce or absent. The community's health is too important to be held hostage to conditions that may never be perfect.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Obligation.** The Health Service is charged with providing the best healthcare available to every member of the Ben Franklin Society. Healthcare is a right of membership. The Health Service shall pursue this obligation at whatever level of capacity the community can sustain, scaling up as capacity grows and never using the absence of ideal resources as justification for providing nothing.

**§2. Tiered Capacity.** The Health Service operates on a tiered capacity model. Whatever licensed and trained health personnel are available — physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, registered nurses, paramedics, emergency medical technicians, community health workers, midwives — shall be deployed at their full scope of practice. The Health Service does not hold a lesser practitioner idle waiting for a greater one. It uses what it has, fully.

**§3. Planning for Scarcity.** The Health Service explicitly plans for circumstances in which conventional medical resources — physicians, pharmaceuticals, specialist equipment — are scarce or unavailable. Community health worker training, medical knowledge preservation, traditional and botanical medicine, and inter-society coordination are not fallback options. They are planned components of a resilient health system.

**§4. Functions.** The Health Service carries out its mandate through the following functions: primary care; dental care; maternal and reproductive care; mental health and addiction support; mobile and outreach care; medical supply and stockpile management; pharmaceutical capacity; community health worker development; specialist and referral capacity as available; and inter-society health coordination.

**§5. Oversight.** The Health Service is overseen jointly by the Medical College and the Caregivers College. The breadth of the Health Service's mandate spans both domains. The two Colleges shall establish clear protocols for their joint oversight, including coordination on standards, staffing, and service delivery.

---

## Article II — Primary Care

**§6. Primary Clinic.** The Health Service shall maintain at least one primary care clinic providing ongoing clinical care to members. The primary clinic is the foundation of the Health Service's clinical capacity — the first point of contact for member health needs and the institution through which ongoing care relationships are built.

**§7. Scope.** The primary clinic provides the full scope of primary care available given the Health Service's current personnel and resources. Scope is not fixed — it expands as capacity grows. The clinic shall document its current scope clearly so that members understand what care is available and what requires referral or inter-society coordination.

**§8. Preventive Care.** The primary clinic shall maintain a strong emphasis on preventive care — screenings, vaccinations, health education, and early intervention. Prevention is the most cost-effective medicine and the most appropriate focus for a community health system with limited resources.

**§9. Specialist Clinics.** The Health Service may establish specialist clinics as personnel and resources allow. Specialist capacity is not a founding requirement but a development goal. The Health Service shall pursue specialist capacity as a priority as the community grows and as the Medical College develops its membership.

---

## Article III — Dental Care

**§10. Dental Clinic.** The Health Service shall maintain at least one dental clinic. Dental care is not supplementary to health — it is health. Untreated dental disease causes systemic illness, chronic pain, and significant reduction in quality of life. The Society's commitment to member health is not fulfilled without dental care.

**§11. Scope.** The dental clinic shall provide the full scope of dental care available given current personnel and resources, with emphasis on prevention, basic restorative care, and pain relief. Scope shall be documented and communicated to members clearly.

**§12. Preventive Emphasis.** The dental clinic shall emphasize preventive dental care — cleaning, education, fluoride treatment, and early intervention — as the most effective use of limited dental capacity. Prevention reduces the burden of restorative care and keeps members in better long-term dental health.

---

## Article IV — Maternal and Reproductive Care

**§13. Mandate.** The Health Service shall provide maternal and reproductive healthcare to members. The collapse of obstetric capacity in rural communities is among the most serious healthcare crises of the current era. The Society does not accept that its members must travel long distances for prenatal care or deliver without qualified attendance. Maternal care is a foundational health obligation.

**§14. Midwifery.** The Health Service shall develop and maintain a midwifery capacity. Midwifery does not require a physician — it requires trained, experienced practitioners with good judgment and appropriate equipment. The community can train and sustain midwives even in circumstances where physician capacity is limited. Midwifery is both a clinical function and a knowledge tradition the Health Service shall actively develop.

**§15. Prenatal and Postnatal Care.** The Health Service shall provide prenatal care to pregnant members and postnatal care to new mothers and newborns. Continuity of care through pregnancy, birth, and the postpartum period is a health obligation the Society takes seriously.

**§16. Reproductive Healthcare.** The Health Service shall provide reproductive healthcare to members within the scope of its clinical capacity. Reproductive health is health.

**§17. Coordination with Caregivers College.** Maternal and newborn care spans the Medical and Caregivers domains. The Health Service shall coordinate closely with the Caregivers College on postpartum support, newborn care, and the caregiving dimensions of maternal health.

---

## Article V — Mental Health and Addiction Support

**§18. Mandate.** The Health Service shall maintain a mental health and addiction support function. Mental health is health. Addiction is a health condition. Both are deeply prevalent in rural communities and both have been chronically underserved by conventional healthcare systems. The Society does not replicate that neglect.

**§19. Delivery Model.** Mental health and addiction support does not require a dedicated physical clinic. It may be delivered through counseling staff embedded in the primary clinic, through community outreach, through peer support programs, and through coordination with the Caregivers College's community-embedded functions. The Health Service shall develop the delivery model that best serves the actual needs of members.

**§20. Community Factors.** The Society's foundational commitments — economic security through the Frank, social connection through community institutions, meaningful participation through governance — are themselves protective factors for mental health. The Health Service's mental health function operates within a community designed to reduce the social and economic causes of mental illness. It shall recognize and build on this.

**§21. Addiction.** The Health Service shall provide addiction support to members without judgment. Addiction is a health condition, not a moral failure. Support shall include harm reduction, treatment referral, peer support, and such clinical interventions as the Health Service's capacity allows. The Health Service shall coordinate with the Caregivers College on the community support dimensions of addiction recovery.

**§22. Privacy.** Mental health and addiction records are among the most sensitive personal information the Health Service holds. The Health Service shall maintain the strictest privacy standards for these records and shall not disclose them without explicit member consent except as required by an immediate safety necessity.

---

## Article VI — Mobile and Outreach Care

**§23. Mobile Clinic.** The Health Service shall maintain a mobile clinic capable of reaching members who cannot readily access fixed clinic locations. In a rural community of up to thirty thousand people spread across a significant geographic area, a fixed clinic alone does not fulfill the mandate to provide healthcare to every member. The mobile clinic goes to the people.

**§24. Outreach.** Beyond the mobile clinic the Health Service shall maintain a community health outreach function — identifying members with unmet health needs, connecting them with services, conducting home visits where appropriate, and ensuring that geographic or physical barriers do not result in members going without care.

**§25. Coordination with Caregivers College.** Identifying and reaching members with unmet health needs is a caregiving function as much as a medical one. The Health Service shall coordinate closely with the Caregivers College on outreach to ensure that no member falls through the gap between the two services.

---

## Article VII — Medical Supply and Stockpile

**§26. Supply Mandate.** The Health Service is responsible for procuring, managing, and distributing medical supplies and medicines necessary for community health. Supply chain fragility is a known and serious risk. The Health Service treats supply security as a core operational responsibility, not an administrative function.

**§27. Medical Stockpile.** The Health Service shall maintain a strategic stockpile of essential medicines, medical supplies, and equipment sufficient to sustain health operations through periods of external supply disruption. The stockpile is not a convenience reserve — it is a resilience requirement. What the Health Service has on hand when supply chains fail is what it has. The stockpile shall be maintained, rotated, and inventoried continuously.

**§28. Stockpile Standards.** The Medical College shall establish minimum stockpile standards for essential medicines and supplies. *[Placeholder: stockpile categories and minimum levels to be established by Medical College with Assembly approval.]*

**§29. Inter-Society Supply Coordination.** The Health Service shall actively coordinate medical supply procurement and distribution with other Ben Franklin Societies. Collective purchasing across societies provides greater leverage and resilience than individual procurement. When supplies are scarce, the Health Service shall participate in inter-society triage — directing scarce resources to the communities of most acute need, accepting the same discipline when it is itself in deficit.

**§30. Pharmaceutical Capacity.** The Health Service shall maintain pharmaceutical dispensing capacity to the extent that medicines can be reliably obtained. The Society recognizes that pharmaceutical supply chains are fragile and geographically concentrated, and that the ability to obtain medicines may be significantly constrained in disruption scenarios. The Health Service shall be honest about the limits of its pharmaceutical capacity and shall plan accordingly.

**§31. Botanical and Traditional Medicine.** The Health Service shall develop and maintain knowledge of botanical and traditional medicines as a supplement to pharmaceutical capacity. In circumstances where pharmaceutical supply is limited, botanical medicine represents a genuine community resource. The Medical College shall oversee the development of this knowledge base in coordination with the Agricultural College. Botanical medicine is a complement to modern medicine, not a replacement for it.

---

## Article VIII — Community Health Workers

**§32. Purpose.** Community health workers are trained members of the Society who provide basic health services, health education, and health navigation within the community. They are not physicians. They are not replacements for clinical care. They are a layer of health capacity that extends the reach of the Health Service into the community and that provides genuine value even when physician capacity is absent.

**§33. Training.** The Health Service shall actively train community health workers from the Society's membership. Training shall be developed and overseen by the Medical College. The goal is to distribute basic health knowledge and competency broadly across the community so that the community's health capacity does not reside only in a small number of practitioners.

**§34. Scope of Practice.** The scope of practice for community health workers shall be established by the Medical College and shall reflect what can be safely and effectively done without physician supervision. Community health workers operate within their scope. They do not replace clinical care — they extend its reach.

**§35. Continuity.** Community health worker capacity is among the most resilient forms of health infrastructure the Society can build. Unlike physicians, community health workers are community members. They do not leave when conditions deteriorate. They are present precisely when they are most needed. The Health Service shall treat community health worker development as a long-term investment in irreplaceable community capacity.

---

## Article IX — Referral and Specialist Care

**§36. Referral Obligation.** When a member's health needs exceed the Health Service's current capacity, the Health Service is obligated to assist that member in accessing appropriate care from external providers. Referral coordination is a health service. The Health Service shall maintain relationships with external healthcare systems and shall support members in navigating them.

**§37. Specialist Development.** The Health Service shall develop specialist clinic capacity as personnel and resources allow. Advanced surgery and highly specialized care may be beyond reach in the near term. The Health Service shall be honest about this while actively working to expand capacity. Specialist care that can be developed within the community shall be developed.

**§38. Inter-Society Specialist Coordination.** Some specialist capacity may be better developed at the federation level than within individual societies. The Health Service shall explore with other Ben Franklin Societies the development of shared specialist resources — visiting practitioners, coordinated specialist clinics, and shared equipment — that no single society could sustain alone.

---

## Article X — Medical Knowledge and Resilience

**§39. Knowledge as Infrastructure.** Medical knowledge is among the most valuable forms of community infrastructure. The Health Service and the Medical College shall treat the preservation, development, and transmission of medical knowledge as a core responsibility — not only in good times but especially in preparation for circumstances where conventional resources are scarce.

**§40. Medical Library.** The Health Service shall maintain a medical reference library accessible to practitioners and community health workers. The library shall include foundational medical texts, clinical references, botanical medicine resources, and public health materials. Physical reference materials that do not depend on electronic systems are a resilience priority.

**§41. Training Continuity.** The Health Service shall maintain ongoing training programs for practitioners at all levels — from community health workers to the most qualified clinicians available. Health knowledge must be continuously transmitted and developed within the community. It cannot be assumed to persist without active cultivation.

---

## Article XI — Transparency and Reporting

**§42. Annual Report.** The Health Service shall publish an annual report covering: clinical capacity and scope at each facility; patient encounter volumes by function; stockpile status; supply coordination activity; community health worker numbers and training activity; referral volumes; mental health and addiction support activity in aggregate; and inter-society coordination. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§43. Stockpile Reporting.** Stockpile levels for essential medicines and supplies shall be reported to the Assembly quarterly. Any material decline below established minimums shall be reported immediately.

**§44. Member Privacy.** All individual member health information is strictly private. The Health Service shall maintain robust privacy protections for all health records. Aggregate data on service utilization is public. Individual health data is never disclosed without explicit member consent except as required by an immediate safety necessity involving risk to the member or others.

**§45. Capacity Transparency.** The Health Service shall communicate clearly and honestly to members about its current scope and capacity — what care is available, what requires referral, and what is currently beyond its reach. Honest communication about capacity is itself a health service. Members who understand the Health Service's limits can plan accordingly.
