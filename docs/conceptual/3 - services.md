# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on Services
*Article II of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

The Ben Franklin Society exists not only to govern but to serve. The Society establishes and maintains Services — operational bodies through which the community fulfills its obligations to its members. Where the Colleges and Assembly decide, the Services do. Services are the productive and caring expression of the community's collective will, providing for the health, sustenance, financial security, and flourishing of every member.

---

## Article I — Nature and Establishment of Services

**§1. Definition.** A Service is an operational body established by the Society to provide ongoing functions essential to community life. Services are instruments of the community's mutual obligation to its members. They exist to serve, not to govern.

**§2. Establishment.** Services are established by the Assembly. The Assembly shall define the mission, scope, and name of each Service upon establishment. The Assembly may dissolve or reorganize any Service with the concurrence of the community.

**§3. Oversight.** The Assembly may oversee any Service directly or delegate oversight to a College whose domain encompasses the Service's mission. It is expected that most Services will be overseen by the appropriate College. Where a Service crosses multiple domains, the Assembly may assign joint oversight to more than one College.

**§4. Foundational and Essential Services.** Certain Services are so fundamental to community life that they must be established at the Society's founding and maintained without interruption. These are designated Foundational Services. Other Services are Essential — they must be established as early as practicable and maintained as a community obligation, but may be stood up as capacity allows.

**§5. Participation.** Any member of the Society in good standing may work within any Service. Participation in the work of Services is open and shall not be gatekept. Named staff roles within Services are distinct from general participation and are governed by the provisions of Article III.

**§6. Budgets.** Each Service shall prepare and submit an annual budget to the Assembly. The Assembly shall review, deliberate upon, and appropriate funding for each Service from the common dues of the membership, denominated in Franks. Services shall operate within their appropriated budgets and shall account to their overseeing body for all expenditures.

## Article II — Staff of Services

**§8. Paid Staff.** All Services shall maintain paid staff sufficient to carry out their mission. Compensation for staff is paid in Franks from the Service's appropriated budget. Staff are compensated for service to the community, not employed in the capitalist sense. The relationship between a staff member and a Service is one of community obligation and trust, not private contract.

**§9. Nomination.** Staff are nominated rather than hired. For Services overseen by a College, nominations originate with that College. For Services overseen directly by the Assembly, nominations originate with the Assembly. The community retains the right to object to any nomination. *[Placeholder: nomination confirmation process and community objection mechanism to be defined in standing rules.]*

## Article III — Foundational Financial Services

The following four Services are Foundational. They shall be established at the Society's founding and maintained without interruption thereafter. The community's capacity for self-governance, mutual aid, and economic life depends upon them entirely. Faith in these institutions is among the Society's most vital assets and shall be protected by radical transparency — the books, records, and operations of all Foundational Financial Services shall be open to inspection by any member at any time.

### The Central Bank

**§13. Mission.** The Central Bank is responsible for the issuance and integrity of the Frank, the Society's currency. The Central Bank does not exercise discretionary monetary policy. Its function is faithful administration of the currency principles established in the Society's foundational documents.

**§14. Issuance.** The Central Bank issues Franks in accordance with the population of the Society. Issuance is equal for every member — two thousand Franks per member per year, deposited on each member's birthday — and follows faithfully from an accurate and current count of the membership. The count is the primary function; issuance follows from it without discretion.

**§15. Population Count.** The Central Bank shall maintain a continuous and accurate count of the Society's membership. The integrity of the count is the integrity of the currency. The count shall be open to verification by any member.

**§16. Demurrage.** The Central Bank administers demurrage on Frank holdings in accordance with the rates established in the standing rules. Demurrage shall be applied consistently and without exception to all holders.

**§17. Oversight.** The Central Bank is overseen directly by the Assembly. Given the foundational nature of its function, the Assembly shall not delegate Central Bank oversight to any College.

### The Community Bank

**§18. Mission.** The Community Bank is the financial infrastructure of the Society. It holds member accounts, processes transactions, and maintains the ledger of Frank holdings. It is a utility — as essential and as taken for granted as clean water when functioning well.

**§19. Accounts.** Every member of the Society is entitled to an account with the Community Bank. Accounts are a right of membership and shall not be denied, suspended, or closed except by due process established in the standing rules.

**§20. Ledger Integrity.** The Community Bank shall maintain an accurate, tamper-resistant ledger of all accounts and transactions. The ledger is the foundation of economic trust within the Society. Each member shall have full access to their own account records at all times.

**§21. Oversight.** The Community Bank is overseen directly by the Assembly.

### The Treasury

**§22. Mission.** The Treasury is the fiscal agent of the Society. It receives membership dues, holds the Society's common reserves, and disburses funds to Services in accordance with Assembly appropriations. The Treasury does not determine how funds are spent — that is the Assembly's authority. The Treasury ensures that funds are received, held securely, and disbursed faithfully.

**§23. Dues Collection.** The Treasury collects membership dues denominated in Franks on such schedule and at such rates as the Assembly establishes. Dues are the primary funding mechanism for all Services.

**§24. Disbursement.** The Treasury disburses funds to Services solely in accordance with Assembly appropriations. No disbursement shall be made without Assembly authorization. The Treasury shall maintain full records of all receipts and disbursements, open to inspection by any member.

**§25. Oversight.** The Treasury is overseen directly by the Assembly.

### The Social Insurance Fund

**§26. Mission.** The Social Insurance Fund expresses the Society's foundational commitment that every member shall live with dignity regardless of their capacity to contribute. The Fund provides regular allowances to elderly and disabled members as a right of membership, not a charity. Human presence in the community generates value; the Fund recognizes this materially.

**§27. Eligibility — Elderly Members.** Members who have reached the age established in the standing rules are entitled to a regular allowance from the Fund as a right of membership. No means test shall be applied. *[Placeholder: age threshold to be established in standing rules.]*

**§28. Eligibility — Disabled Members.** Members whose capacity to contribute is diminished by disability are entitled to support from the Fund calibrated to their circumstances. Disability is assessed on a spectrum of contribution capacity rather than as a binary condition. Assessment is conducted jointly by the Medical College and the Caregivers College. Support is proportional to assessed need and shall be reviewed periodically with dignity and good faith.

**§29. Allowance.** Allowances are paid regularly in Franks at rates established by the Assembly and reviewed annually. Allowances shall be sufficient to meet basic needs with dignity. *[Placeholder: allowance rates and payment schedule to be established in standing rules.]*

**§30. Funding.** The Social Insurance Fund is funded through membership dues held in reserve for this purpose. The Assembly shall ensure that the Fund maintains reserves sufficient to meet its obligations. The Fund's reserve status shall be reported to the membership annually.

**§31. Oversight.** The Social Insurance Fund is overseen by the Assembly with input from the Medical College and Caregivers College on matters of eligibility assessment.

## Article IV — Essential Services

The following Services are Essential. The Society is obligated to establish and maintain them as early as practicable. Their absence represents an unfulfilled community obligation. Each shall be established by the Assembly, named appropriately for the community, and overseen as provided below.

### The Health Service

**§32. Mission.** The Health Service provides comprehensive primary healthcare to all members of the Society as a right of membership. It operates as a unified institution encompassing fixed and mobile clinical care and medical supply.

**§33. Primary Clinic.** The Health Service shall maintain a primary clinic providing ongoing clinical care to members. The clinic is the first point of contact for member health needs and the foundation of the Society's healthcare capacity.

**§34. Mobile Clinic.** The Health Service shall maintain a mobile clinic capable of reaching members who cannot readily access the primary clinic. The mobile clinic expresses the Society's commitment to serve all members, not only those who are able to present themselves.

**§35. Medical Supply.** The Health Service shall maintain a medical supply function responsible for procuring, storing, and distributing medicines, equipment, and supplies necessary for community health. The Health Service shall maintain strategic reserves of essential medicines and supplies sufficient to sustain operations through periods of external supply disruption. The medical supply function shall coordinate with the corresponding functions of other Ben Franklin Societies where possible.

**§36. Scope and Referral.** The Health Service provides primary care within its capacity. It shall maintain relationships with external specialist and emergency services for needs beyond its scope, and shall support members in accessing such services.

**§37. Oversight.** The Health Service is overseen jointly by the Medical College and the Caregivers College.

### The Food Service

**§38. Mission.** The Food Service manages the Society's food processing, preparation, and storage infrastructure. It ensures that the community's food supply is processed, preserved, and accessible to all members.

**§39. Community Kitchens.** The Food Service shall operate community kitchens providing prepared food to members. Community kitchens reduce the individual burden of food preparation, support members unable to cook for themselves, and serve as centers of food knowledge and culture.

**§40. Grain Mills.** The Food Service shall maintain grain milling capacity sufficient to process the community's grain production into usable food. Ownership of milling capacity is a foundational expression of food sovereignty.

**§41. Food Reserve.** The Society is obligated to maintain a strategic reserve of food sufficient to sustain the membership through periods of crop failure, supply disruption, or acute crisis. The maintenance of this reserve is not optional — it is a standing community obligation. The Food Service is responsible for maintaining, rotating, and accounting for the reserve. Reserve levels shall be reported to the membership annually. *[Placeholder: minimum reserve levels to be established in standing rules.]*

**§42. Oversight.** The Food Service is overseen by the Agricultural College.

### The Agricultural Service

**§43. Mission.** The Agricultural Service builds and sustains the Society's productive growing capacity. Where the Food Service manages what has been grown, the Agricultural Service develops the community's capacity to grow. This is inherently long-term work, measured in seasons, years, and generations.

**§44. Seed Library.** The Agricultural Service shall maintain a seed library of open-pollinated, locally-adapted seed varieties. The seed library is a foundational expression of food sovereignty — a community that controls its seed stock controls its food future. The library shall be actively developed, not merely preserved.

**§45. Growing Capacity.** The Agricultural Service shall actively develop the community's growing capacity through soil health improvement, establishment of perennial food systems, support for member growers, assessment and development of community land for food production, and such other means as the Agricultural College determines appropriate.

**§46. Knowledge Transmission.** The Agricultural Service shall actively transmit agricultural knowledge within the community, ensuring that growing skills, soil knowledge, and farming practice are held broadly and do not reside only in a few individuals.

**§47. Long-Term Planning.** The Agricultural Service shall maintain long-term plans for the development of community growing capacity. The Assembly shall recognize in its appropriations that agricultural investment may take years or decades to mature, and shall fund accordingly.

**§48. Oversight.** The Agricultural Service is overseen by the Agricultural College.

## Article V — Future Services

**§49. Establishment by Assembly.** The Assembly may establish additional Services as the needs and capacity of the community develop. New Services shall be established in accordance with Article I of this document. The founding Services named in this document do not constitute a closed set — the Society is expected to grow its service capacity over time.

**§50. Naming.** Each Service shall be named by the Assembly upon establishment in a manner appropriate to its mission and the character of the community. Names need not include the word Service.

**§51. Federation.** Services, particularly those with supply, logistics, or coordination functions, are encouraged to establish working relationships with the corresponding Services of other Ben Franklin Societies. Such relationships strengthen the resilience of all participating communities.
