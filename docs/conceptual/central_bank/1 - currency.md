# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Frank
*Article IV of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

The Frank is the currency of the Ben Franklin Society. It is not backed by gold, by government decree, or by the promise of distant institutions. It is backed by the community itself — by the productive capacity, the caring labor, the collective knowledge, and the mutual commitment of its members. Every Frank in circulation represents a real claim on what the community can do and provide. The Frank exists to facilitate the community's internal economy, to circulate the fruits of collective effort, and to express in material terms the Society's foundational belief that human presence generates value.

---

## Article I — Nature of the Frank

**§1. The Community Currency.** The Frank is the official currency of the Ben Franklin Society. It is denominated, issued, and administered within the Society and is the required medium for dues payments, staff compensation, Service transactions, and Social Insurance Fund allowances.

**§2. Backing.** The Frank is backed by the community's collective capacity — its labor, its services, its productive infrastructure, and its mutual obligations. It is not a fiat currency issued by decree alone, nor a commodity currency tied to a scarce physical resource. Its value is the community's value.

**§3. Not a Universal Basic Income.** The Frank is not issued directly to individual members as a universal basic income. Issuance flows into the community commons through the Treasury, from which it is appropriated by the Assembly to fund Services, staff, and obligations. Members receive Franks through their participation in the community's economic life — through compensated service, staff roles, Social Insurance Fund allowances, and such bootstrap provisions as the Assembly establishes. The Frank rewards contribution to and membership in the community, not mere existence.

**§4. The Ledger.** The Frank is a ledger currency. The Community Bank maintains the authoritative ledger of all Frank holdings and transactions. The ledger is the source of truth. Physical transaction slips serve as verification instruments and as a resilient backup medium when the ledger is unavailable. The ledger and the slips together constitute the Frank system.

**§5. Transaction Slips.** Physical transaction slips shall be maintained as a parallel record of Frank transactions. Slips provide a paper trail for ledger reconciliation and a functional medium of exchange during periods of ledger unavailability. The form, issuance, and reconciliation of transaction slips shall be established in the standing rules.

**§6. Accounts.** Every member holds a Frank account with the Community Bank. Accounts are a right of membership. Frank balances may not go negative except in exceptional circumstances as provided in Article V of this document.

---

## Article II — Issuance

**§7. The Person-Year.** The Frank is issued on the basis of the person-year — the unit of one full year of human membership in the Society. Every member, regardless of age, occupation, or capacity, represents one person-year per annum. Person-years are equal. The Society makes no distinction in the value of human presence.

**§8. Issuance Rate.** The Society shall issue two thousand Franks per person-year. This rate may be adjusted by the Assembly with the concurrence of the community, but such adjustment shall be made with care and deliberation, as the issuance rate is foundational to the Frank economy.

**§9. Birthday Issuance.** On each member's birthday, the Central Bank shall deposit two thousand Franks into the Treasury on that member's behalf. This is the community's annual acknowledgment of the member's presence and the material expression of the person-year. The birthday issuance is automatic and without condition.

**§10. Destination.** All issuance flows into the Treasury. The Treasury holds these funds as part of the community commons, to be appropriated by the Assembly for Services, staff compensation, Social Insurance Fund obligations, and such other community purposes as the Assembly determines. Issuance does not flow directly to individual member accounts.

**§11. Money Supply.** The total Frank money supply at any time reflects the size and history of the membership. As the community grows, issuance expands the supply. As the community contracts, demurrage gradually reduces the supply in proportion. This automatic relationship between population and money supply requires no discretionary policy and cannot be manipulated.

**§12. Membership Records.** Accurate issuance depends upon accurate membership records. The Central Bank shall maintain a continuous and current roll of all members, including each member's date of birth for purposes of birthday issuance calculation. The integrity of the membership roll is the integrity of the currency.

---

## Article III — The Central Bank

**§13. Mandate.** The Central Bank's mandate is narrow and absolute: maintain accurate membership rolls, issue Franks faithfully in accordance with those rolls, and administer demurrage as established in Article IV. The Central Bank does not exercise discretionary monetary policy. Its function is faithful administration, not governance.

**§14. Integrity.** The Central Bank is among the Society's most trust-sensitive institutions. Its operations shall be radically transparent — membership rolls, issuance records, and demurrage calculations shall be open to inspection by any member at any time. The integrity of the count is the integrity of the currency, and the integrity of the currency is the integrity of the community's economic life.

**§15. Independence.** The Central Bank is overseen by the Assembly but its core functions — membership roll maintenance and faithful issuance — shall be insulated from political interference. No Assembly decision, Committee ordinance, or community vote may direct the Central Bank to issue Franks in excess of or below the established rate, or to alter membership rolls for financial rather than factual reasons.

**§16. Coordination with Community Bank.** The Central Bank coordinates closely with the Community Bank in the administration of issuance and demurrage. The Central Bank determines what shall be issued and to whom; the Community Bank executes the ledger entries. The two institutions shall maintain clear protocols for this coordination.

## Article IV — Demurrage

Demurrage is a periodic charge on Frank holdings above a defined threshold. It is not a punishment but a design principle — it discourages the hoarding of Franks and encourages their circulation through the community economy. A Frank that circulates does work. A Frank that sits idle serves no one. Demurrage keeps the currency moving.

**§17. Threshold.** Demurrage applies only to Frank holdings above a defined threshold. Holdings at or below the threshold are exempt and shall not be reduced by demurrage. This protects modest balances and ensures that demurrage targets accumulation rather than ordinary saving. *[Placeholder: threshold amount to be established in standing rules, set with reference to a meaningful community standard such as a defined number of months of average dues or basic living costs in Franks.]*

**§18. Rate.** *[Placeholder: demurrage rate to be established in standing rules. The rate shall be set at a level sufficient to discourage significant hoarding and to gradually contract the money supply when membership declines, without being so aggressive as to prevent reasonable saving or planning.]*

**§19. Application.** Demurrage is applied periodically to all accounts holding Franks above the threshold. Application is automatic and without exception. Demurrage applies equally to member accounts, Service accounts, and institutional accounts. No account is exempt above the threshold.

**§20. Destination of Demurrage.** Franks collected through demurrage are returned to the Treasury and treated as community revenue available for Assembly appropriation. Demurrage recirculates wealth that would otherwise sit idle back into the community commons.

**§21. Monetary Contraction.** When membership declines, birthday issuance falls with it. Demurrage then gradually reduces the existing Frank supply toward a level appropriate to the smaller community. This automatic contraction requires no policy decision and cannot be manipulated. The money supply breathes with the community.

## Article V — Negative Balances

**§22. Exceptional Status.** A negative Frank balance is an exceptional condition. The Society's hardship provisions — including the Social Insurance Fund and such other provisions as the Assembly establishes — are designed to prevent members from reaching negative balances in ordinary circumstances. A negative balance indicates that existing provisions have not been sufficient and that additional support is needed.

**§23. Permitted.** Negative balances are permitted. The Community Bank shall not refuse transactions that would bring a member's account below zero in genuine circumstances of need. The Society does not punish hardship.

**§24. Response.** A member carrying a negative balance shall be connected with the Society's support provisions. The goal is restoration of positive balance through community support, not recovery of debt through punitive means. *[Placeholder: negative balance response process to be established in standing rules.]*

**§25. Demurrage on Negative Balances.** Demurrage does not apply to negative balances. A member in deficit shall not have their situation worsened by demurrage charges.

## Article VI — Integrity and Faith

**§26. Foundation of the Economy.** The Frank economy rests entirely on the community's faith in the currency. That faith rests on three things: the honesty of the count, the faithfulness of the issuance, and the accuracy of the ledger. If any of these three fail, the Frank fails. The Society shall treat the integrity of these functions as among its highest obligations.

**§27. Radical Transparency.** All Frank issuance records, membership rolls used for issuance calculation, demurrage calculations, and ledger summaries shall be open to inspection by any member at any time. Transparency is not a courtesy — it is the mechanism by which members verify the integrity of the currency they depend upon.

**§28. Prohibition on Manipulation.** No person, body, or institution within the Society may direct the Central Bank to alter membership rolls for financial rather than factual reasons, to issue Franks outside the established rate, or to apply demurrage selectively or unevenly. Such acts constitute a fundamental breach of community trust and shall be treated as among the most serious violations of the Society's rules.

**§29. Annual Report.** The Central Bank and Community Bank shall jointly publish an annual report on the state of the Frank — total issuance, total money supply, demurrage collected, account summary statistics, and membership roll totals. This report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.
