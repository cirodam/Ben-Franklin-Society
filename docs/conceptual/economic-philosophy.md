# Economic Philosophy

## Core Principle

Markets are powerful coordination tools for certain goods and services, but they are fundamentally ill-suited to provision basic human needs. The BFS economic model distinguishes between **social provisioning** (necessities guaranteed through public services) and **market allocation** (preferences satisfied through commerce).

## Market Failures in Essential Services

### Healthcare
- Cannot negotiate while dying or seriously ill
- Extreme information asymmetry between patients and providers
- Inelastic demand makes price signals meaningless
- Profit motive incentivizes denying care

### Housing
- Landlords profit from scarcity (incentive NOT to build)
- Financialization: empty homes held as investments while people are homeless
- Speculation disconnects prices from actual housing value
- Market treats shelter as commodity rather than human right

### Food & Agriculture
- Farmers destroy crops to maintain prices (see Great Depression)
- Boom/bust cycles: overproduction → price crash → bankruptcy → underproduction
- Markets don't guarantee distribution to those who need food
- Profit maximization can mean people go hungry while food is wasted

### Other Essential Services
- **Fire/Police**: Cannot negotiate while emergency is occurring
- **Education**: Society-wide benefits, not just individual
- **Water/Sanitation**: Natural monopolies, health externalities
- **Infrastructure**: Massive capital requirements, natural monopolies

## Service Categories

### Permanent Public Services
These services remain fully public and never transition to market provision:

1. **Healthcare Service** - Medical care, clinics, hospitals
2. **Food Service** - Food production coordination, distribution, emergency provision
3. **Agricultural Service** - Farming coordination, land management, crop planning
4. **Housing Service** - Construction, allocation, maintenance of housing stock
5. **Fire Service** - Emergency response, prevention
6. **Constabulary Service** - Public safety, peacekeeping
7. **Education Service** - Schools, training, knowledge transmission
8. **Water Service** - Water treatment, distribution
9. **Sanitation Service** - Waste collection, treatment
10. **Social Insurance Fund** - Mutual aid, welfare, unemployment support
11. **Energy Service** - Power generation and distribution
12. **Transit Service** - Public transportation infrastructure

### Bootstrap Services
These services start as full public provision but support emergence of private/cooperative sector:

1. **Manufacturing Service** - Initial tool/goods production → supports worker cooperatives and private firms
2. **Commerce Service** - Initial retail/trade → supports private merchants
3. **Printing Service** - Initial capacity → supports private publishers

### Hybrid Services
Public option alongside private provision:

1. **Childcare Service (Mother's College)** - Public baseline with private options
2. **Enrichment Service** - Public programs alongside private instruction
3. **Courier Service** - Public postal service alongside private delivery

## Services as Economic Engine

### Phase 1: Startup (Years 1-3)
- Society forms with minimal private sector
- Services employ 80%+ of members
- Members earn Franks, gain skills, build relationships and capital
- Creates stable demand for goods and services
- Builds infrastructure and production capacity

**Example:** Food Service runs communal kitchens, pays cooks, servers, procurement staff. Members learn culinary skills, logistics, management.

### Phase 2: Emergence (Years 3-7)
- Skilled workers open private ventures (bakery, restaurant, construction firm)
- Bootstrap services shrink provision, maintain employment floor
- Public/private split approaches 50/50
- Services shift toward inspection, standards, regulation

**Example:** Experienced Food Service cook opens a bakery. Food Service reduces communal kitchen hours, shifts staff to food inspection and nutrition standards.

### Phase 3: Mature Economy (Years 7+)
- Robust private sector with cooperatives and sole proprietorships
- Services employ 20-30% (baseline employment guarantee + permanent services)
- Counter-cyclical stabilization: services expand employment during downturns
- Permanent services maintain full public provision

**Example:** Thriving private restaurant scene. Food Service focuses on food safety standards, emergency food provision, supporting agricultural planning. Housing Service maintains public housing stock and builds new units as population grows.

## Employment Guarantee

Services provide a **job guarantee** - any member can always find work in public services:

- Prevents involuntary unemployment
- Maintains skills during economic downturns
- Provides stable income floor
- Counter-cyclical: expands in recessions, contracts in booms
- Dignified work (not "workfare")

## Relationship to Markets

**BFS is not anti-market.** Markets excel at:
- Coordinating production of rival, excludable goods
- Driving innovation through competition
- Satisfying diverse consumer preferences
- Efficiently allocating scarce resources with elastic demand

**BFS is anti-commodification of basic needs.** When healthcare, housing, food, water, and safety are treated as commodities:
- Profit motive incentivizes artificial scarcity
- Price signals fail (demand is inelastic)
- Inequality becomes death sentence
- Speculation disconnects value from use

## Historical Precedents

### New Deal (USA, 1933-1943)
- CCC and WPA employed millions in public works
- Built infrastructure while training workers
- Participants gained skills and capital
- Fueled post-war economic boom

### Mondragon Cooperatives (Spain, 1956-present)
- Worker cooperatives in Basque region
- Mix of public services and cooperative enterprises
- Salary grades allow rotation between roles
- Limited wage differentials (6:1 vs 350:1 in US corporations)
- Demonstrates viability of democratic workplace organization

### Mutual Aid Societies (19th-20th century)
- Fraternal orders provided healthcare, death benefits, unemployment support
- Membership dues funded collective services
- Demonstrated voluntary cooperation for essential services
- BFS adapts this model with democratic governance and modern scale

## Connection to Currency Design

BFS uses **demurrage currency** (Franks) which discourages hoarding and speculation:
- Negative interest on large balances
- Encourages spending/investment over accumulation
- Makes housing speculation unprofitable
- Aligns currency with social provisioning goals
- See Silvio Gesell's *The Natural Economic Order* (1916)

## Democratic Control

Services remain democratically accountable:
- Officers selected by sortition from experienced members
- Fixed terms with rotation
- General Assembly can override service decisions
- Budgets approved through democratic process
- Recall mechanisms for accountability

**Hierarchy for coordination, not domination.**

## Guiding Questions

When evaluating whether something should be public or market-based:

1. **Is it a basic human need?** (food, shelter, health, safety)
2. **Are there natural monopolies or huge capital requirements?**
3. **Is demand inelastic?** (can't refuse to buy)
4. **Are there severe information asymmetries?** (expert vs layperson)
5. **Do positive externalities dominate?** (benefits society broadly)
6. **Can profit motive create perverse incentives?** (profit from scarcity/denial)

If yes to multiple questions → **Public service**  
If mostly no → **Market provision or hybrid model**

## Conclusion

The BFS economic model rejects false dichotomies:
- Not capitalism vs socialism, but **appropriate tool for appropriate task**
- Not market vs planning, but **markets for preferences, planning for needs**
- Not efficiency vs equality, but **recognition that markets are inefficient for inelastic goods**

Services provide the foundation—guaranteed employment, housing, food, healthcare, safety—upon which members can build prosperous private ventures, knowing their basic needs are secure.
