# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Colleges
*Article XXVI of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

The word College comes from the Latin *collegium* — a body of people bound together by common purpose, mutual obligation, and shared practice. In the original sense, which this Society recovers, a College is not a school. It is a community of peers who have developed genuine knowledge and skill in a defined domain, who hold each other accountable to standards of practice, and who transmit that knowledge to those who come after them. The Colleges of the Ben Franklin Society serve a specific constitutional function: they are the qualified pools from which the General Assembly draws when it needs competent people to govern in domains requiring expertise. They exist because the community acknowledges that some decisions require knowledge it does not universally share — and because randomly selecting from a pool of qualified peers is more honest, more democratic, and less corruptible than appointing experts. The Colleges are not a governing class. They are the community's mechanism for ensuring that qualified sortition is genuinely qualified.

---

## Article I — The Nature of Colleges

**§1. Definition.** A College is a self-governing body of peers bound by common purpose, mutual obligation, and shared standards within a defined domain of knowledge and practice. Colleges are the qualified pools from which the General Assembly draws members for committees requiring domain expertise. They are not governing bodies in their own right. Their authority derives entirely from the General Assembly's delegation and the community's acknowledgment of their expertise.

**§2. Community Acknowledgment.** The community, through the General Assembly, acknowledges the expertise of each College within its defined domain. This acknowledgment is an act of community sovereignty — the community choosing to recognize and draw upon expertise it does not universally share. It is not a surrender of authority. The General Assembly creates Colleges, defines their domains, holds them accountable, and may dissolve them. The community retains override authority over all College functions at all times.

**§3. Subordination to the General Assembly.** Colleges are subordinate to the General Assembly. They advise from expertise. They supply qualified practitioners for sortition. They maintain standards within their domain. They do not govern independently. A College committee exercises authority only because the General Assembly has delegated it. That delegation can be withdrawn. The chain of authority runs: Community — General Assembly — College committees. Colleges sit alongside that chain as the source of qualified people, not within it as a governing tier.

**§4. Legislative Function.** When the General Assembly draws a committee from a College pool and delegates legislative authority to that committee, the committee legislates within its domain. It may draft and enact ordinances, set standards, and make determinations that carry the force of community law within their defined scope. This legislative function is real and consequential. It is also always bounded by the General Assembly's delegation and subject to General Assembly and community override.

**§5. College Membership and Service Work.** College membership is distinct from service employment. Any member of the Society may work in any Service regardless of College membership. College membership is a civic role — it places a member in the qualified sortition pool for governance functions in that domain. It is not an employment credential and does not confer privilege in Service operations. A nurse need not be in the College of Nursing to work in the Health Service. A cook need not be in the Culinary Arts College to work in the community kitchen.

**§6. Multiple Membership.** A member may belong to more than one College. Multiple membership reflects the reality that knowledge domains overlap and that skilled practitioners often develop competence across related fields. A member who welds and machines belongs in both the College of Fabricators and whatever other College their additional competencies warrant. Multiple membership strengthens the qualified sortition pools and reflects genuine community knowledge.

**§7. Small Colleges.** A College with few members is not a failed College. It is a signal. When a College is small, the community recognizes that a skill or knowledge domain is underrepresented and that the Education Service, the Manufacturing Service, and relevant community programs should work to develop more practitioners. Small Colleges create visible community need. Growing them is a community governance interest, not merely an individual aspiration.

## Article II — Establishment and Dissolution

**§8. Establishment by General Assembly.** Colleges are established by resolution of the General Assembly. No College exists without General Assembly creation. The General Assembly defines the College's domain, establishes initial credentialing standards in consultation with related existing Colleges, and names the College. A College may be established at any time the General Assembly determines the community needs a qualified pool in a defined domain.

**§9. No Prescribed Set.** This document does not prescribe which Colleges must exist. The Society names the Colleges it has established and anticipates establishing, but the list is not closed. The community will create Colleges as needs emerge and as knowledge bases develop sufficient depth to sustain them. The framework established in this document applies to all Colleges equally, whether established at founding or in the future.

**§10. Domain Definition.** Each College's domain shall be defined clearly enough that membership criteria can be established and maintained. Domain boundaries shall be respected — a College shall not expand its credentialing or governance claims beyond its defined domain without General Assembly approval. When domains overlap, the relevant Colleges shall establish coordination protocols.

**§11. Dissolution.** The General Assembly may dissolve a College that is no longer needed, whose domain has been absorbed into another College, or that has failed to maintain standards and institutional integrity. Dissolution requires community concurrence. Members of a dissolved College retain their knowledge and standing as individuals — dissolution ends the institutional form, not the expertise.

**§12. Differentiation.** A broad founding College may differentiate into more specialized Colleges as the community develops deeper capacity in specific sub-domains. The College of Engineers, for example, may differentiate into the College of Builders, College of Electricians, College of Plumbers, and College of Fabricators as the community develops sufficient practitioners in each specialty to sustain distinct qualified pools. Differentiation requires General Assembly resolution and shall not leave existing practitioners without a College home.

## Article III — College Membership

**§13. Basis of Membership.** College membership is based on demonstrated competence within the College's domain as recognized by existing members. Each College establishes its own credentialing standards within the framework the General Assembly sets. Standards shall be specific enough to mean something and accessible enough to be achievable by practitioners who genuinely develop the relevant knowledge and skill.

**§14. Peer Recognition.** College membership is granted by peers — by existing members who have the knowledge to assess competence in their domain. This peer recognition is the heart of what makes a College a genuine credentialing body rather than a self-selecting club or a bureaucratic certification. Peers know what competence looks like. They assess it with the judgment that only shared practice produces.

**§15. Experiential Credentialing.** Most Colleges credential on the basis of demonstrated knowledge and skill. The Mother's College credentials on the basis of lived experience of pregnancy. This distinction is established in the Mother's College's foundational document and reflects the community's recognition that some knowledge is produced by experience rather than study or practice. Other Colleges may establish experiential components within their credentialing standards as appropriate to their domain.

**§16. Mutual Obligation.** College membership carries mutual obligation — to the College's standards, to the development of other members, to the transmission of knowledge to new practitioners, and to the community whose governance the College supports. Membership is not merely a credential. It is an ongoing commitment to the College's purpose.

**§17. Loss of Membership.** A member may lose College membership through voluntary withdrawal, sustained failure to meet the College's standards, or serious conduct violations subject to the College's internal process and the community's due process protections. Loss of College membership does not affect Society membership. *[Placeholder: College membership loss processes to be established in individual College charters.]*

**§18. Growing the Colleges.** The community has a governance interest in growing its College pools. A College with few members produces thin qualified sortition capacity. The Education Service, Manufacturing Service, and such other community institutions as are relevant shall actively develop practitioners in domains where College pools are insufficient. The General Assembly shall consider College pool adequacy as part of its annual oversight of community governance capacity.

## Article IV — Qualified Sortition

**§19. The Core Mechanism.** Qualified sortition is the drawing of committee members by lot from a College's membership pool. It combines the democratic legitimacy of random selection — no one controls who is drawn — with the competence assurance of membership in a credentialed peer body. It is the mechanism by which the Society ensures that governance in expert domains is both qualified and incorruptible.

**§20. When Qualified Sortition Applies.** The General Assembly draws from College pools when establishing committees whose functions genuinely require domain expertise — clinical standard-setting, agricultural practice governance, engineering and infrastructure oversight, safety and emergency management, and such other functions as the General Assembly determines require qualified rather than general sortition. Not all committees require College pools. The General Assembly determines which functions require qualified sortition and from which College or Colleges the pool should be drawn.

**§21. General Sortition Alongside.** The General Assembly may establish committees drawn from the general eligible membership rather than College pools when democratic legitimacy and community perspective serve the function better than domain expertise. Budget oversight, service performance review, accountability proceedings, and the Permanent Review Committee draw from the general membership. General and qualified sortition serve different governance needs. The General Assembly deploys each where it is appropriate.

**§22. Mixed Committees.** The General Assembly may establish committees that draw from both College pools and general membership — College members for domain expertise and general members for community accountability. Mixed committees ensure that domain governance does not become entirely self-referential while maintaining the competence that qualified sortition provides.

**§23. Committee Authority.** A committee's authority derives from the General Assembly's delegation, not from the College that supplied its members. College membership places a practitioner in the pool. Sortition draws them. The General Assembly's delegation gives them authority. The community's ongoing oversight bounds that authority. College membership alone confers no governance power.

**§24. Declining Service.** Service on a committee drawn from a College pool is a civic obligation of membership. A College member drawn by sortition is expected to serve. Grounds for declining shall be established in standing rules and shall be limited to genuine incapacity, material conflict of interest, and such other circumstances as the General Assembly determines. Declining sortition service routinely undermines the qualified sortition system and shall not become normal practice.

## Article V — Institutional Life of Colleges

**§25. Ongoing Existence.** A College has ongoing institutional life beyond its governance function. College members gather, deliberate, develop shared knowledge, support one another's practice, and transmit expertise to new practitioners continuously — not only when a committee is being drawn or a credentialing decision is being made. This ongoing institutional life is what makes a College a genuine community of practice rather than merely a credentialing registry.

**§26. Knowledge Development.** Each College is responsible for developing knowledge within its domain — staying current with practice, developing community-specific knowledge, and building the collective understanding that peer governance requires. A College whose members have stopped learning has stopped being a genuine credentialing body. Knowledge development is a standing College obligation.

**§27. Knowledge Transmission.** Colleges are responsible for transmitting their domain knowledge to new practitioners — through mentorship, apprenticeship, formal training, and such other means as each domain requires. Colleges that do not transmit their knowledge produce thin pools and eventually disappear. Transmission is survival. It is also the community's mechanism for growing the qualified sortition capacity it needs.

**§28. Self-Governance.** Each College governs its own internal affairs — membership processes, knowledge development programs, standard-setting, and such other internal functions as the College determines necessary. The General Assembly establishes the framework within which Colleges operate but does not govern their internal life. Colleges are self-governing peer bodies within the bounds the General Assembly sets.

**§29. Charters.** Each College shall maintain a charter establishing its domain, credentialing standards, internal governance, and relationship to relevant Services. Charters are adopted by the College and approved by the General Assembly. They may be amended by the College with General Assembly concurrence. Charters are the College's foundational self-governing document.

**§30. Inter-College Coordination.** Where College domains overlap — as in the Health Service where Medical, Caregivers, and Mother's Colleges share oversight responsibilities — the relevant Colleges shall establish coordination protocols. No domain boundary is perfectly clean. Colleges shall approach boundary questions with good faith and the community's interests rather than institutional territoriality.

## Article VI — College Oversight of Services

**§31. Oversight Role.** Where the General Assembly delegates service oversight to a College, the College oversees the Service through its committee functions — setting standards of practice, reviewing service performance, advising on staffing nominations, and ensuring the Service operates within its mandate and to the standards the community expects. College oversight is domain-expert oversight. It is not day-to-day management.

**§32. Standards Setting.** Colleges with service oversight responsibility set the technical and practice standards that Services must meet. Clinical standards in the Health Service. Agricultural practice standards in the Agricultural Service. Safety standards in the Constabulary Service. These standards carry the authority of the General Assembly's delegation. Services must meet them. The General Assembly may override standards it determines are inconsistent with community interests.

**§33. Staffing Input.** Colleges with service oversight shall have input into the nomination of Service staff in their domain. This input is advisory to the nominating body — it does not control staffing decisions — but it carries the weight of domain expertise and shall be given genuine consideration. A College's assessment of whether a nominated staff member meets domain standards is important information for the nominating body.

**§34. Assembly Direct Oversight.** Some Services are overseen directly by the General Assembly without College delegation — primarily the foundational financial institutions and Services whose functions are too broad or too foundational to delegate to a specialist pool. The General Assembly may at any time withdraw service oversight from a College and retain it directly. College service oversight is a delegation, not a right.

## Article VII — College Accountability

**§35. Accountability to General Assembly.** Every College is accountable to the General Assembly. Colleges shall report annually to the General Assembly on their membership, credentialing activity, knowledge development programs, and oversight functions. The General Assembly shall review College performance and shall act when a College is failing its mandate.

**§36. Accountability to Community.** Colleges are ultimately accountable to the community. Any member may petition the General Assembly regarding a College's conduct, standards, or performance. The community retains override authority over all College decisions and functions. No College action is beyond community reach.

**§37. Preventing Capture.** Colleges are vulnerable to capture by incumbent members who use credentialing standards to exclude qualified practitioners or maintain insider dominance. The General Assembly shall monitor College credentialing practices and shall act when standards appear designed to exclude rather than to ensure competence. Open, accessible, and fairly administered credentialing is a condition of General Assembly recognition.

**§38. Transparency.** Each College shall maintain transparency in its membership, credentialing processes, and governance activities. Membership lists, credentialing standards, and committee activity shall be available to any member of the Society. A College that operates in opacity is not operating as a community institution.

**§39. Annual Report.** Each College shall publish an annual report covering: total membership; new members credentialed; members who have left; credentialing standards in effect; knowledge development and training activities; committee service fulfilled; service oversight activities; and any significant developments in College governance or domain knowledge. Reports shall be presented to the General Assembly and made available to all members.

## Article VIII — The Colleges of the Society

The following Colleges are recognized by the Ben Franklin Society. This list represents the Colleges the community has established or anticipates establishing as knowledge bases and practitioner communities develop. It is not a closed list. The General Assembly may establish additional Colleges at any time. All Colleges, whether named here or established in the future, are governed by the principles established in this document.

### Justice and Safety

- **College of Conciliation.** Mediation, conflict resolution, accountability proceedings, community navigation, and character assessment for the College of the Charge. The most select College in the Society. Credentialed by recognized character, emotional intelligence, and wisdom rather than examination alone. Supplies mediators, conciliators, accountability panel members, and community navigators.
- **College of the Charge.** Community physical safety, de-escalation practice, and constabulary operations. Highly select. Joint character assessment with College of Conciliation. Explicitly selects against authoritarian personality. Supplies constables and safety practitioners to the Constabulary Service.
- **College of Emergency Services.** Fire suppression, emergency medical response, search and rescue, hazardous materials response, and disaster coordination. Supplies practitioners to the Fire and Emergency Service.

### Health and Care

- **Medical College.** Broad clinical medicine, diagnosis, treatment, community health worker standards, and medical knowledge base. The founding health College from which specialized health Colleges emerge. Joint oversight of Health Service with Caregivers and Mother's Colleges.
- **College of Physicians.** Diagnosis, clinical treatment, prescribing authority, and physician practice standards.
- **College of Dentistry.** Dental practice, oral health, and dental care standards.
- **College of Nursing.** Nursing practice, patient care, clinical support, and care coordination.
- **College of Pharmacy.** Pharmaceutical knowledge, medication management, dispensing standards, and botanical medicine.
- **College of Paramedicine.** Emergency medical response, pre-hospital care, trauma response, and EMS practice.
- **College of Mental Health.** Psychology, counseling, addiction treatment, psychiatric support, and mental health practice standards.
- **Caregivers College.** Dependent care for elderly, disabled, and those requiring ongoing support. Joint oversight of Health Service, Community Care Service, and Childcare Service. Advises on disability spectrum assessment for Social Insurance Fund.
- **Mother's College.** Pregnancy, birth, postpartum recovery, infant care, and reproductive health. The only College credentialed by lived experience rather than examination — membership is open to any woman member who has experienced pregnancy in any form including loss. Oversight of maternal functions within the Health Service.

### Agriculture and Food

- **Agricultural College.** Farming practice, soil science, food production, and seed saving. Oversees Agricultural Service and Food Service. The knowledge base for the community's food sovereignty.
- **Culinary Arts College.** Food preparation, kitchen management, food safety, food preservation, fermentation, nutritional practice, and food culture. Oversight of kitchen and food preparation functions within the Food Service alongside the Agricultural College.

### Education

- **Educators College.** Teaching, curriculum development, library science, adult education, civic education, and home education support. Oversees Education Service. The connective tissue between the knowledge of all other Colleges and the broader community membership.

### Physical Infrastructure

- **College of Engineers.** Systems planning, infrastructure design, technical standards across physical domains, water systems, and energy systems integration. The broad founding infrastructure College from which more specialized Colleges emerge as practitioner communities develop sufficient depth.
- **College of Builders.** Structural construction, foundations, framing, masonry, civil infrastructure, and roads.
- **College of Electricians.** Electrical systems, wiring, solar installation, battery systems, and electrical safety standards.
- **College of Plumbers.** Water systems, drainage, septic systems, pipe work, and sanitation infrastructure.
- **College of Fabricators.** Welding, machining, metal forming, and woodworking. Oversight of manufacturing functions within the Manufacturing Service.

---

Additional Colleges shall be established by the General Assembly as the community's needs and knowledge bases develop. The list above is a beginning, not a boundary.
