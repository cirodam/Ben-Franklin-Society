# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Commerce Service
*Article XI of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

The Frank circulates most visibly and most personally in the marketplace. The community's Services provide for foundational needs — food, health, education, financial security — but the long tail of human economic life extends far beyond what any institution can centrally plan or provide. Surplus produce, skilled labor, handmade goods, professional knowledge, spare time, creative work — these flow between members through commerce. The Commerce Service exists to facilitate that flow: creating the spaces, the tools, and the infrastructure through which members can offer and find what they need from one another. It does not govern commerce. It enables it.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Purpose.** The Commerce Service facilitates exchange between members of the Society and, through the federation clearinghouse, between members of different Ben Franklin Societies. It establishes and maintains the physical and virtual infrastructure through which members offer goods and services to one another, denominated in Franks.

**§2. Frank Circulation.** The marketplace is the primary venue through which Franks circulate among ordinary members in everyday economic life. The Commerce Service shall design and operate its infrastructure with Frank circulation as a central purpose — ensuring that transactions are easy, that teller support is available, and that no member is excluded from economic participation by barriers to Frank transactions.

**§3. Facilitation Not Governance.** The Commerce Service facilitates commerce — it does not govern it. It creates the conditions for exchange without directing what is exchanged or at what price. Standards of safety and fair dealing applicable to specific goods and services are the domain of the relevant Colleges. The Commerce Service maintains the marketplace; the community governs what happens within it.

**§4. Functions.** The Commerce Service carries out its mandate through two primary functions: the physical marketplace and the virtual marketplace. Together these constitute the Society's commercial infrastructure — one building social fabric through in-person exchange, the other extending economic activity across the full membership continuously.

**§5. Oversight.** The Commerce Service is overseen directly by the Assembly. Commerce is a general human activity that does not belong to any specialized domain. A standing committee of the Assembly, drawn by sortition from the general eligible membership, shall handle marketplace governance, dispute resolution, and virtual marketplace moderation on the Assembly's behalf.

---

## Article II — The Physical Marketplace

**§6. Establishment.** The Commerce Service shall establish and operate at least one regular physical marketplace where members may offer goods and services to one another. The physical marketplace is a community institution and a social occasion as much as an economic one — a place where members meet, relationships form around transactions, and the community's economic life becomes visible.

**§7. Physical Infrastructure.** The Commerce Service shall establish marketplaces using whatever physical infrastructure is accessible to the community — parking lots, fairgrounds, community halls, open fields, church grounds, and such other spaces as are available and suitable. The Commerce Service does not require dedicated purpose-built market infrastructure. It works with what the community has.

**§8. Coordinators.** Each physical marketplace session shall be staffed by one or two coordinators responsible for managing the space, supporting vendors, resolving minor disputes, and ensuring the marketplace runs smoothly. Coordinators are nominated staff of the Commerce Service serving defined terms.

**§9. Tellers.** Each physical marketplace session shall be staffed by tellers capable of facilitating Frank transactions for members who need assistance. Tellers operate as an outpost of the Community Bank at the marketplace, enabling members to conduct account transactions in person during market hours. The Commerce Service shall coordinate with the Community Bank to ensure teller capacity at each marketplace session.

**§10. Participation.** Any member in good standing may offer goods or services at the physical marketplace. Participation is open. *[Placeholder: vendor registration, space allocation, and marketplace rules to be established in standing rules.]*

**§11. Service Listings.** The Society's Services may participate in the physical marketplace to make their offerings visible and accessible to members — the Food Service listing pantry stock, the Agricultural Service listing seed library offerings, and so on. Service participation shall not crowd out member vendors.

## Article III — The Virtual Marketplace

**§13. Purpose.** The virtual marketplace extends the community's commercial infrastructure beyond the limits of physical market days. It allows members to list goods and services, find what they need, and transact in Franks continuously — not only during market hours and not only among those who can attend in person. It is the Society's equivalent of a community-owned exchange platform, serving members rather than extracting value from them.

**§14. Listings.** Any member in good standing may list goods and services on the virtual marketplace. Listings shall include sufficient description for members to understand what is being offered and at what price in Franks. The Commerce Service shall maintain the virtual marketplace as a reliable and easy-to-use platform. *[Placeholder: listing standards and moderation procedures to be established in standing rules.]*

**§15. Service Listings.** The Society's Services may list their offerings on the virtual marketplace. The virtual marketplace is the natural home for a continuously updated directory of what the community's Services provide and how members can access them.

**§16. Frank Transactions.** Virtual marketplace transactions shall be denominated in Franks and shall settle through the Community Bank ledger. The Commerce Service shall maintain integration with the Community Bank sufficient to enable virtual marketplace transactions to settle accurately and promptly.

**§17. Universal Access.** The virtual marketplace shall be accessible to all members regardless of their personal access to digital technology. The Commerce Service shall maintain physical access terminals at the Community Bank and at such other Society locations as the Assembly determines sufficient to ensure that no member is excluded from virtual marketplace participation by lack of personal connectivity. Rural connectivity constraints are a known condition — the Commerce Service plans for them.

**§18. Moderation.** The virtual marketplace shall be moderated by the Commerce Service's standing committee to ensure listings are honest, transactions are conducted in good faith, and the platform serves its purpose. Moderation shall be light-touch — the goal is a functional marketplace, not a controlled one. *[Placeholder: moderation standards and procedures to be established in standing rules.]*

**§19. Resilience.** The virtual marketplace shall be maintained with sufficient resilience to survive disruption to digital systems. The physical marketplace remains functional when the virtual marketplace is unavailable. The two systems are complementary — neither depends on the other to function.

## Article IV — Inter-Society Commerce

**§20. Member Right.** Members of the Ben Franklin Society may conduct trade with members of other Ben Franklin Societies. Inter-society commerce is a right of membership in the federation and an expression of the mutual aid relationships between communities. The Commerce Service shall facilitate inter-society trade through both its physical and virtual marketplace infrastructure.

**§21. Common Currency.** All Ben Franklin Societies issue the Frank as their common currency. Inter-society trade is therefore conducted in Franks without currency conversion. A Frank earned in one society spends in another. This common currency is among the most powerful expressions of federation solidarity.

**§22. Clearinghouse Settlement.** Inter-society Floren transactions settle through the federation clearinghouse — a multilateral accounting system that tracks the flow of Florens between societies. Franks are locally tied and cannot leave their issuing society; only Florens move between societies. The clearinghouse is addressed in the federation foundational documents. Individual Commerce Service transactions that cross society boundaries are recorded locally and reported to the clearinghouse for settlement.

**§23. Rebalancing.** The clearinghouse may redistribute Florens between societies in cases of severe trade imbalance that threaten a society's economic stability. This rebalancing mechanism protects societies from Floren depletion while maintaining healthy trade relationships. Its mechanics are defined in the federation foundational documents.

**§24. Visiting Members.** Members of other Ben Franklin Societies attending a local physical marketplace shall be welcomed as trading partners. Tellers shall be capable of facilitating Frank transactions for visiting members through clearinghouse protocols. *[Placeholder: inter-society transaction protocols to be established in coordination with federation standards.]*

## Article V — Dispute Resolution

**§25. Minor Disputes.** Minor disputes arising from marketplace transactions — disagreements over quality, delivery, or price — shall be handled by the Commerce Service's coordinators or standing committee in the first instance. The goal is resolution through good faith mediation rather than formal process wherever possible.

**§26. Serious Disputes.** Disputes that cannot be resolved by the Commerce Service, or that involve allegations of fraud or serious bad faith, shall be referred to the Assembly. The Assembly may convene an ad hoc committee to investigate and resolve such disputes. *[Placeholder: dispute resolution process and escalation procedures to be established in standing rules.]*

**§27. Frank Reversals.** In cases where a transaction is found to have been fraudulent or conducted in serious bad faith, the Commerce Service may recommend to the Community Bank that a Frank transaction be reversed. Reversals require Assembly authorization. *[Placeholder: reversal process to be established in standing rules in coordination with Community Bank procedures.]*

---

## Article VI — Standards and Fair Dealing

**§28. Fair Dealing.** Members participating in the marketplace — physical or virtual — are expected to deal honestly and in good faith. Honest representation of goods and services, fair pricing, and fulfillment of commitments are expectations of marketplace participation consistent with the Society's broader membership obligations.

**§29. Product Safety.** Standards of safety applicable to specific goods — food safety, structural integrity, medical products — are the domain of the relevant Colleges. The Commerce Service shall coordinate with the relevant College when safety concerns arise in the marketplace and shall act on College recommendations regarding unsafe products.

**§30. Weights and Measures.** The Commerce Service shall establish and maintain standards for weights and measures used in marketplace transactions. Honest measurement is the foundation of fair trade. *[Placeholder: weights and measures standards to be established in standing rules.]*

---

## Article VII — Transparency and Reporting

**§31. Annual Report.** The Commerce Service shall publish an annual report covering: physical marketplace sessions held, vendor participation, and estimated transaction volumes; virtual marketplace listings, active users, and transaction volumes; inter-society trade volumes; dispute resolution activity; and any significant developments in marketplace infrastructure or policy. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§32. Frank Circulation.** The Commerce Service shall report to the Assembly on Frank circulation patterns in the marketplace — what is trading, at what volumes, and between whom in aggregate. This data informs the Assembly's understanding of the community's economic life and the Frank's health as a circulating currency.

**§33. Member Privacy.** Aggregate marketplace data is public. Individual member transaction data is private and shall not be disclosed without member consent, consistent with the Community Bank's privacy standards.
