# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Childcare Service
*Article XIV of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Children are the community's future and their care is the community's present obligation. The Childcare Service exists because the community understands two things clearly: that children deserve safe, nurturing, developmentally appropriate care in their earliest years, and that parents cannot fully participate in the life of the community without knowing their children are well cared for. Childcare is not a private arrangement to be solved individually by each family — it is community infrastructure as essential as any other. The people who provide it deserve recognition, training, and fair compensation. The children who receive it deserve quality and continuity. The parents who depend on it deserve reliability.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Obligation.** The Childcare Service is charged with ensuring that children of members from birth to school age have access to quality care, and that parents and guardians can participate fully in community life knowing their children are safe and well cared for. Childcare is community infrastructure — it enables everything else the community does.

**§2. Distributed Model.** The Childcare Service operates primarily through family childcare settings and in-home care rather than center-based facilities. Family childcare — a credentialed caregiver looking after a small group of children in their own home — is well-suited to the community's rural character and is more resilient than large centralized facilities. Center-based care may be established by the Assembly where community need and capacity warrant it.

**§3. Age Range.** The Childcare Service serves children from birth to school age. At school age children transition to the Education Service's community school. The Childcare Service and Education Service shall maintain a smooth and supportive transition process for each child.

**§4. Childcare as Valued Labor.** Childcare is skilled, essential work. The systematic undervaluation and undercompensation of childcare labor is among the most consequential failures of the current economic system. The Childcare Service shall treat childcare as the skilled professional work it is — compensating caregivers fairly in Franks, training them well, credentialing them through the appropriate Colleges, and recognizing their contribution as foundational to community life.

**§5. Functions.** The Childcare Service carries out its mandate through the following functions: childcare placement and coordination; caregiver compensation; caregiver credentialing and training; caregiver capacity development; emergency childcare response; and transition coordination with the Education Service.

**§6. Oversight.** The Childcare Service is overseen jointly by the Educators College and the Caregivers College. The Educators College holds primary responsibility for developmental and educational standards in childcare settings. The Caregivers College holds primary responsibility for health, wellbeing, and care quality standards. The two Colleges shall establish clear protocols for their joint oversight.

## Article II — Childcare Settings

**§7. Family Childcare.** Family childcare settings — in which a credentialed caregiver cares for a small group of children in the caregiver's own home — are the primary setting for Childcare Service provision. Family childcare is appropriate across the full age range from birth to school age, is well-suited to rural community geography, and provides the continuity and relationship depth that young children particularly benefit from. *[Placeholder: maximum group sizes by age to be established in standing rules with Educators and Caregivers College input.]*

**§8. In-Home Care.** In-home care — in which a credentialed caregiver provides care in the child's own home — is available for infants, for children with particular needs, and for families whose circumstances make family childcare settings less suitable. In-home care may also be provided by a family member who meets credentialing requirements and receives compensation through the Service budget.

**§9. Center-Based Care.** The Childcare Service may establish center-based care facilities where the Assembly determines that community need and available capacity warrant them. Center-based care is not a founding requirement but a development option. Where established, centers shall be operated with the same quality standards and caregiver compensation principles as family and in-home settings.

**§10. Quality Standards.** All childcare settings operating through the Childcare Service shall meet quality standards established by the Educators and Caregivers Colleges. Standards shall address the physical environment, caregiver-to-child ratios, developmental programming, health and safety practices, and nutrition. Quality is not optional — it is the obligation the community takes on when it accepts responsibility for children's care.

**§11. Resilience.** The distributed family childcare model is inherently more resilient than center-based care. The Childcare Service shall maintain sufficient caregiver capacity across the community that the unavailability of any single caregiver does not leave families without options. Backup care arrangements shall be part of every family's care plan.

## Article III — Placement and Coordination

**§12. Placement Function.** The Childcare Service shall match children with appropriate caregivers and settings based on the child's age and needs, the family's circumstances and preferences, caregiver availability and capacity, and geographic proximity. Good placement — matching that works well for the child, the family, and the caregiver — is a core service function. Poor placement undermines everything else.

**§13. Care Plans.** Each child receiving care through the Childcare Service shall have a care plan developed in collaboration with the family and the caregiver. Care plans shall document the child's needs, routines, developmental goals, health considerations, and emergency contacts. They shall be reviewed regularly and updated as the child develops. The family's voice in their child's care plan is essential.

**§14. Family Partnership.** The Childcare Service shall treat families as partners in their children's care, not as recipients of a service. Families have knowledge of their children that no caregiver or service can replicate. Caregivers and families shall maintain open communication, shared understanding of the child's development, and genuine collaboration in the child's daily care.

**§15. Coordination with Other Services.** The Childcare Service shall coordinate with the Health Service on children's health needs, the Community Care Service where families have overlapping care needs, and the Social Insurance Fund where family circumstances affect childcare access. No family navigating multiple services shall fall through the gap between them.

## Article IV — Caregiver Compensation

**§16. Compensation Mandate.** All caregivers providing childcare through the Childcare Service shall be compensated in Franks through the Service budget. Compensation is not conditional on whether the caregiver is a family member or an unrelated community member — care is care and it is compensated. The rate of compensation shall reflect the skill, responsibility, and community importance of childcare work.

**§17. Family Caregivers.** A family member who provides childcare for their own young children while also caring for other children in a family childcare setting is eligible for compensation through the Service. The community recognizes that family-based childcare is real work that enables community participation and shall compensate it accordingly.

**§18. Compensation Rates.** Compensation rates shall be established by the Assembly with input from the Educators and Caregivers Colleges and reviewed annually. Rates shall reflect the number of children in care, the age range served, and any special needs requirements. *[Placeholder: compensation rates and structures to be established in standing rules.]*

## Article V — Credentialing and Training

**§19. Credentialing Requirement.** All caregivers providing childcare through the Childcare Service shall be credentialed by the Educators and Caregivers Colleges. Credentialing ensures that children are cared for by people with demonstrated knowledge and competence in child development, health and safety, and childcare practice. It is a quality assurance mechanism and a professional recognition.

**§20. Credentialing Standards.** Credentialing standards shall be established jointly by the Educators and Caregivers Colleges. Standards shall cover child development knowledge, health and safety practices, nutrition, emergency response, communication with families, and recognition of developmental concerns requiring specialist referral. Standards shall be appropriate to the setting — infant care, toddler care, and preschool age care have different requirements. *[Placeholder: credentialing standards to be established by Colleges.]*

**§21. Training.** The Childcare Service shall provide training to caregivers seeking credentialing and ongoing professional development to credentialed caregivers. Training shall be accessible — offered at times and in formats that work for people who may already be caring for children. The Childcare Service shall not create credentialing requirements without providing a clear and supported path to meeting them.

**§22. First Aid and Emergency Response.** All credentialed caregivers shall be trained in pediatric first aid and emergency response. Children's emergencies require immediate competent response. This training shall be maintained current and is a non-negotiable component of credentialing.

**§23. Ongoing Development.** Credentialed caregivers shall participate in ongoing professional development. Child development knowledge evolves. Best practices in early childhood care develop. Caregivers who stop learning stop improving. The Childcare Service shall make ongoing development accessible and shall treat it as a normal expectation of credentialed practice.

## Article VI — Caregiver Capacity Development

**§24. Mandate.** The Childcare Service shall actively develop childcare caregiver capacity within the community. The availability of quality childcare depends entirely on the availability of quality caregivers. The Service shall not wait for caregivers to present themselves — it shall actively recruit, train, and credential new caregivers from within the membership to meet the community's needs.

**§25. Recruitment.** The Childcare Service shall actively recruit community members to childcare practice — communicating the value of the work, the compensation available, the training provided, and the community importance of the role. Members who might not have considered childcare as a contribution to community life should understand that it is among the most important contributions they can make.

**§26. Pipeline.** The Childcare Service shall maintain a pipeline of caregivers in training so that capacity can grow with community need. A community whose childcare capacity cannot expand to meet need is a community that will fail families when families most need support.

**§27. Caregiver Support.** The Childcare Service shall actively support caregivers in their work — through peer networks, mentorship from experienced caregivers, access to resources, and recognition of their contribution. Childcare can be isolating work, particularly in family settings. The Service shall ensure that caregivers are connected to each other and to the broader community of practice.

## Article VII — Emergency Childcare

**§28. Purpose.** Families face acute crises that require immediate childcare without the normal placement process — a parent's medical emergency, a household crisis, an unexpected work situation. The Childcare Service shall maintain an emergency childcare function capable of placing children in safe, credentialed care on short notice when families face these situations.

**§29. Readiness.** The emergency childcare function shall be maintained in a state of readiness — with identified caregivers available for emergency placement, clear protocols for rapid response, and coordination with the Community Care Service and Health Service for situations where the family's crisis involves those services as well.

**§30. Transition to Regular Care.** Emergency childcare is a bridge. The Childcare Service shall work to transition children from emergency placements to stable regular care arrangements as quickly as the family's circumstances allow. Emergency caregivers and regular caregivers may be the same people — stability for the child is the goal.

---

## Article VIII — Transition to the Education Service

**§31. Smooth Transition.** When a child reaches school age and transitions from the Childcare Service to the Education Service's community school, the transition shall be managed with the child's continuity and wellbeing as the primary consideration. School entry is a significant transition for young children. The Childcare Service and Education Service shall collaborate to make it as smooth and supported as possible.

**§32. Information Transfer.** With family consent, relevant information about the child's development, needs, and care history shall be shared between the Childcare Service and the Education Service to support continuity of care and educational planning. Information transfer shall serve the child — it is not a bureaucratic exercise.

**§33. School Readiness.** The Childcare Service's developmental programming shall support school readiness — not as a narrow academic preparation but as broad developmental preparation for the social, emotional, and cognitive demands of school life. Children who arrive at school ready to learn and relate make the Education Service's work more effective and their own early school experience more positive.

**§34. After-School Care.** The Childcare Service and Education Service shall coordinate on after-school care needs for school-age children whose parents are working or otherwise engaged in community activities. After-school care may be provided through the Childcare Service, the Education Service, or a joint arrangement as the Assembly determines appropriate.

## Article IX — Child Safety and Wellbeing

**§35. Paramount Obligation.** The safety and wellbeing of children in the Childcare Service's care is the paramount obligation from which all other functions derive. Every standard, every credentialing requirement, every quality measure exists to serve this obligation. When any other consideration conflicts with child safety and wellbeing, child safety and wellbeing prevail.

**§36. Reporting.** Credentialed caregivers are mandatory reporters of suspected harm to children in their care. The Childcare Service shall establish clear protocols for reporting suspected harm — to the Health Service, to the accountability function, and where necessary to external child protective systems. No caregiver shall face retaliation for making a good-faith report of suspected harm.

**§37. Developmental Concerns.** Caregivers shall be trained to recognize developmental concerns — delays, behavioral changes, signs of distress — and to communicate these to families and, with family consent, to the Health Service. Early identification of developmental concerns is one of the most valuable contributions quality childcare makes to children's long-term outcomes.

**§38. Privacy.** Individual child and family records held by the Childcare Service are strictly private. Information about a child's care, development, or family circumstances shall not be disclosed without family consent except as required for the delivery of care, a mandatory reporting obligation, or an immediate safety necessity.

---

## Article X — Transparency and Reporting

**§39. Annual Report.** The Childcare Service shall publish an annual report covering: number of children served by setting type and age range; number of credentialed caregivers; caregiver compensation totals; training and credentialing activity; emergency childcare responses; capacity gaps and development activity; transition coordination with the Education Service; and any significant developments in childcare capacity or policy. All data shall be aggregate. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§40. Capacity Transparency.** The Childcare Service shall communicate clearly to members what childcare is currently available, what the wait for placement is if applicable, and what gaps exist in current capacity. Families planning for childcare need honest information about what the community can provide and when.
