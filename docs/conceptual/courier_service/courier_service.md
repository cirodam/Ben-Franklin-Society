# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Courier Service
*Article XXII of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Benjamin Franklin served as the first Postmaster General of the United States and understood the post as foundational civic infrastructure — the means by which a community communicates with itself, by which documents and goods move reliably between members, by which the threads of community life are kept connected across distance. The Courier Service stands in that tradition. It delivers mail, packages, and such other items as members need moved within the community, by whatever means are available, on whatever schedule resources allow. It is not a commercial delivery company. It is a community institution that keeps the community connected — and whose importance grows precisely when external postal systems become unreliable.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Purpose.** The Courier Service provides reliable delivery of mail and packages within the community. It is the community's internal postal system — carrying correspondence, documents, transaction slips, goods, medications, and such other items as members need delivered between them. Its purpose is community connectivity: ensuring that distance within the community is not a barrier to communication or exchange.

**§2. Resilience Function.** The Courier Service is community infrastructure whose importance grows as external postal and delivery systems become less reliable. A community with its own internal courier capacity remains connected regardless of what happens to national postal systems. The Service shall be designed and maintained with this resilience function explicitly in mind.

**§3. Resource Realism.** The Courier Service operates within the community's actual resources — available vehicles, personnel, fuel, and infrastructure. Delivery schedules and coverage shall reflect what the community can genuinely sustain. Honest, reliable service within realistic limits is more valuable than promised service that fails.

**§4. Mode of Travel.** The Courier Service shall use whatever mode of travel is available and appropriate — vehicles for rural distances, bicycles where practical, foot where necessary. Mode diversity increases resilience. The Service shall not be dependent on any single mode of transport.

**§5. Universal Reach.** The Courier Service shall reach every member of the Society. No member's distance from the community's center shall prevent them from sending and receiving mail and packages. Routes shall be designed to cover the full geographic area of the community's membership.

**§6. Oversight.** The Courier Service is overseen by the College of the Post — a peer body of practitioners who maintain expertise in route management, reliable delivery, handling of sensitive items, and the logistics of community-scale courier operations. The College of the Post stands in the tradition of the post rider: trusted, knowledgeable, essential to community connectivity.

## Article II — What the Service Delivers

**§7. Mail and Correspondence.** The Courier Service delivers letters, documents, and correspondence between members and between members and community institutions. Written correspondence remains essential even in communities with digital communication — for formal matters, for members without digital access, and as a resilient backup when digital systems fail.

**§8. Packages and Goods.** The Courier Service delivers packages and goods of appropriate size and weight between members and from community services to members. *[Placeholder: size and weight limits to be established in standing rules based on available transport capacity.]*

**§9. Transaction Slips.** The Courier Service shall deliver Frank transaction slips between members and to the Community Bank for ledger reconciliation. Transaction slip delivery is a financial infrastructure function supporting the Community Bank's paper backup system. The Courier Service and Community Bank shall maintain clear protocols for transaction slip handling.

**§10. Medications and Health Items.** The Courier Service shall deliver medications and health items from the Health Service to members who cannot collect them in person. Medication delivery requires secure and confidential handling. The Courier Service and Health Service shall establish clear protocols for the secure handling of medications and health items.

**§11. Food Pantry Items.** The Courier Service shall support the Food Service's delivery function by carrying pantry items and prepared food to homebound members as capacity allows. Coordination between the Courier Service and Food Service on food delivery shall be established in standing rules.

**§12. Official Documents.** The Courier Service shall deliver official community documents — Assembly notices, College communications, service notifications, and such other official correspondence as community institutions need to reach members. Every member's ability to participate in community governance depends in part on reliably receiving official communications.

**§13. Commerce Service Integration.** The Courier Service shall coordinate with the Commerce Service on local delivery of goods purchased through the virtual marketplace. A virtual marketplace that can deliver purchased goods increases its practical utility for members who cannot collect in person.

## Article III — Routes and Scheduling

**§14. Route Design.** The College of the Post shall design delivery routes covering the full geographic area of the community's membership. Routes shall be designed for reliability and efficiency given the community's actual transport capacity and reviewed regularly as the community evolves.

**§15. Scheduling.** Delivery schedules shall be established based on available resources and community need. Schedules shall be published and reliable — members shall know when to expect delivery and when to have items ready for collection. *[Placeholder: delivery frequency and schedule to be established in standing rules based on available capacity.]*

**§16. Collection Points.** The Courier Service shall maintain collection points where members can drop off items for delivery — at the Community Bank, the Commerce Service marketplace, and such other convenient locations as the College of the Post determines appropriate.

**§17. Route Resilience.** Routes shall be designed with resilience in mind — alternative routes when primary routes are unavailable, alternative transport modes when primary vehicles cannot operate, and sufficient courier coverage that the absence of any single courier does not leave a route unserved.

## Article IV — Handling and Security

**§18. Care of Items.** The Courier Service shall handle all items with care appropriate to their nature. Mail and documents shall be protected from weather and damage. Packages shall be handled without breakage. Medications shall be maintained at appropriate conditions. The College of the Post shall establish handling standards for each category of item the Service delivers.

**§19. Confidentiality.** Couriers handle private correspondence, financial documents, and medical items. The confidentiality of items in the Courier Service's possession is absolute. Couriers shall not open, read, or disclose the contents of any item in their care. Breach of confidentiality is a serious violation of member trust.

**§20. Sensitive Items.** Transaction slips, medications, and official documents require particular handling care and security. The Courier Service shall establish specific protocols for each sensitive item category — secure carrying, chain of custody documentation, confirmed delivery, and such other measures as the College of the Post and relevant partner services determine necessary.

**§21. Delivery Confirmation.** The Courier Service shall maintain delivery records sufficient to confirm that items were delivered and received. Delivery confirmation is particularly important for transaction slips, medications, and official documents.

**§22. Lost or Damaged Items.** The Courier Service shall maintain a process for reporting and addressing lost or damaged items. Members whose items are lost or damaged by the Service shall have recourse. The College of the Post shall establish procedures for investigating and resolving such matters in good faith.

---

## Article V — Courier Character and Trust

**§23. Trust as Foundation.** The post rider was among the most trusted figures in early American community life — entrusted with correspondence, financial documents, and the private communications of neighbors. The Courier Service operates in that tradition. Its effectiveness depends entirely on community trust that items entrusted to it will be delivered intact, confidentially, and on time.

**§24. Credentialing.** The College of the Post shall credential couriers — assessing their knowledge of routes, their reliability, their understanding of handling and confidentiality requirements, and their character as trusted community members.

**§25. Community Knowledge.** Couriers develop intimate knowledge of the community's geography, its members, and its patterns of life. This knowledge is valuable and shall be developed, maintained, and shared within the College of the Post. A courier who knows every road and every household is a community resource beyond their delivery function.

**§26. Wellbeing Observation.** Couriers who regularly visit members — particularly elderly and isolated members — are often among the first to notice when something is wrong. The Courier Service shall establish protocols for couriers to report welfare concerns to the Community Care Service when their regular contact with a member gives them reason for concern. This observation function is secondary to the delivery function and entirely voluntary for the member — it is an expression of community care, not surveillance.

---

## Article VI — Inter-Society Courier Coordination

**§27. Federation Post.** The Courier Service shall coordinate with the courier services of other Ben Franklin Societies to enable mail and package delivery between societies. A federation whose member societies can communicate and exchange goods reliably is a more integrated and resilient federation. The College of the Post shall develop inter-society delivery protocols in coordination with other societies' Colleges of the Post.

**§28. Transaction Slip Exchange.** Inter-society Frank transactions generate transaction slips that must reach the appropriate Community Banks for ledger reconciliation and clearinghouse settlement. The Courier Service shall coordinate with other societies' courier services on inter-society transaction slip delivery — establishing reliable exchange points and handoff protocols.

**§29. External Post.** The Courier Service shall coordinate with external postal systems for mail and packages that originate outside the community or are addressed to destinations beyond the federation. External postal relationships shall be maintained while community courier capacity is developed, so that members can send and receive from the broader world.

---

## Article VII — Transparency and Reporting

**§30. Annual Report.** The Courier Service shall publish an annual report covering: delivery volumes by category; route coverage and any gaps; transport modes in use; delivery reliability metrics; inter-society delivery activity; lost or damaged item reports and resolutions; and any significant developments in courier capacity or operations. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§31. Schedule Transparency.** Current delivery schedules and collection point locations shall be published and made available to all members. Members shall always know when and where they can send and receive items through the Courier Service.

**§32. Item Confidentiality.** While delivery volumes and schedules are public, the contents and parties of individual deliveries are strictly private. The Courier Service shall maintain confidentiality of all individual delivery information as an absolute obligation.
