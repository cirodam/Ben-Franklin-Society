# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Housing Service
*Article XIX of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Shelter is the second most fundamental human need after food. A community that guarantees its members food but not shelter has only half-fulfilled its obligation. The Ben Franklin Society commits to ensuring that every member has somewhere adequate to sleep — not as an aspiration but as a standing obligation. When a member loses their shelter, the community responds. When a member cannot maintain their home, the community helps. When a home is cold and deteriorating, the community acts. Housing is not an investment asset in this community. It is a human necessity and the community's responsibility.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Obligation.** The Housing Service is charged with ensuring that every member of the Ben Franklin Society has access to adequate shelter. No member shall be without a place to sleep through failure of the community to act. This obligation is absolute — it applies regardless of a member's circumstances, financial situation, or prior housing history.

**§2. Housing as Necessity.** The Housing Service approaches housing as a human necessity rather than an investment asset. Its functions are designed to ensure that members have adequate, maintained, and livable shelter — not to manage housing as a market commodity. The community's interest in housing is its members' wellbeing, not property value.

**§3. Functions.** The Housing Service carries out its mandate through the following functions: shelter guarantee and emergency housing; housing assessment; maintenance and repair support; weatherization; housing assistance coordination; and inter-society coordination.

**§4. Private Homes.** Members' homes are privately owned. The Housing Service does not own, manage, or control members' private housing. It serves members in maintaining and accessing adequate shelter. The Service relationship with members is one of support, not management.

**§5. Oversight.** The Housing Service is overseen by the Engineering College. The Engineering College sets standards for housing adequacy, maintenance quality, and weatherization practice, and credentials the skilled trades practitioners who carry out the Service's maintenance and repair functions.

---

## Article II — The Shelter Guarantee

**§6. The Guarantee.** The Ben Franklin Society guarantees that no member will be without adequate shelter. This guarantee is the Housing Service's most fundamental obligation and the standard against which all its other functions are measured. When the guarantee is at risk for any member, the Housing Service acts.

**§7. Adequacy.** Adequate shelter means shelter that is structurally sound, weatherproof, safe from immediate hazard, and appropriate to the member's needs. It does not mean perfect housing — it means housing that a person can live in with dignity. The Engineering College shall establish minimum adequacy standards. *[Placeholder: minimum adequacy standards to be established by Engineering College with Assembly approval.]*

**§8. Proactive Identification.** The Housing Service shall not wait for members to present themselves as homeless or housing-insecure. It shall actively identify members whose housing is inadequate or at risk — through coordination with the Health Service, Community Care Service, and College of Conciliation — and shall reach out to provide support before shelter is lost entirely.

**§9. No Member Outside.** A member without shelter shall not remain without shelter for want of community action. The Housing Service shall have authority to act immediately when a member has no place to sleep — finding available space, coordinating with other members and services, and placing the member in adequate temporary shelter while longer-term solutions are developed. Speed matters. A member sleeping outside tonight cannot wait for a committee to meet.

## Article III — Emergency Housing

**§10. Emergency Mandate.** When a member is displaced from their home — by fire, by disaster, by domestic crisis, or by any other acute circumstance — the Housing Service shall provide emergency shelter immediately. Emergency housing is a 24-hour obligation. Displacement does not wait for business hours.

**§11. Available Space.** The Housing Service shall use whatever space is available to shelter displaced members — community buildings, shared households, the homes of members willing to host, and such other spaces as the community can make available. The Service shall maintain a current knowledge of available emergency shelter options within the community and shall develop relationships that make rapid placement possible.

**§12. Fire and Emergency Service Coordination.** The Fire and Emergency Service responds to the incident that causes displacement. The Housing Service responds to where displaced members sleep. The two services shall maintain clear coordination protocols so that members displaced by fire or disaster are connected with the Housing Service immediately and without gap.

**§13. Transition to Stable Housing.** Emergency housing is a bridge. The Housing Service shall work actively to transition members from emergency placement to stable long-term housing as quickly as their circumstances allow. Emergency placement is not a permanent solution — it is immediate care while the path to stability is found.

**§14. Dignity.** Emergency housing shall be provided with full regard for member dignity. Displaced members are in crisis — they shall not be made to feel that the community's help is charity or that their situation diminishes their standing. The Housing Service shall ensure that emergency placement preserves the member's privacy, autonomy, and sense of self as much as circumstances allow.

---

## Article IV — Housing Assessment

**§15. Assessment Function.** The Housing Service shall offer housing assessments to members — evaluating the condition of their homes, identifying maintenance needs, safety hazards, and weatherization opportunities, and connecting members with appropriate support. Assessments are offered as a service, not conducted as an inspection. The Housing Service serves members; it does not regulate their private homes.

**§16. Priority Assessment.** The Housing Service shall prioritize assessments for members most likely to have housing adequacy concerns — elderly members, disabled members, members with limited financial resources, and members identified through coordination with the Community Care Service and Health Service as having housing-related health risks.

**§17. Assessment Outcomes.** Following an assessment the Housing Service shall provide the member with a clear picture of their home's condition, a prioritized list of maintenance and improvement needs, and a plan for addressing those needs through the Service's maintenance and repair function, weatherization program, or housing assistance coordination as appropriate.

---

## Article V — Maintenance and Repair Support

**§18. Mandate.** The Housing Service shall provide maintenance and repair support to members who cannot maintain adequate shelter on their own — due to age, disability, physical limitation, or financial constraint. A home that deteriorates because its occupant cannot maintain it is a home that will eventually fail to provide adequate shelter. Maintenance support prevents that failure before it becomes a crisis.

**§19. Skilled Staff.** The Housing Service shall employ paid staff with skills in the trades required for home maintenance and repair — carpentry, plumbing, electrical work, roofing, general repair, and such other trades as the community's housing stock requires. Staff are compensated in Franks through the Service budget and credentialed by the Engineering College. Trade skill in service of community members is among the most valuable work the Service supports.

**§20. Scope of Support.** Maintenance and repair support covers work necessary to maintain housing adequacy — structural repairs, roof maintenance, plumbing and electrical work essential to habitability, and such other repairs as the Engineering College determines necessary to maintain minimum adequacy standards. The Service does not provide cosmetic improvements or upgrades — it maintains what members need to live safely.

**§21. Priority.** The Housing Service shall prioritize maintenance and repair support for members most at risk — elderly and disabled members whose homes are deteriorating and who cannot address repairs themselves, members with identified safety hazards, and members whose housing is approaching inadequacy. Prevention is more effective and less costly than crisis response.

**§22. Community Care Coordination.** Many members who need housing maintenance support are the same members who receive Community Care Service support. The Housing Service and Community Care Service shall coordinate actively to ensure that members receiving care in their homes have homes that are adequate for that care — safe, warm, structurally sound, and accessible.

---

## Article VI — Weatherization

**§23. Purpose.** Weatherization — insulation, air sealing, window improvement, and related measures — makes homes warmer in winter, cooler in summer, and less expensive to heat and cool. It directly improves member health, comfort, and financial security. The Housing Service shall maintain an active weatherization program as a joint function with the Energy Service.

**§24. Energy Service Coordination.** Weatherization sits at the boundary of housing and energy. The Housing Service and Energy Service shall coordinate their weatherization programs — sharing assessment data, coordinating staff deployment, and ensuring that weatherization and energy system improvements are planned together rather than in isolation. A well-insulated home with an efficient heating system is the goal; the two services work toward it jointly.

**§25. Priority Members.** Weatherization support shall be prioritized for members most vulnerable to temperature extremes — elderly members, disabled members, members with young children, and members identified by the Health Service as having health conditions exacerbated by cold or heat. Energy poverty is a health risk. Weatherization addresses it directly.

**§26. Standards.** Weatherization work shall meet standards established by the Engineering College ensuring that improvements are effective, durable, and do not create secondary problems — moisture, ventilation, or structural issues. Good weatherization requires technical competence. The Housing Service shall ensure its weatherization staff are trained and credentialed to deliver it.

## Article VII — Housing Assistance Coordination

**§27. Mandate.** Some members face housing insecurity not because their home needs repair but because they lack the financial resources to maintain adequate shelter. The Housing Service shall coordinate housing assistance for these members — connecting them with the Social Insurance Fund, Assembly appropriations, and such other resources as are available to ensure their shelter is secured.

**§28. Social Insurance Fund Coordination.** The Social Insurance Fund provides monthly allowances to elderly and disabled members. The Housing Service shall coordinate with the Fund to ensure that members whose allowances are insufficient to maintain adequate shelter receive additional housing assistance. Housing security and income security are connected — the Housing Service and Social Insurance Fund shall address that connection actively.

**§29. Assembly Appropriation.** The Housing Service shall identify the community's aggregate housing assistance needs and shall report them to the Assembly annually. The Assembly shall appropriate funds for housing assistance as part of its budgetary obligations to ensure the shelter guarantee is maintained. Housing assistance is not discretionary — it is the financial expression of the shelter guarantee.

**§30. Future Development.** The community's approach to housing tenure, community land trust, and housing development are matters for future foundational documents as the Society develops capacity and makes collective decisions about these questions. The Housing Service shall provide the Assembly with information relevant to these decisions — housing needs assessments, adequacy data, and analysis of housing insecurity patterns — to inform the community's deliberations.

---

## Article VIII — Inter-Society Housing Coordination

**§31. Network.** The Housing Service shall maintain relationships with the housing services of other Ben Franklin Societies. Members who relocate between societies, members displaced by disasters that cross society boundaries, and the sharing of technical knowledge about housing maintenance and weatherization all benefit from inter-society coordination.

**§32. Disaster Displacement.** Large-scale disasters may displace members beyond the capacity of a single society to house. The Housing Service shall coordinate with other societies on emergency housing for displaced members in mass displacement scenarios. The federation's mutual aid obligations extend to shelter as they do to food and health.

**§33. Knowledge Exchange.** Building maintenance knowledge, weatherization techniques, and housing assessment approaches are community knowledge that benefits from sharing across the federation. The Engineering College shall facilitate inter-society knowledge exchange on housing practice through its relationships with Engineering Colleges of other societies.

---

## Article IX — Transparency and Reporting

**§34. Annual Report.** The Housing Service shall publish an annual report covering: housing adequacy assessment across the community in aggregate; emergency shelter responses; maintenance and repair engagements; weatherization completions; housing assistance provided; identified housing needs gaps; and inter-society coordination activity. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§35. Shelter Guarantee Reporting.** The Housing Service shall report to the Assembly any instance in which the shelter guarantee was threatened or failed for any member — what happened, how the Service responded, and what systemic issues contributed. The shelter guarantee is the community's most fundamental housing commitment. The Assembly shall know when it is at risk.

**§36. Member Privacy.** Individual member housing situations are private. The Housing Service shall maintain strict privacy protections for all information about members' housing circumstances. Aggregate housing data is public. Individual member situations are not disclosed without member consent.
