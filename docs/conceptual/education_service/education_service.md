# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Education Service
*Article X of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Education is how a community reproduces itself across generations — not merely its people but its knowledge, its values, its capacity, and its understanding of itself. The Ben Franklin Society takes seriously its obligation to educate the children in its care and the adults in its membership. It maintains educational capacity not as a supplement to external systems but as a genuine community capacity that can stand alone when external systems fail. It treats literacy not as an assumption but as an obligation. And it teaches its own institutions to its own members, because a self-governing community that does not understand how it governs itself cannot long remain one.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Obligation.** The Education Service is charged with ensuring that children in the Society's community have access to quality education, that adults in the membership have access to practical learning and literacy support, and that the community as a whole maintains the knowledge and understanding necessary for genuine self-governance. Education is a right of membership and a community obligation.

**§2. Relationship to External Systems.** The Society recognizes that public schools and other external educational institutions may be functioning in the community and that many children may be enrolled in them. The Education Service does not seek to replace functioning external educational systems. It maintains genuine educational capacity that can serve the community fully in their absence. When external systems are functioning, the Education Service complements them. When they are not, it replaces them.

**§3. Tiered Capacity.** The Education Service operates on a tiered capacity model. K-12 equivalent education is the goal. The Education Service shall operate at whatever level of capacity it can currently sustain — in personnel, space, and resources — while actively working to expand that capacity. It shall document its current scope clearly so that members understand what is available.

**§4. Functions.** The Education Service carries out its mandate through the following functions: community school operation; public library maintenance; adult education including literacy; home education support; and civic education.

**§5. Oversight.** The Education Service is overseen by the Educators College — a body of peers comprising teachers, librarians, adult educators, curriculum developers, and others whose work is the transmission of knowledge. The Educators College sets educational standards, credentials practitioners, and maintains the intellectual life of the Society's educational institutions.

---

## Article II — The Community School

**§6. Establishment.** The Education Service shall maintain at least one community school providing education to children of the Society's membership. The school is a standing institution — maintained continuously, not convened only when external schools fail. Its existence in good times is what makes it reliable in bad ones.

**§7. Grade Range.** The community school's goal is K-12 equivalent coverage. The Education Service shall operate the widest grade range its current capacity allows and shall expand coverage as capacity grows. The current scope of the school shall be documented and communicated to members annually. The absence of full K-12 coverage is a gap the Education Service is obligated to close over time.

**§8. Curriculum.** The Educators College is responsible for developing and maintaining the school's curriculum. Curriculum shall include foundational academic subjects, practical skills relevant to community life, and civic education as specified in Article VI. The curriculum shall be reviewed regularly and shall reflect the community's actual needs and values.

**§9. Practical Education.** The community school shall incorporate practical education alongside academic subjects. Farming, food preservation, basic construction, health literacy, and such other practical skills as the Educators College determines relevant are not extracurricular — they are core to what a child in this community needs to know. The school shall treat practical and academic education as equally valued.

**§10. Cross-College Curriculum.** The Educators College shall draw on the knowledge of other Colleges in developing curriculum — the Agricultural College on farming and food, the Medical College on health literacy, the Caregivers College on community care. The community school is where the Society's collective knowledge meets the next generation.

**§11. Access.** The community school is open to all children of members in good standing. Education shall not be conditioned on anything beyond membership. The school shall make reasonable accommodations for children with different learning needs within its capacity.

---

## Article III — The Public Library

**§12. Establishment.** The Education Service shall maintain a public library accessible to all members of the Society. The library is a community knowledge commons — freely accessible, continuously developed, and maintained as a physical collection that does not depend on external systems to function.

**§13. Physical Collection.** The library shall maintain a physical collection of books and reference materials. Physical holdings that do not depend on electricity, connectivity, or external platforms are a resilience priority. The library shall function fully from its physical collection alone when digital access is unavailable.

**§14. Collection Development.** The library's collection shall be developed to serve the community's actual needs. The Educators College shall develop the collection in consultation with other Colleges — the Agricultural College on agricultural and soil science references, the Medical College on clinical and health references, and so on. A library that serves a mutual aid society in a rural community should reflect that community's knowledge needs alongside general literature, history, and reference.

**§15. Access.** The library is open to all members. Library access shall not be conditioned on good standing or any other criterion beyond membership. The library is among the most universally accessible institutions the Society maintains.

**§16. Local Knowledge.** The library shall actively collect and preserve local knowledge — local history, agricultural records, oral histories, community documents, and such other materials as document the life and knowledge of the community. Local knowledge is irreplaceable once lost. The library is its custodian.

---

## Article IV — Adult Education

**§17. Mandate.** The Education Service shall maintain an adult education program accessible to all members. Adult education serves members at every stage of life and at every level of prior education. It is not remedial — it is the community's commitment to lifelong learning and to ensuring that no member is left without the knowledge they need to participate fully in community life.

**§18. Literacy.** Literacy support is a core function of the adult education program. The Education Service shall not assume literacy as a prerequisite for participation. A significant number of adults in any rural community may need support with reading, writing, and numeracy. The adult education program shall provide this support without stigma, with dignity, and as a foundational service rather than an afterthought. Functional literacy is the prerequisite for almost everything else the community's knowledge systems offer.

**§19. Practical Skills.** Adult education shall have a strong practical component. Farming, food preservation, construction and repair, health and first aid, mechanics, fabrication, and such other practical skills as the community needs are legitimate and valuable subjects of adult education. Practical knowledge is community resilience. The Education Service shall treat it as seriously as academic subjects.

**§20. Cross-College Instruction.** Adult education shall draw on the expertise of all Colleges. College members are an extraordinary educational resource — experienced practitioners in their fields who can teach practical knowledge that no curriculum document fully captures. The Educators College shall develop formal relationships with other Colleges to integrate their expertise into adult education programs.

**§21. Flexible Delivery.** Adult education shall be delivered in formats accessible to working adults — evening programs, weekend intensives, one-on-one tutoring, peer learning groups, and such other formats as the Educators College determines effective. Adults have competing obligations. The Education Service meets them where they are.

**§22. No Prerequisites.** Adult education programs shall not require prior qualifications for participation. Any member may participate in any program at any time. The Education Service shall provide foundational support — literacy, numeracy — to any member who needs it in order to participate in other programs.

---

## Article V — Home Education Support

**§23. Recognition.** The Society recognizes home education as a legitimate and valuable form of education. Parents and guardians who choose to educate their children at home are making a significant commitment to their children's learning. The Education Service exists to serve the child's education — not to privilege any particular institutional form of it.

**§24. Support.** The Education Service shall actively support home-educating families with library access, curriculum resources, teaching materials, access to adult education programs for parents seeking to strengthen their own knowledge, and connection to the broader educational community of the Society. Home-educating families shall be treated as partners in the community's educational mission, not as exceptions to it.

**§25. Community Integration.** Home-educated children shall have access to community school resources, library facilities, practical education programs, and social and learning opportunities within the community school environment where their families choose this. Home education and community schooling are not mutually exclusive.

**§26. Educators College Support.** The Educators College shall maintain resources and expertise available to home-educating families — curriculum guidance, learning assessments offered voluntarily, and connection to experienced home educators within the community. The College's knowledge is a community resource available to all.

---

## Article VI — Civic Education

**§27. Mandate.** A self-governing community that does not understand how it governs itself cannot long remain one. The Education Service shall ensure that both children and adults understand the Society's institutions, principles, and governance systems. Civic education is not indoctrination — it is the transmission of the knowledge members need to participate meaningfully in the community's life.

**§28. School Curriculum.** The community school's curriculum shall include civic education covering the Society's foundational principles, its authority structure, the role of the Assembly and the Colleges, the Frank and the community's financial institutions, the Services and their mandates, and the rights and obligations of membership. Children who grow up in this community should understand it.

**§29. Adult Civic Education.** The adult education program shall include civic education accessible to all members — particularly new members who join the Society as adults and members whose prior educational experience did not include the Society's institutions. Understanding sortition, the Frank, the college system, and community oversight is not optional knowledge for a participating member.

**§30. Critical Engagement.** Civic education shall cultivate critical engagement with the Society's institutions, not mere compliance with them. Members who understand the Society's systems are better positioned to improve them. The Education Service shall teach members not only how the Society works but why it was designed as it was, what problems it was designed to solve, and how members can participate in its ongoing development.

**§31. Educators College Independence.** Civic education is delivered by the Educators College, not by the Assembly or any other governing body. This independence is intentional. The community's educational institutions teach how the Society works — they do not advocate for how the Assembly has chosen to exercise its authority in any particular instance. The distinction between civic education and institutional propaganda depends on this independence.

---

## Article VII — Knowledge as Community Infrastructure

**§32. Knowledge Infrastructure.** The Education Service and the Educators College are the primary custodians of the Society's knowledge infrastructure. Knowledge — agricultural, medical, civic, practical, cultural — is as much a community resource as land, tools, or currency. The Education Service shall treat its preservation, development, and transmission as a responsibility of the highest order.

**§33. Inter-College Knowledge.** The Education Service is the institution through which the knowledge of all Colleges flows into the community's broader membership. It is the connective tissue of the Society's intellectual life. The Educators College shall cultivate active relationships with every other College, treating their expertise as a community resource to be transmitted and not merely held.

**§34. Resilience.** The Education Service shall maintain its core functions — school, library, adult education — in a form resilient to external disruption. Physical teaching materials, physical library collections, and trained community educators who are community members rather than external hires are resilience priorities. The Education Service shall not be dependent on external systems for its ability to function.

---

## Article VIII — Transparency and Reporting

**§35. Annual Report.** The Education Service shall publish an annual report covering: school enrollment, grade range currently offered, and capacity gaps; library collection size, usage, and development activity; adult education program offerings and participation; literacy program enrollment and outcomes in aggregate; home education families supported; civic education activity; and inter-society educational coordination. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§36. Capacity Transparency.** The Education Service shall communicate clearly to members what educational services are currently available and what gaps exist. Members with children in the community need to know what the school can currently provide and what requires supplementation from other sources.

**§37. Student Privacy.** Individual student records — academic performance, attendance, learning needs — are private and shall not be disclosed without the consent of the student or, for minor students, their parent or guardian. Aggregate educational data is public. Individual student data is not.
