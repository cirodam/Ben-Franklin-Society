# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Manufacturing Service
*Article XXV of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Physical things break. Custom parts are needed. Equipment fails at inconvenient moments and the supply chains that normally provide replacement parts are neither guaranteed nor permanent. A community that can only consume what others manufacture — that cannot make a bracket, machine a shaft, weld a frame, or fabricate a fitting — is a community whose resilience depends entirely on systems it does not control. The Manufacturing Service exists because someone has to be able to make things. It provides the community with fabrication capacity across the full range of manufacturing disciplines — metal forming, machining, woodworking, electronics, general fabrication — available to the community's services, to member associations, and to individual members. It attains raw materials from wherever they can be found. It makes what needs making, repairs what can be repaired, and trains the community in the skills that keep physical things working. When the supply chain cannot deliver a part, the Manufacturing Service makes one.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Purpose.** The Manufacturing Service provides the Ben Franklin Society with fabrication and manufacturing capacity — the ability to make, repair, and modify physical things. It is community productive infrastructure of the most fundamental kind. Every other service depends in some degree on the ability to fabricate and maintain the physical objects their functions require. The Manufacturing Service is what makes the community capable of maintaining itself.

**§2. Dual Role.** The Manufacturing Service operates in two complementary modes. It manufactures and fabricates directly — making parts, components, and products as needed by the community's services, institutions, and members. And it supports — providing equipment access, technical expertise, and workspace to member associations and individuals undertaking their own fabrication work. Both modes serve the community's productive capacity. Neither excludes the other.

**§3. Scope.** The Manufacturing Service's scope encompasses the full range of fabrication disciplines — metal forming and welding, machining, woodworking, electronics fabrication and repair, general fabrication, and such other manufacturing disciplines as the community develops capacity for. No fabrication need that the community has is outside the Service's scope if the Service can develop the capacity to address it.

**§4. On-Demand and Production.** The Manufacturing Service operates on both an on-demand basis — fabricating custom parts and components as specific needs arise — and a production basis — manufacturing standard components in quantity when the community's needs justify it. The appropriate mode is determined by the need. The Service maintains the capability for both.

**§5. Resilience Function.** The Manufacturing Service's deepest value is resilience. A community that can fabricate its own parts is a community that can maintain its infrastructure, repair its equipment, and adapt its physical environment without depending on external supply chains for every component. This resilience function shall inform every aspect of how the Service is built and operated — prioritizing broad capability over narrow specialization, and community skill development over Service-only expertise.

**§6. Oversight.** The Manufacturing Service is overseen by the Engineering College, which sets technical standards for fabrication practice, safety, and quality, and credentials manufacturing practitioners. The Engineering College's broad technical knowledge base makes it the natural home for manufacturing oversight across the full range of fabrication disciplines.

## Article II — Fabrication Capabilities

**§7. Metal Fabrication.** Metal fabrication — cutting, forming, welding, and finishing metal components — is the Manufacturing Service's primary and most critical capability. Metal parts are required by virtually every other community service and by agricultural, household, and infrastructure maintenance throughout the community. The Service shall develop and maintain comprehensive metal fabrication capability including welding, cutting, forming, and finishing across common metals.

**§8. Machining.** Machining — the precise shaping of metal and other materials through cutting, turning, milling, and drilling — enables the production of custom parts to exact specifications. Machining capability transforms the Manufacturing Service from a general fabrication shop into a genuine precision manufacturing resource. The Service shall develop and maintain machining capability including lathe, mill, and drill press operations.

**§9. Woodworking.** Woodworking capability supports construction, furniture, agricultural implements, and such other community needs as require wood fabrication. The community's agricultural and housing functions in particular generate ongoing woodworking needs. The Service shall maintain woodworking capability appropriate to the community's needs.

**§10. Electronics.** Electronics fabrication and repair capability enables the community to maintain, repair, and in some cases manufacture the electronic components that its energy systems, communication systems, and equipment depend upon. As electronic devices become increasingly central to community infrastructure, the ability to repair rather than replace them becomes increasingly important.

**§11. General Fabrication.** Beyond specific disciplines the Manufacturing Service maintains general fabrication capability — the tools, workspace, and skills to address fabrication needs that don't fit neatly into a single category. General fabrication capability is the Service's most flexible and adaptive asset.

**§12. Capability Development.** The Manufacturing Service shall continuously develop its capabilities — acquiring new equipment, training practitioners in new disciplines, and expanding the range of what the community can make. The Engineering College shall guide capability development toward the areas of greatest community need.

## Article III — Raw Materials

**§13. Material Acquisition.** The Manufacturing Service shall attain raw materials from wherever they can be found — recovered metals from the Sanitation Service's material recovery function, purchased stock when available and affordable, salvaged materials from decommissioned equipment and structures, inter-society trade, and such other sources as the community can access. Material acquisition is a standing operational function, not a one-time procurement exercise.

**§14. Sanitation Service Coordination.** Recovered metals from the Sanitation Service's material recovery function are a primary raw material source for the Manufacturing Service. The two services shall maintain formal coordination protocols — the Sanitation Service communicating what metals are available and in what condition, the Manufacturing Service communicating what materials it needs. Waste metal flowing into productive fabrication is the community material loop operating as intended.

**§15. Material Inventory.** The Manufacturing Service shall maintain a current inventory of available raw materials — what is on hand, in what quantity, and in what condition. Material inventory is the foundation of production planning. The Service shall manage its materials actively — acquiring, storing, and deploying them to serve the community's fabrication needs.

**§16. Strategic Reserves.** The Manufacturing Service shall maintain strategic reserves of the most critical raw materials — particularly common metals and fasteners that are required across a wide range of fabrication work. Reserves buffer the Service against supply disruptions and ensure that urgent fabrication needs can be met even when procurement is difficult.

**§17. Inter-Society Trade.** Raw materials may be traded between Ben Franklin Societies through the federation's commerce infrastructure. A society with surplus metal stock and a society needing it can exchange through the clearinghouse. The Manufacturing Service shall participate in inter-society material trade as both a supplier and a recipient.

## Article IV — Service to the Community

**§18. Community Services Priority.** The Manufacturing Service shall prioritize fabrication work for the community's other services — the Energy Service needing custom mounting hardware, the Agricultural Service needing equipment repair, the Housing Service needing fabricated components, the Health Service needing instrument repair, the Fire and Emergency Service needing vehicle parts. Community service needs are the Manufacturing Service's primary production mandate.

**§19. Energy Service.** Solar panel mounting, battery enclosures, electrical conduit fabrication, and such other energy infrastructure components as require custom fabrication shall be a standing Manufacturing Service function. The Energy Service and Manufacturing Service shall coordinate on infrastructure development to ensure fabrication capacity is available when installation requires it.

**§20. Agricultural Service.** Farm equipment repair and fabrication is among the most practically important Manufacturing Service functions. When a tractor breaks at planting time or a grain mill needs a replacement part, the Manufacturing Service is the community's recourse. The Service shall develop and maintain agricultural equipment repair capability as a priority function.

**§21. Member Associations and Individuals.** Beyond community services the Manufacturing Service supports member associations and individual members in their own fabrication work. Members with farming operations, construction projects, equipment maintenance needs, or creative fabrication work may access the Service's equipment, workspace, and expertise. Community productive capacity distributed across members who can use it is more resilient than capacity concentrated only in the Service itself.

**§22. Access Protocols.** The Manufacturing Service shall establish protocols for member access to equipment and workspace — scheduling, safety requirements, supervision levels based on demonstrated competence, and such other arrangements as allow broad access while maintaining safe and orderly operations. *[Placeholder: member access protocols to be established in standing rules with Engineering College input.]*

**§23. Production for Community Needs.** Where the community's needs justify production runs — standard fasteners, common fittings, replacement parts for community equipment — the Manufacturing Service shall produce them. Community-manufactured standard components reduce dependence on external supply chains for the most commonly needed parts.

## Article V — Equipment and Workspace

**§24. Community Equipment.** The Manufacturing Service's equipment — lathes, mills, welders, drill presses, saws, and such other fabrication tools as the Service maintains — is community property held in trust for community productive use. Equipment shall be maintained in safe working order, made available for community and member use according to established protocols, and replaced or expanded as community needs and resources allow.

**§25. Equipment Maintenance.** Manufacturing equipment requires regular maintenance to operate accurately and safely. The Manufacturing Service shall maintain all equipment in calibrated, safe operating condition. Equipment that is poorly maintained produces poor work and creates safety hazards. The Engineering College shall establish maintenance standards for all manufacturing equipment.

**§26. Safety.** Manufacturing equipment is inherently hazardous. The Manufacturing Service shall maintain rigorous safety standards — personal protective equipment requirements, equipment guarding, ventilation for welding and finishing operations, fire prevention, and such other safety measures as the work requires. Safety training shall be required before any member accesses manufacturing equipment. The Engineering College shall establish and enforce safety standards.

**§27. Workspace.** The Manufacturing Service shall maintain workspace adequate to its fabrication functions — sufficient floor space for equipment and work in progress, appropriate lighting, ventilation, and utilities, and organized storage for materials and finished work. Workspace is as important as equipment — a cluttered, inadequate workspace produces unsafe and poor quality work.

**§28. Equipment Acquisition.** The Manufacturing Service shall continuously work to expand and improve its equipment inventory — acquiring additional capabilities as resources allow, rehabilitating salvaged equipment, and maintaining relationships with other societies and external sources for equipment acquisition. A manufacturing service with more capable equipment serves the community more completely.

## Article VI — Knowledge and Training

**§29. Knowledge as Infrastructure.** Manufacturing knowledge — how to weld, how to operate a lathe, how to read technical drawings, how to select materials, how to troubleshoot fabrication problems — is as important as the equipment itself. A shop full of equipment without people who know how to use it is not a manufacturing service. The Manufacturing Service shall treat knowledge development and transmission as a core function equal in importance to production.

**§30. Practitioner Credentialing.** The Engineering College shall credential Manufacturing Service practitioners — assessing competence in specific fabrication disciplines and authorizing practice at appropriate levels. Credentialing ensures that the people operating manufacturing equipment are genuinely competent to do so safely and productively.

**§31. Member Training.** The Manufacturing Service shall actively train community members in fabrication skills — welding, basic machining, woodworking, electronics repair, and such other skills as the community needs distributed broadly. Training programs shall be accessible to members regardless of prior experience. The goal is a community in which fabrication knowledge is held widely, not concentrated only in the Manufacturing Service's staff.

**§32. Education Service Coordination.** Basic fabrication skills — hand tools, safety, materials awareness, basic metalwork and woodwork — belong in both the community school's practical curriculum and the adult education program. The Manufacturing Service shall coordinate with the Education Service to incorporate fabrication skills into community education at both levels. Practical making skills are community resilience.

**§33. Technical Library.** The Manufacturing Service shall maintain a technical reference library — machinist handbooks, welding references, materials specifications, engineering standards, and such other technical documentation as practitioners need. Physical reference materials that do not depend on digital systems are a resilience priority. The technical library shall be accessible to credentialed practitioners and to members developing their fabrication knowledge.

**§34. Apprenticeship.** The Manufacturing Service shall maintain an apprenticeship model for developing skilled fabricators — pairing less experienced members with experienced practitioners for hands-on learning in specific disciplines. Apprenticeship transmits the tacit knowledge that formal training alone cannot capture: how a cut feels when it is right, how a weld sounds when it is good, how a material behaves under specific conditions. This knowledge is irreplaceable and must be actively transmitted.

## Article VII — Inter-Society Manufacturing Coordination

**§35. Network.** The Manufacturing Service shall maintain relationships with the manufacturing services of other Ben Franklin Societies. Specialized capabilities, surplus materials, technical knowledge, and production capacity are all resources that benefit from sharing across the federation network. A part that one society cannot make may be within another's capability.

**§36. Capability Specialization.** Over time, different societies may develop particular manufacturing specializations — one with strong machining capability, another with electronics expertise, another with foundry capacity. The federation network allows these specializations to serve all member societies rather than only the one that developed them. The Manufacturing Service shall participate in inter-society capability sharing and shall identify opportunities for productive specialization.

**§37. Knowledge Exchange.** Manufacturing knowledge, techniques, and problem solutions shall be actively shared across the federation. The Engineering College shall facilitate inter-society technical knowledge exchange through its relationships with Engineering Colleges of other societies. A fabrication solution developed in one community is knowledge available to all.

## Article VIII — Transparency and Reporting

**§38. Annual Report.** The Manufacturing Service shall publish an annual report covering: fabrication work completed by discipline and volume; community services supported and work performed; member and association access utilization; raw materials acquired by source; equipment inventory and condition; training programs and participation; apprenticeship activity; inter-society coordination; and any significant developments in manufacturing capacity. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§39. Capability Transparency.** The Manufacturing Service shall communicate clearly to the community what fabrication capabilities it currently has, what it cannot yet do, and what it is working toward. Community services and members planning projects need honest information about what the Manufacturing Service can deliver and when.

**§40. Material Inventory.** Current raw material inventory shall be reported to the Assembly quarterly and made available to community services and members planning fabrication work. Services and members who know what materials are available can plan their fabrication work accordingly.
