# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Transit Service
*Article XXIII of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Personal mobility is one of the most underappreciated resilience vulnerabilities in a rural community. Franklin County is geographically large and historically car-dependent — a condition that functions adequately when fuel is available and affordable, and fails seriously when it is not. The Transit Service exists because the community recognizes that its members' ability to move within it — to reach the clinic, the marketplace, community events, and each other — cannot be left entirely to individual vehicle ownership. As energy becomes less accessible this need grows more acute, not less. The Transit Service is designed accordingly: built from available resources, operated with honest scheduling, and planned explicitly for a future in which lower-energy modes of transport become necessary. It moves people. Everything else the community does depends in part on that.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Purpose.** The Transit Service moves members within the community — connecting them to the community's services, institutions, events, and each other. Personal mobility is a condition of full participation in community life. Members who cannot move within the community cannot access its healthcare, its food service, its education, its commerce, or its governance. The Transit Service makes mobility a community function rather than a purely individual one.

**§2. People Only.** The Transit Service moves people. The movement of goods, packages, and mail is the mandate of the Courier Service. The two services shall coordinate operations to maximize efficiency — shared routes, shared vehicles where practical — while maintaining their distinct mandates.

**§3. Resource Realism.** Routes, schedules, and modes of transport shall reflect the community's actual resources — available vehicles, fuel, personnel, and infrastructure. The Transit Service shall make honest commitments it can keep rather than aspirational promises it cannot. Published schedules shall be reliable. Routes that cannot be sustained shall not be promised.

**§4. Energy Transition Mandate.** The Transit Service is founded with explicit awareness that liquid fuel availability may decline significantly over the community's lifetime. The Service shall plan continuously for lower-energy modes of transit — electric vehicles as electrification capacity grows, bicycles, and such other modes as become practical — so that the community's transit capacity evolves with its energy reality rather than collapsing when fuel becomes scarce or unaffordable.

**§5. Intra-Society Scope.** The Transit Service operates within the community's geographic area. Inter-society transit is a future development to be addressed as federation capacity grows. The Service shall design its routes and infrastructure to be extensible to inter-society connections without requiring fundamental redesign.

**§6. Oversight.** The Transit Service is overseen by the Engineering College, which sets standards for vehicles, routes, safety, and the technical dimensions of transit operations.

## Article II — Routes and Scheduling

**§7. Route Design.** The Engineering College shall design transit routes based on where members live, where community services and institutions are located, and what the available transport resources can sustainably cover. Routes shall connect members to the Health Service, Food Service, Commerce Service marketplace, community school, community bank, and such other institutions as members regularly need to reach.

**§8. Coverage Goal.** The Transit Service shall work toward reaching every member within a reasonable distance of a transit route or stop. Full geographic coverage is a goal to be achieved as resources allow — the Service shall document its current coverage and its gaps honestly, and shall expand coverage as capacity grows.

**§9. Scheduling.** Schedules shall be established based on available resources and route demand. Schedules shall be published, reliable, and reviewed regularly. A transit service whose schedules are unreliable is not a transit service — it is an unreliable promise. The Service shall run what it commits to running. *[Placeholder: route frequencies and schedules to be established in standing rules based on available capacity.]*

**§10. Stop Design.** Transit stops shall be located at community institutions, collection points shared with the Courier Service, and such other locations as serve the community's travel patterns. Stops shall be safe, clearly marked, and accessible to members with mobility limitations where possible.

**§11. Route Coordination with Courier Service.** The Transit Service and Courier Service shall coordinate routes wherever possible. A vehicle running a transit route that also carries mail and packages serves the community more efficiently than two separate vehicles running the same road. Coordination shall be managed jointly by the Engineering College and the College of the Post.

## Article III — Modes of Transport

**§12. Mode Diversity.** The Transit Service shall develop and maintain multiple modes of transport rather than depending on a single mode. Mode diversity is resilience — when one mode is unavailable others can continue. The Service shall not be caught without transit capacity because a single mode has failed.

**§13. Motorized Vehicles.** Motorized vehicles — buses, vans, cars — are the primary transit mode for a geographically large rural community where distances exceed practical cycling or walking range. The Transit Service shall maintain motorized vehicle capacity appropriate to its route commitments. Vehicles are community property maintained for community benefit.

**§14. Electric Vehicles.** As the Energy Service develops community electrical generation and storage capacity, the Transit Service shall transition motorized routes to electric vehicles wherever practical. Electric vehicles powered by community generation are the most energy-sovereign transit option available. The Transit Service and Energy Service shall coordinate on electrification planning.

**§15. Bicycles and Non-Motorized Transport.** The Transit Service shall develop non-motorized transit capacity — bicycles, cargo bikes, and such other human-powered transport as is practical for the community's terrain and distances. Non-motorized transport requires no fuel, generates no emissions, and continues functioning when motorized options cannot. It is the most resilient transit mode available and shall be developed as a genuine community capacity, not a fallback afterthought.

**§16. Contingency Modes.** The Transit Service shall maintain contingency plans for periods when primary transit modes are unavailable — fuel shortages, vehicle failures, severe weather. Contingency planning shall include alternative modes, reduced but maintained route coverage, and coordination with the Courier Service and Energy Service on resource sharing during disruptions.

**§17. Vehicle Maintenance.** All community transit vehicles shall be maintained in safe operating condition. Vehicle maintenance is not optional — a transit vehicle that fails carries passengers and cannot be allowed to be unsafe. The Engineering College shall establish vehicle maintenance standards and the Transit Service shall maintain compliance with them continuously.

## Article IV — Passenger Safety and Dignity

**§18. Safety.** The Transit Service shall operate all transport with passenger safety as the paramount consideration. Vehicles shall be maintained to safety standards. Drivers shall be credentialed and competent. Routes and schedules shall not require unsafe driving to maintain. The Engineering College shall establish safety standards for all Transit Service operations.

**§19. Dignity.** Transit is a community service used by members across the full range of the community's diversity. All members shall be treated with dignity on community transit — with the same respect regardless of their reason for traveling, their appearance, their ability, or any other personal characteristic. The Transit Service shall maintain a culture of dignity in all passenger interactions.

**§20. Accessibility.** The Transit Service shall work toward making transit accessible to members with mobility limitations — accessible vehicles, accessible stops, and such other accommodations as the community's resources allow. Accessibility shall be a planning priority, not an afterthought. *[Placeholder: accessibility standards to be established by Engineering College with input from Medical and Caregivers Colleges.]*

**§21. Driver Credentialing.** All Transit Service drivers shall be credentialed by the Engineering College in safe vehicle operation, passenger handling, route knowledge, and emergency response. Drivers carry community members. Their competence and character matter. Credentialing is how the community ensures both.

## Article V — Energy and Fuel Management

**§22. Energy Coordination.** The Transit Service is one of the community's largest consumers of liquid fuel. The Service shall coordinate actively with the Energy Service on fuel supply, strategic reserves, and long-term energy transition planning. Transit fuel needs shall be factored into the Energy Service's reserve planning. The two services shall plan together rather than competing for scarce resources.

**§23. Conservation.** The Transit Service shall operate with fuel conservation as a standing operational principle — efficient routing, appropriate vehicle sizing for passenger loads, maintenance practices that maximize fuel efficiency, and route coordination with the Courier Service to reduce total vehicle miles traveled. Conservation is not a crisis response — it is standard practice.

**§24. Fuel Shortage Protocols.** The Transit Service shall maintain protocols for periods of fuel shortage — reduced route frequency, priority routes serving the most essential community connections, and transition to lower-energy modes where available. Fuel shortage protocols shall be planned and published before they are needed so that members understand what to expect when they are activated.

**§25. Long-Term Transition.** The Transit Service shall maintain a long-term energy transition plan — a current assessment of the community's transit energy situation, a trajectory toward lower-energy modes, and milestones for electrification and non-motorized capacity development. The transition plan shall be reviewed annually and presented to the Assembly. The community shall not be surprised by energy transition — it shall plan for it continuously.

## Article VI — Service Integration

**§26. Health Service.** Access to healthcare requires the ability to reach it. The Transit Service shall coordinate with the Health Service on route design to ensure that members can reach the primary clinic and other health facilities. Clinic appointment times and transit schedules shall be coordinated to the greatest degree possible.

**§27. Food Service.** The Transit Service shall coordinate with the Food Service on routes that connect members to the community kitchen and pantry. Members who cannot drive to food services need transit to reach them. Community kitchen meal times and transit schedules shall be coordinated.

**§28. Community Care Service.** Many members receiving Community Care Service support have mobility limitations that make transit particularly important. The Transit Service and Community Care Service shall coordinate to ensure that members with care needs have transit access to the services they require.

**§29. Enrichment Service.** Community events are only accessible to members who can reach them. The Enrichment Service and Transit Service shall coordinate on transit provision for major community events — festivals, performances, and gatherings — so that no member is excluded from community cultural life by lack of transportation.

**§30. Courier Service.** Route coordination between the Transit and Courier Services shall maximize efficiency and minimize fuel consumption. Where a transit vehicle and a courier route serve the same road on the same schedule, combined operation shall be the default approach subject to practical operational constraints.

## Article VII — Transparency and Reporting

**§31. Annual Report.** The Transit Service shall publish an annual report covering: routes in operation and coverage gaps; passenger volumes by route; vehicle fleet condition and maintenance status; fuel consumption and efficiency metrics; energy transition progress; accessibility status; Courier Service coordination; service integration outcomes; and any significant developments in transit capacity or operations. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§32. Schedule Publication.** Current routes, schedules, and stop locations shall be published and made available to all members. Members shall always know what transit is available and when. Schedule changes shall be communicated in advance with adequate notice.

**§33. Coverage Transparency.** The Transit Service shall communicate honestly about its coverage gaps — areas of the community not yet served by transit routes. Members in uncovered areas deserve to know the Service's plans and timeline for extending coverage to them.

**§34. Energy Transition Reporting.** The long-term energy transition plan shall be presented to the Assembly annually alongside the annual report. The community shall be continuously informed of the Transit Service's energy situation and its trajectory toward lower-energy operation.
