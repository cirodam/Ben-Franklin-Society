# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Community Care Service
*Article XIII of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Some members of the community cannot provide for their own daily care. The elderly who can no longer bathe or feed themselves. The disabled whose needs exceed what they can meet alone. The chronically ill who require continuous supervision. These are not peripheral cases — they are the condition that every human being may eventually reach, and the community's response to them is a measure of what the community actually values. The Community Care Service exists to ensure that members who cannot care for themselves are cared for with dignity, in their own homes wherever possible, by people who know them and are part of their community. It treats caregiving as the skilled, essential, and compensable labor it has always been.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Obligation.** The Community Care Service is charged with ensuring that members who cannot provide for their own daily care — due to age, disability, or chronic illness — receive the care they need with dignity. This obligation is absolute. The Service shall not consider its mandate fulfilled by the mere availability of care — it must ensure that care is genuinely accessible, genuinely dignified, and genuinely suited to the member's needs.

**§2. Home First.** The Community Care Service operates on a home-first principle. Care in a member's own home, among their own relationships and their own community, is preferable to institutional care in almost all circumstances. The Service shall design its programs, staff its caregivers, and allocate its resources with home-based care as the default and shared or facility-based care as the exception.

**§3. Caregiving as Valued Labor.** Caregiving is skilled, essential work. The Community Care Service shall treat it as such — compensating caregivers fairly in Franks through the Service budget, training them well, supporting them actively, and recognizing their contribution as among the most important work the community does. The systematic devaluation of caregiving labor is a failure of the world the Society is trying to move beyond.

**§4. Functions.** The Community Care Service carries out its mandate through four functions: home care coordination and delivery; shared household operation; care facility operation; and caregiver support.

**§5. Oversight.** The Community Care Service is overseen by the Caregivers College. The Caregivers College sets standards of care, credentials caregiving practitioners, and maintains the knowledge base on which quality community care depends. The Health Service provides medical support to the Service's care facility and coordinates on members whose care needs span both services.

---

## Article II — Home Care

**§6. Purpose.** Home care enables members who need assistance with daily living — feeding, bathing, dressing, mobility, medication management, household tasks, and companionship — to remain in their own homes rather than moving to shared or facility care. The Community Care Service shall identify members needing home care, match them with appropriate caregivers, and ensure that care is delivered consistently and well.

**§7. Caregiver Matching.** The Service shall match members needing home care with caregivers suited to their needs, preferences, and circumstances. Good matching — of personality, proximity, skills, and schedule — is as important as caregiver competence. A care relationship that works well for both the member and the caregiver is a sustainable one. The Service shall invest in matching as a core function.

**§8. Family Caregivers.** Family members who provide home care to a dependent member are eligible for compensation through the Service budget on the same basis as unrelated caregivers. The community recognizes that family caregiving is real work with real costs — in time, in income foregone, and in personal toll. Compensating family caregivers is both just and practical: it sustains care relationships that would otherwise break under financial pressure.

**§9. Caregiver Training.** All caregivers providing home care through the Service shall receive training appropriate to the needs of the members they serve. Training shall be provided by the Caregivers College and shall cover practical care skills, safe patient handling, communication, and recognition of medical deterioration requiring Health Service involvement. Training is not a one-time event — it is continuous.

**§10. Care Plans.** Each member receiving home care shall have a care plan developed in collaboration with the member, their caregivers, and such Health Service input as is appropriate. Care plans shall document the member's needs, preferences, and goals. They shall be reviewed regularly and updated as circumstances change. The member's voice in their own care plan is essential.

**§11. Coordination with Health Service.** Many members receiving home care also have health needs. The Community Care Service shall maintain close coordination with the Health Service to ensure that medical and caregiving functions complement rather than duplicate each other, and that members whose condition changes receive appropriate Health Service attention promptly.

## Article III — Shared Households

**§12. Purpose.** Some members who need care do not have family caregivers and cannot be safely supported alone in their own home. For these members the Community Care Service shall operate shared households — community-managed group homes where several members live together and receive care from dedicated staff. Shared households are not institutions. They are homes, operated by the community, for members who need the community's support to have one.

**§13. Community Character.** Shared households shall be operated with the character of genuine homes rather than care facilities. They shall be embedded in the community — located within it, visited by community members, connected to community life. Residents of shared households remain full members of the Society with all attendant rights. They are not set apart.

**§14. Staffing.** Shared households shall be staffed by caregivers employed through the Service, compensated in Franks, and trained by the Caregivers College. Staffing levels shall be sufficient to provide genuine care — not minimum supervision. *[Placeholder: staffing ratios to be established in standing rules with Caregivers College input.]*

**§15. Resident Rights.** Residents of shared households retain all membership rights. They retain the right to participate in governance, to access all community services, to have visitors, to make decisions about their own daily life within their capacity, and to leave the shared household if they choose. Residence in a shared household is not a reduction in membership status.

**§16. Scale.** Shared households shall be maintained at a human scale — small enough that residents know their caregivers and each other, and that the household functions as a genuine community rather than a small institution. *[Placeholder: maximum household size to be established in standing rules.]*

## Article IV — The Care Facility

**§17. Purpose.** Some members require a level of continuous supervision and care that cannot be provided in a home or shared household setting — members with advanced chronic illness, severe cognitive impairment, or complex care needs that require near-continuous trained attendance. For these members the Community Care Service shall operate a care facility providing the intensive care they need within the community rather than in distant institutional settings.

**§18. Community Connection.** The care facility shall be operated as a community institution, not as a closed facility. Residents remain full members of the Society. The facility shall be open to visitors, connected to community life, and operated with the understanding that its residents are community members temporarily requiring intensive support — not people who have left the community.

**§19. Chronic Illness Focus.** The care facility is oriented toward chronic illness management and severe dependency care rather than acute medical treatment. Acute medical needs are the domain of the Health Service. The care facility and the Health Service shall maintain clear protocols for when a resident's needs require Health Service intervention and how that transition is managed.

**§20. Health Service Coordination.** The Health Service shall provide regular medical support to the care facility — clinical visits, medication management, and response to acute health events. The care facility is operated by the Community Care Service and overseen by the Caregivers College; the Health Service is a partner in its medical functions, not its operator.

**§21. Staffing.** The care facility shall be staffed at levels sufficient to provide genuine continuous care. Staff shall be trained by the Caregivers College in the specific competencies required for intensive care — including dementia care, palliative care, and complex physical care needs. *[Placeholder: staffing ratios and competency standards to be established in standing rules with Caregivers College and Health Service input.]*

**§22. Resident Rights.** Residents of the care facility retain all membership rights to the full extent of their capacity. The care facility shall make every reasonable accommodation to support resident participation in community life — voting, receiving visitors, accessing community services, and maintaining relationships. Cognitive or physical impairment does not diminish membership.

**§23. Palliative Care.** The care facility shall provide palliative and end-of-life care to members whose condition is terminal. Dying with dignity, in one's community, with adequate care and human presence, is something the Society can provide and shall provide. The Caregivers College shall maintain expertise in palliative care as a core competency.

## Article V — Caregiver Support

**§24. Mandate.** Caregiving is demanding work — physically, emotionally, and cognitively. Caregivers who are unsupported, isolated, or exhausted cannot provide good care. The Community Care Service shall actively support the caregivers who make its functions possible. Caregiver support is not a supplementary program — it is a prerequisite for sustainable care.

**§25. Respite Care.** Every caregiver — whether a family member or a service-employed caregiver — is entitled to regular respite from caregiving responsibilities. The Community Care Service shall provide or arrange substitute care enabling caregivers to take breaks, attend to their own needs, and sustain their capacity over time. Respite is not a luxury for caregivers — it is a clinical necessity for the quality of care they provide.

**§26. Training and Development.** The Community Care Service shall provide ongoing training and professional development to all caregivers — employed and family. Training shall cover practical care skills, communication, emotional resilience, recognition of health deterioration, and such other competencies as the Caregivers College determines relevant. Training makes caregivers more effective and more confident. Confidence reduces burnout.

**§27. Peer Support.** The Community Care Service shall facilitate peer support networks among caregivers — opportunities to connect with others doing similar work, share experiences, and support one another. Caregiving can be isolating. Peer connection reduces isolation and builds the shared knowledge that makes individual caregivers more effective.

**§28. Mental Health Support.** Caregivers experience elevated rates of depression, anxiety, and grief. The Community Care Service shall ensure that caregivers have access to mental health support through the Health Service's mental health function. Caregiver mental health is not a personal matter separate from the Service's operations — it is directly relevant to the quality of care members receive.

**§29. Recognition.** The community shall actively recognize caregiving as essential and valued work. The Community Care Service shall support community recognition of caregivers — not as a formality but as a genuine expression of the community's understanding of what caregiving contributes. Recognition is part of what makes caregiving sustainable as a vocation.

---

## Article VI — Identification and Outreach

**§30. Proactive Identification.** The Community Care Service shall not wait for members in need to present themselves. It shall actively identify members who may need care — through coordination with the Health Service, the Social Insurance Fund, and the broader community — and shall reach out to ensure they receive the support they need. Members who need care most are often those least able to ask for it.

**§31. Coordination.** The Community Care Service shall maintain active coordination with the Health Service, the Social Insurance Fund, the Food Service's delivery function, and the Energy Service's member support function to ensure that members with care needs are known to all relevant services and that no member falls through the gap between them.

**§32. Privacy.** Information about a member's care needs is among the most sensitive personal information the Community Care Service holds. The Service shall maintain strict privacy standards for all care records and shall not disclose a member's care status or needs without their consent except as required for the delivery of care or an immediate safety necessity.

---

## Article VII — Transparency and Reporting

**§33. Annual Report.** The Community Care Service shall publish an annual report covering: number of members receiving home care; number of shared household residents; number of care facility residents; caregiver numbers and compensation totals; caregiver support program activity; training participation; coordination activity with other services; and any significant developments in care capacity or policy. All data shall be aggregate — individual member information is private. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§34. Capacity Transparency.** The Community Care Service shall communicate clearly to members what care services are currently available, what the wait for services is if applicable, and what gaps exist in current capacity. Members planning for their own future care needs or those of family members need honest information about what the community can provide.

**§35. Member Privacy.** Individual member care records — care plans, health information, living arrangements — are strictly private. The Community Care Service shall maintain the strictest privacy protections for all individual care information. Aggregate service data is public. Individual care data is never disclosed without explicit member consent.
