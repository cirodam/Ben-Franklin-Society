# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Food Service
*Article VII of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Food is the most fundamental requirement of human life and therefore the most fundamental obligation of any community that takes mutual aid seriously. The Ben Franklin Society commits to ensuring that every member has access to nutritionally adequate food. This is not an aspiration. It is a guarantee. The Food Service exists to make that guarantee real — through planning, processing, provision, and emergency response. It is likely the largest and most operationally complex Service in the Society, and it is so because the obligation it fulfills is the largest and most fundamental.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Obligation.** The Food Service is charged with ensuring that every member of the Ben Franklin Society has access to nutritionally adequate food. This obligation is absolute. The Food Service shall not consider its mandate fulfilled by the mere availability of food — it must ensure that food is genuinely accessible to every member, including those who are elderly, disabled, geographically isolated, or otherwise unable to access food through normal means.

**§2. Functions.** The Food Service carries out its mandate through eight distinct but interrelated functions: nutritional planning and projection; food reserve management; community kitchen operation; community pantry operation; food delivery; emergency food response; food processing; and inter-society food coordination. Each function is essential. Together they constitute a comprehensive community food system.

**§3. Nutritional Standard.** The Food Service's obligation is not merely to provide calories but to provide nutritionally adequate food. The Food Service shall plan, procure, process, and provision food with reference to nutritional adequacy as a baseline standard. *[Placeholder: nutritional standards to be established in standing rules with input from the Medical College.]*

**§4. Scale.** The Food Service is expected to be the largest and most operationally complex Service in the Society. The Assembly shall resource it accordingly. Inadequate resourcing of the Food Service is a failure of the community's most fundamental obligation.

**§5. Oversight.** The Food Service is overseen by the Agricultural College. Given the breadth of the Food Service's mandate, the Agricultural College shall maintain close working relationships with the Medical College on nutritional standards and the Caregivers College on food accessibility for vulnerable members.

---

## Article II — Nutritional Planning and Projection

**§6. Planning Function.** The Food Service shall maintain an ongoing nutritional planning function that projects the community's food requirements across seasons, years, and demographic shifts. Food security is a planning problem, not a market problem. The Food Service treats it as such.

**§7. Data Sources.** Nutritional projections shall draw on membership data from the Central Bank, production data from the Agricultural Service, reserve inventory from the Food Service's own reserve function, and such other data as the Food Service determines relevant. The Food Service shall establish formal data-sharing protocols with the Central Bank and Agricultural Service.

**§8. Coordination with Agricultural Service.** The Food Service and the Agricultural Service shall maintain a close and formal planning relationship. The Food Service communicates projected requirements; the Agricultural Service plans production accordingly. This coordination is among the most important institutional relationships in the Society. Both Services are overseen by the Agricultural College, which shall facilitate this coordination.

**§9. Reporting.** The Food Service shall report its nutritional projections to the Assembly annually and shall update the Assembly when projections change materially. Projections shall inform Assembly decisions on Food Service budgets, reserve requirements, and agricultural priorities.

---

## Article III — The Food Reserve

**§10. Obligation.** The Society is obligated to maintain a strategic reserve of food sufficient to sustain the membership through periods of crop failure, supply disruption, or acute crisis. Maintaining the food reserve is not optional and not subject to annual budgetary discretion. It is a standing community obligation that the Assembly must fund and the Food Service must fulfill.

**§11. Reserve Standard.** The food reserve shall be maintained at a level sufficient to sustain the membership for a minimum period established by the Assembly, measured in both total caloric capacity and nutritional adequacy across key food categories. *[Placeholder: minimum reserve period and commodity categories to be established by Assembly resolution with input from the Medical College and Agricultural College.]*

**§12. Rotation.** The Food Service shall maintain active rotation protocols for all reserve stocks, ensuring that stored food remains within its useful life and that older stocks are consumed and replaced continuously. A reserve that has spoiled is not a reserve. Rotation is a core operational responsibility of the Food Service.

**§13. Reserve Access — Normal Operations.** In normal operations the food reserve is not a distribution point. Food reaches members through the community kitchen, community pantry, and delivery functions. The reserve feeds these functions as needed but is not directly accessed by members.

**§14. Reserve Access — Emergency.** In declared food emergencies the reserve may be opened for direct distribution to members. The Food Service's emergency function has authority to draw on the reserve in immediate crisis situations and must seek Assembly ratification within a period established in the standing rules. The Assembly may also directly authorize reserve access for declared community or inter-society emergencies.

**§15. Inventory and Reporting.** The Food Service shall maintain a continuous and accurate inventory of the food reserve. Reserve levels shall be reported to the Assembly quarterly and to the membership annually. Any material decline in reserve levels below the established standard shall be reported to the Assembly immediately.

---

## Article IV — The Community Kitchen

**§16. Establishment.** The Food Service shall operate at least one community kitchen providing prepared meals to members of the Society. The community kitchen is not a charity service — it is a community institution open to any member who needs it.

**§17. Free Meals.** Meals at the community kitchen are provided free of charge to members up to a defined monthly limit. The monthly meal limit is established by the Assembly and reviewed annually. *[Placeholder: monthly meal limit per member to be established by Assembly resolution.]*

**§18. Request System.** Members shall request meals in advance through a process established in the standing rules. The request system enables the Food Service to plan production accurately, reduce waste, maintain nutritional standards, and serve members well. Requesting a meal is not an application for assistance — it is how a member uses a community service. The system shall be as simple and low-friction as possible.

**§19. Nutritional Adequacy.** Meals provided by the community kitchen shall meet the nutritional standards established for the Food Service. The kitchen does not merely provide food — it provides nourishment. Menu planning shall reflect nutritional adequacy as a primary criterion.

**§20. Dignity.** The community kitchen shall operate with full regard for the dignity of those it serves. There is no means test, no public declaration of need, and no distinction between members who use the kitchen frequently and those who use it rarely. It is a community service, used by community members.

---

## Article V — The Community Pantry

**§21. Purpose.** The community pantry provides members with food they can prepare themselves. Not every member wishes to eat communally, and not every member's needs are best met by prepared meals. The pantry preserves the dignity of self-sufficiency and ensures that members who prefer to prepare their own food, or whose circumstances require it, are equally served by the Food Service's mandate.

**§22. Monthly Entitlement.** Each member is entitled to a defined Frank value of food from the community pantry each month if needed. This entitlement is a right of membership. The Frank value of the monthly entitlement is established by the Assembly and reviewed annually. *[Placeholder: monthly pantry entitlement value to be established by Assembly resolution.]*

**§23. Access.** The community pantry shall be accessible to members through the Food Service's physical locations and through the delivery function for members who cannot attend in person. Pantry access shall be as simple and low-friction as kitchen access.

**§24. Stock.** The pantry shall be stocked with foods appropriate for home preparation, reflecting nutritional adequacy standards and the community's actual food culture. Stock shall be rotated actively and shall draw where possible on the Agricultural Service's production and the Food Service's own processing capacity.

---

## Article VI — Food Delivery

**§25. Obligation.** The Food Service's mandate to ensure that every member has access to nutritionally adequate food cannot be fulfilled by fixed locations alone. Members who are elderly, disabled, ill, geographically isolated, or otherwise unable to access the kitchen or pantry in person must be reached by the Food Service. Food delivery is a necessary expression of the core obligation.

**§26. Development.** The Food Service shall develop food delivery capacity as a priority. The Assembly shall provide resources for delivery infrastructure — vehicles, routes, staffing — as part of its obligation to fund the Food Service's core mandate. *[Placeholder: delivery standards, routes, and scheduling to be established in standing rules.]*

**§27. Coordination with Caregivers College.** Identifying members who need food delivery and ensuring they receive it is a caregiving function as much as a food function. The Food Service shall coordinate with the Caregivers College to ensure that members who need delivery are known to the Food Service and served.

---

## Article VII — Emergency Food Response

**§28. Purpose.** The Food Service maintains an emergency food response function capable of acting rapidly when a member or group of members faces an acute food crisis outside the normal planning cycle. Individual hardship, household disaster, community emergency, and inter-society crisis all require a response that cannot wait for normal processes.

**§29. Individual Response.** When an individual member or household faces an acute food emergency, the Food Service's emergency function may respond immediately without prior authorization. Emergency food provision to individuals is within the Food Service's standing authority. The emergency function shall notify the overseeing College of individual responses.

**§30. Community Emergency.** When a larger food emergency affecting multiple members or the community as a whole occurs, the Food Service's emergency function may draw on the food reserve and shall immediately notify the Assembly. The Assembly shall ratify or direct the emergency response within a period established in the standing rules. The Food Service shall not wait for Assembly authorization before acting in a genuine emergency — it acts and then seeks ratification.

**§31. Inter-Society Response.** When a member society of the broader Ben Franklin Society federation faces a food crisis, the emergency food function is the primary responder. The Food Service shall maintain protocols and relationships with the corresponding functions of other societies to enable rapid inter-society food assistance. Inter-society emergency response requires Assembly authorization except in immediate life-threatening circumstances.

**§32. Readiness.** The emergency food function shall be maintained in a state of readiness at all times. Staff designated to emergency response shall be available, protocols shall be current, and the reserve shall be accessible. Emergency readiness is not an optional enhancement — it is part of the Food Service's core mandate.

---

## Article VIII — Food Processing

**§33. Mandate.** The Food Service is responsible for processing the community's food from raw agricultural production into forms suitable for storage, distribution, and preparation. Owning this processing capacity is a foundational expression of food sovereignty — a community that cannot process its own food is dependent on external systems for a critical step in its food chain.

**§34. Grain Milling.** The Food Service shall maintain grain milling capacity sufficient to process the community's grain production. Milling is an active operational function, not a backup capability. The Food Service shall operate its mills in coordination with the Agricultural Service's production cycles.

**§35. Additional Processing.** Beyond milling the Food Service shall develop such additional processing capacity as the community's agricultural production and food needs require — preservation, fermentation, drying, canning, and such other methods as the Agricultural College determines appropriate. Processing capacity shall grow with the community's productive capacity.

**§36. Knowledge.** Food processing knowledge — milling, preservation, fermentation — is community knowledge. The Food Service shall actively transmit processing skills within the community so that this knowledge is held broadly and does not reside only in the Food Service's staff.

---

## Article IX — Inter-Society Food Coordination

**§37. Network Mandate.** No community's food security is fully independent. Crop failures are local. Surpluses are local. Disasters are local. The Food Service participates in a network of Ben Franklin Society food functions that collectively manage food security across the broader federation. Moving food where it is needed — from surplus communities to deficit communities — is a core function of this network.

**§38. Surplus Sharing.** When the Food Service holds food stocks beyond its reserve requirements and projected needs, it shall make surplus available to other societies in the federation network. Surplus sharing is an expression of mutual aid at the inter-community scale.

**§39. Deficit Support.** When the Food Service identifies a deficit relative to its obligations, it shall communicate this to the federation network and seek assistance. Accepting food assistance from other societies is not a failure — it is the federation working as intended.

**§40. Protocols.** The Food Service shall maintain active relationships and coordination protocols with the food functions of other Ben Franklin Societies. These relationships shall be cultivated in ordinary times so that they function reliably in extraordinary ones.

---

## Article X — Transparency and Reporting

**§41. Annual Report.** The Food Service shall publish an annual report covering: nutritional projections and actual performance against them; food reserve levels and rotation status; kitchen utilization and meal counts by month; pantry utilization and entitlement data; delivery reach and members served; emergency responses conducted; processing volumes; and inter-society coordination activity. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§42. Reserve Reporting.** Reserve levels shall be reported to the Assembly quarterly. Any material decline below the established minimum shall be reported immediately.

**§43. Member Privacy.** Aggregate data on kitchen, pantry, and delivery utilization is public. Individual member usage data is private and shall not be disclosed without member consent.
