# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Mother's College
*Article XVI of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

For most of human history the knowledge surrounding pregnancy, birth, and the care of new life was held by women and transmitted among women — grandmother to mother, neighbor to neighbor, midwife to apprentice. It was practical, hard-won, rooted in experience, and irreplaceable. The Ben Franklin Society establishes the Mother's College in recognition that this knowledge tradition deserves institutional standing — that the experience of pregnancy and motherhood in all its forms, including loss, is a legitimate basis for peer authority and community oversight. The Mother's College does not make a political statement. It makes a human one: that the experience of bringing life into the world, or attempting to, belongs at the table.

---

## Article I — Nature and Purpose

**§1. Nature.** The Mother's College is a College of the Ben Franklin Society in the original sense — a body of peers bound by shared experience, mutual obligation, and common purpose. Its members are women who have experienced pregnancy in any form. Its purpose is to hold, develop, and transmit the knowledge specific to pregnancy, birth, postpartum care, infant care, and reproductive health, and to exercise oversight of these functions within the Health Service.

**§2. The Statement It Makes.** The Mother's College exists because the community believes that lived experience of pregnancy and motherhood constitutes a legitimate basis for peer authority. This is a deliberate choice. The systematic displacement of women's experiential knowledge around childbearing by medical authority and institutional control has not served women or communities well. The Mother's College recovers the peer knowledge tradition that predates that displacement and gives it institutional standing in the Society's governance.

**§3. Scope.** The Mother's College concerns itself with the full domain of pregnancy, birth, postpartum recovery, infant care, reproductive health, and the physical and emotional experience of motherhood in all its forms. This scope is defined by lived experience rather than clinical category. The College holds knowledge that clinical training alone does not produce.

**§4. Independence.** The Mother's College is independent of the Medical College and the Caregivers College. It works in close partnership with both but answers to neither. Its authority within its domain derives from its members' experience and peer recognition, not from delegation by another College. Where the domains overlap, the Colleges shall coordinate in good faith.

---

## Article II — Membership

**§5. Eligibility.** Membership in the Mother's College is open to any woman member of the Society who has experienced pregnancy. Experience of pregnancy is the sole eligibility criterion. Whether the pregnancy resulted in a live birth, a miscarriage, a stillbirth, or any other outcome does not affect eligibility. The credential is the experience itself — all of it, in all its forms.

**§6. Admission.** Eligible members join the Mother's College by affirmation of their experience and their commitment to the College's purpose. Admission is not gatekept by existing members beyond verification of eligibility. The College's membership is as broad as the community's experience of pregnancy.

**§7. Practitioners.** Midwives, doulas, lactation consultants, and other maternal health practitioners who work within the Health Service need not be members of the Mother's College. Membership in the College is based on lived experience, not professional role. Practitioners who are eligible for membership are welcome to join. Those who are not eligible credential through the Medical or Caregivers Colleges for their clinical functions and work within the standards the Mother's College sets for the maternal domain.

**§8. Mutual Obligation.** Members of the Mother's College accept mutual obligation to one another and to the community's pregnant and postpartum women. This obligation is the heart of what the College is — not a credential or a governance position but a commitment to be present for other women navigating the experience the College's members share.

**§9. Privacy.** A member's pregnancy history is among the most personal information the Society holds. Membership in the Mother's College shall not be publicly disclosed without the member's consent. The College shall maintain its membership records with strict privacy protections.

---

## Article III — Knowledge and Practice

**§10. Knowledge Mandate.** The Mother's College is the primary custodian of the community's knowledge around pregnancy, birth, postpartum recovery, infant care, and reproductive health as lived experience. This knowledge is distinct from clinical knowledge — it is the knowledge that women carry about what pregnancy actually feels like, what birth requires, what postpartum recovery demands, and what new mothers need that medical training alone does not fully capture.

**§11. Knowledge Transmission.** The Mother's College shall actively transmit maternal knowledge within the community — to pregnant members, to new mothers, to partners and families, and to practitioners working in the maternal domain. The College's knowledge is not held privately. It is a community resource made available through the College's active institutional life.

**§12. Peer Support.** The Mother's College shall organize and sustain peer support for pregnant and postpartum members. The knowledge that a woman who has been through a difficult birth can offer another woman facing the same experience, or that a mother who has navigated infant feeding challenges can offer a struggling new mother, is not replicated by clinical support. The College shall make this peer support systematically available rather than leaving it to chance.

**§13. Loss.** The Mother's College holds specific knowledge and responsibility around pregnancy loss — miscarriage, stillbirth, and infant death. These experiences are among the most painful any member may face and among the most poorly supported by conventional systems. The College shall develop and maintain peer support, community acknowledgment, and practical care for members who experience pregnancy loss. Grief of this kind deserves community recognition and the College provides it.

**§14. Institutional Life.** The Mother's College has ongoing institutional life beyond its oversight function. Members gather, share experience, develop knowledge, support one another, and maintain the living tradition of maternal knowledge that the College exists to hold. This institutional life is not incidental to the College's purpose — it is the College's purpose, expressed continuously rather than only when oversight is required.

---

## Article IV — Oversight of Maternal Functions

**§15. Oversight Role.** The Mother's College oversees the maternal and reproductive health functions of the Health Service. This oversight includes setting standards of care consistent with women's dignity and autonomy, reviewing the quality and character of maternal care provided, advising on the development of maternal health capacity, and ensuring that the Health Service's maternal functions reflect the values and knowledge the College holds.

**§16. Standards.** The Mother's College shall establish standards for maternal and reproductive healthcare within the Health Service. Standards shall center women's dignity, autonomy, and health as primary values. They shall reflect both clinical best practice — developed in coordination with the Medical College — and the experiential knowledge the Mother's College holds that clinical training alone does not produce.

**§17. Staffing Input.** The Mother's College shall have input into the nomination of staff serving in the Health Service's maternal and reproductive functions. Practitioners working in this domain should reflect the values and approach the College holds. The College does not control staffing — that remains with the Health Service and the Assembly — but its input shall be sought and given genuine weight.

**§18. Member Advocacy.** The Mother's College serves as an advocate for pregnant and postpartum members within the Health Service. When a member's experience of maternal care raises concerns, the College provides a peer body with standing to raise those concerns formally. No pregnant or postpartum member faces the Health Service alone if they choose to have the College's support.

**§19. Coordination with Medical and Caregivers Colleges.** The Mother's College shall maintain active working relationships with the Medical College and the Caregivers College. Maternal health spans all three domains. The three Colleges shall establish clear protocols for their coordination within the Health Service, with mutual respect for each College's authority in its domain and commitment to the wellbeing of the women and children they collectively serve.

---

## Article V — Women's Dignity and Autonomy

**§20. Foundational Values.** The Mother's College holds women's dignity and autonomy as foundational values in all matters within its domain. A woman's body is her own. Her experience of pregnancy, birth, and reproductive health belongs to her. The College's oversight of maternal functions in the Health Service is exercised with these values as the non-negotiable foundation from which all standards, practices, and decisions derive.

**§21. Informed Decision-Making.** The Mother's College shall ensure that pregnant and postpartum members have access to full, honest, and accessible information about their care options, their health status, and their rights as patients. Informed decision-making is not a clinical courtesy — it is a foundational expression of women's autonomy that the College shall protect actively.

**§22. Non-Judgment.** The Mother's College and the Health Service's maternal functions shall approach every member's pregnancy experience without judgment. The full range of pregnancy outcomes, decisions, and circumstances that members bring to the maternal domain shall be met with care, dignity, and support. The College's membership — drawn from the full range of pregnancy experience — reflects this non-judgmental orientation as a lived reality, not merely a stated principle.

**§23. Community Governance.** Questions about the specific services the Health Service provides within the maternal domain are community governance questions determined by the Assembly. The Mother's College advises the Assembly from its domain expertise and its foundational values. The Assembly makes determinations that reflect the community's collective decisions. The College's role is to ensure those decisions are made with full understanding of their implications for women's health, dignity, and autonomy.

---

## Article VI — Reporting and Accountability

**§24. Annual Report.** The Mother's College shall publish an annual report covering: College membership in aggregate; peer support activity; knowledge transmission programs and participation; oversight activity within the Health Service's maternal functions; maternal care standards in effect; coordination with other Colleges; and any significant developments in maternal health capacity or policy. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§25. Accountability to Community.** The Mother's College is accountable to the community through the Assembly. Its standards and oversight activities are subject to Assembly review. The community's ultimate authority over all Colleges and their functions is preserved.

**§26. Member Privacy.** The Mother's College shall maintain strict privacy protections for all individual member information. A member's pregnancy history, health status, and engagement with maternal care services are among the most personal information the community holds. This information shall never be disclosed without explicit member consent.
