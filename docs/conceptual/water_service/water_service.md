# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Water Service
*Article XX of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

Water is the most immediately essential of all human needs. A person can survive days without food but only hours without water. Clean drinking water is not a commodity — it is a condition of life, and ensuring that every member has access to it is among the community's most fundamental obligations. The Water Service works with whatever water sources exist within the community's area — wells, springs, municipal connections, surface water, collected rainwater — assessing, protecting, treating, testing, and distributing water to ensure that no member goes without. It builds water security from what the community has, not from what it wishes it had.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Core Obligation.** The Water Service is charged with ensuring that every member of the Ben Franklin Society has access to clean, safe drinking water. This obligation is absolute and immediate — more urgent even than food, given the speed at which water deprivation becomes life-threatening. The Water Service shall not rest while any member lacks access to safe drinking water.

**§2. Working with What Exists.** The Water Service works with the water sources available within the community's area — municipal connections, private wells, springs, streams, ponds, and collected rainwater. It does not assume dedicated infrastructure. It assesses what exists, supports and improves it, and builds community water security from the existing patchwork of sources. Resilience comes from knowing and maintaining multiple sources, not from dependence on a single system.

**§3. Water as Commons.** The Water Service approaches water as a community commons — a shared resource whose protection and management is a collective obligation. No member's access to water shall be contingent on their ability to pay for it as a market commodity. Water security is community security.

**§4. Functions.** The Water Service carries out its mandate through the following functions: water source assessment and mapping; water quality testing and monitoring; treatment support; member well support; emergency water reserve; rainwater harvesting support; drought protocols; and inter-society coordination.

**§5. Oversight.** The Water Service is overseen by the Engineering College, which sets infrastructure and treatment standards and credentials water system practitioners. The Medical College advises on water safety standards, testing requirements, and the health implications of water quality. Safe water is both an engineering and a medical question — the two Colleges shall coordinate their oversight to ensure both dimensions are adequately addressed.

---

## Article II — Water Source Assessment and Mapping

**§6. Mapping Mandate.** The Water Service shall maintain a comprehensive and current map of all water sources within the community's area — municipal connections, private and community wells, springs, streams, ponds, cisterns, and such other sources as exist. You cannot manage what you have not mapped. The water source map is the foundation of the Water Service's entire function.

**§7. Source Assessment.** The Water Service shall assess each mapped water source — its yield, its current quality, its vulnerability to contamination, its seasonal reliability, and its treatment requirements. Assessment is the basis for all Water Service planning. Sources that are adequate, marginal, contaminated, or at risk shall be known and categorized.

**§8. Redundancy Planning.** The Water Service shall plan for the failure of any single water source. A community dependent on one well, one municipal connection, or one spring is vulnerable to a single point of failure. The Service shall identify backup sources for all primary sources and shall ensure that members can access safe water if their primary source fails.

**§9. Contamination Risk.** The Water Service shall assess contamination risks to all community water sources — agricultural runoff, septic systems, industrial activity, surface contamination, and such other risks as the community's specific geography creates. Contamination prevention is more effective than contamination response. The Service shall work with the Agricultural Service on agricultural water management practices that protect water sources.

## Article III — Water Quality Testing and Monitoring

**§10. Testing Mandate.** The Water Service shall conduct regular water quality testing of all community water sources. Clean water cannot be assumed — it must be verified. Testing is a continuous obligation, not a one-time certification. The frequency and scope of testing shall be established by the Medical College based on public health standards and the specific risk profile of each source.

**§11. Testing Standards.** Water quality testing shall cover the contaminants most relevant to the community's specific sources and context — bacterial contamination, nitrates, heavy metals, agricultural chemicals, and such other parameters as the Medical College determines necessary for safe drinking water. *[Placeholder: testing parameters and frequency to be established by Medical College with Engineering College input.]*

**§12. Transparency.** Water quality testing results shall be made available to all members promptly and in a form they can understand. Members have the right to know the quality of the water they drink. Results shall not be withheld, delayed, or minimized. When testing reveals a problem, members shall be informed immediately and clearly.

**§13. Member Well Testing.** The Water Service shall offer water quality testing to members with private wells. Many rural members depend on private wells whose quality is unknown or untested. Testing offered as a service — not a regulation — helps members understand and protect their own water supply. Results belong to the member; the Service provides the testing and interpretation.

**§14. Contamination Response.** When testing reveals contamination in any community water source the Water Service shall respond immediately — notifying affected members, providing alternative water access, investigating the source of contamination, and coordinating with the Health Service on any health impacts. Contamination response is a public health emergency. The Service shall treat it as such.

## Article IV — Treatment Support

**§15. Treatment Mandate.** Where water sources require treatment to be safe for drinking the Water Service shall provide or support treatment. Treatment requirements vary by source — some wells produce safe water without treatment, others require filtration, disinfection, or removal of specific contaminants. The Service shall ensure that treatment is matched to the specific needs of each source.

**§16. Community Treatment Capacity.** The Water Service shall develop and maintain community-scale treatment capacity — filtration systems, disinfection equipment, and such other treatment infrastructure as the community's water sources require. Treatment infrastructure is community property maintained for community benefit.

**§17. Member Treatment Support.** The Water Service shall support members in treating their private water sources where treatment is needed. Advice, technical assistance, and access to treatment equipment shall be provided as a service to members whose wells or other sources require improvement to meet safety standards.

**§18. Treatment Standards.** Treatment standards shall be established by the Medical College and implemented by the Engineering College. Standards shall reflect genuine public health requirements — not minimal compliance thresholds but actual safe drinking water. The community's water shall be as safe as the community can make it.

---

## Article V — Member Well Support

**§19. Mandate.** Many members of a rural community depend on private wells as their primary water source. The Water Service shall actively support members in maintaining, improving, and protecting their wells — providing technical advice, assessment, testing, and maintenance support similar to the Housing Service's approach to private homes. A member's well is their water security; the community has an interest in its health.

**§20. Well Assessment.** The Water Service shall offer well assessments to members — evaluating well condition, yield, casing integrity, and contamination risk. Assessments are offered as a service, not conducted as inspections. Members who know the condition of their wells can make informed decisions about maintenance, treatment, and backup water planning.

**§21. Well Maintenance Support.** The Water Service shall provide maintenance support for member wells — cleaning, casing repair, pump maintenance, and such other work as keeps wells producing safe water. Priority shall be given to elderly and disabled members who cannot maintain their wells independently and to members whose wells are the sole water source for their household.

**§22. Failed Wells.** When a member's well fails entirely the Water Service shall treat this as a water access emergency. The member shall be connected with alternative water sources immediately while the well failure is assessed and addressed. No member shall go without water because their well has failed.

## Article VI — The Emergency Water Reserve

**§23. Obligation.** The Water Service shall maintain a strategic reserve of safe drinking water sufficient to sustain the community through periods when primary water sources are unavailable or contaminated. Water emergency is more immediately life-threatening than food emergency. The reserve is a non-negotiable community obligation.

**§24. Reserve Standards.** The emergency water reserve shall be maintained at levels sufficient to provide minimum safe drinking water to all members for a period established by the Assembly. Reserve water shall be stored in appropriate containers, rotated regularly to maintain quality, and tested before rotation. *[Placeholder: minimum reserve period and storage standards to be established by Assembly with Medical and Engineering College input.]*

**§25. Distribution Capacity.** Maintaining a reserve is necessary but not sufficient — the Water Service must also be able to distribute reserve water to members when primary sources fail. The Service shall maintain distribution capacity — containers, transport, distribution points — that can reach all members during a water emergency.

**§26. Priority Distribution.** In water emergencies distribution shall prioritize members most vulnerable to water deprivation — infants, elderly members, members with medical conditions that make dehydration particularly dangerous, and members without personal water storage. The Health Service shall advise on priority distribution criteria.

## Article VII — Rainwater Harvesting

**§27. Encouragement.** Rainwater harvesting — collecting and storing rainfall for use — is a distributed water resilience measure that the Water Service shall actively encourage and support. Every household with rainwater collection capacity is a household with greater water independence. Distributed rainwater harvesting reduces the community's dependence on centralized sources and provides a resilient supplementary supply.

**§28. Member Support.** The Water Service shall support members in establishing rainwater harvesting systems — providing technical advice on system design, storage, and treatment; connecting members with appropriate equipment; and ensuring that harvested water is used safely. Rainwater requires treatment before drinking. The Service shall ensure that members understand and implement appropriate treatment.

**§29. Community Harvesting.** The Water Service shall develop community-scale rainwater harvesting capacity — collection systems on community buildings and infrastructure that contribute to the community water supply and emergency reserve. Community harvesting supplements individual systems and provides water security for members without suitable collection surfaces of their own.

## Article VIII — Drought Protocols

**§30. Drought Planning.** Drought is a real and recurring risk in the community's climate. The Water Service shall maintain current drought response plans — graduated conservation measures, priority allocation protocols, and emergency response procedures — ready to activate before water sources reach critical levels. Planning for drought before it arrives is essential; improvising during it is dangerous.

**§31. Drought Declaration.** When water source levels or quality decline to a threshold established in the standing rules, the Water Service shall notify the Assembly. The Assembly may declare a drought condition activating drought protocols. Drought declaration authority rests with the Assembly — not the Water Service — because drought protocols affect all members and require community-level decision-making.

**§32. Conservation Measures.** Drought protocols shall include graduated conservation measures appropriate to the severity of the drought — public education on water conservation, reduction in non-essential water use, priority allocation to drinking and essential sanitation needs, and such other measures as the Water Service and Assembly determine necessary. Conservation shall be applied equitably across all members.

**§33. Agricultural Coordination.** Drought affects agricultural water needs as severely as household needs. The Water Service shall coordinate with the Agricultural Service on drought response — balancing drinking water priority with agricultural water needs, developing shared conservation approaches, and ensuring that drought management serves the community's full water needs including food production.

**§34. Sunset.** Drought protocols activated by Assembly declaration shall lapse when the Assembly determines that water source conditions have recovered sufficiently. The Assembly shall review drought declaration status regularly and shall lift it as soon as conditions warrant.

---

## Article IX — Inter-Society Water Coordination

**§35. Network.** The Water Service shall maintain relationships with the water services of other Ben Franklin Societies. Water knowledge, treatment expertise, and emergency water supply are all resources that benefit from sharing across the federation network.

**§36. Emergency Water Sharing.** When a member society faces a water emergency — contamination, source failure, or severe drought — the Water Service shall coordinate with other societies to provide emergency water supply assistance. Water emergency is among the most acute community crises. Federation mutual aid is essential.

**§37. Knowledge Exchange.** Water source management, treatment approaches, conservation techniques, and drought response strategies are community knowledge that benefits from sharing. The Engineering College shall facilitate knowledge exchange on water practice through its relationships with Engineering Colleges of other societies.

---

## Article X — Transparency and Reporting

**§38. Annual Report.** The Water Service shall publish an annual report covering: water source inventory and condition; testing results in aggregate by source; treatment systems in operation; member well assessments and support provided; emergency reserve levels; rainwater harvesting capacity; drought planning status; and inter-society coordination activity. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§39. Testing Results Public.** Water quality testing results for all community water sources shall be published promptly and made available to all members. Members have an absolute right to know the quality of the water they drink. Testing results shall never be withheld, delayed, or obscured for any reason.

**§40. Reserve Reporting.** Emergency water reserve levels shall be reported to the Assembly quarterly. Any material decline below established minimums shall be reported immediately.

**§41. Member Privacy.** Individual member well testing results and household water situations are private. The Water Service shall maintain privacy protections for individual member water information while ensuring that aggregate community water quality data is fully public.
