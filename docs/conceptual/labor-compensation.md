# Labor and Compensation

## Core Principle

BFS rejects hourly wages as the primary compensation model. Hourly wages have become a tool for exploitation, creating instability and failing to recognize the full cost workers bear when they give their time to work.

## The Problem with Hourly Wages

### Exploitation Mechanisms
1. **Schedule manipulation** - Employers keep workers at 29 hours to avoid benefit thresholds
2. **Unstable income** - Workers cannot budget or plan when hours fluctuate weekly
3. **Hidden unpaid labor** - Commute time, preparation, childcare arrangements not compensated
4. **Part-time trap** - Workers cobble together multiple part-time jobs, none with benefits
5. **Surveillance and micromanagement** - Constant time tracking, productivity monitoring
6. **Race to the bottom** - Competition drives toward minimum wage with minimum hours

### Philosophical Issues
- **Commodifies human time** - Treats people as machines to be rented by the hour
- **Ignores life costs** - If you work today, you can't do other things today
- **Creates perverse incentives** - Incentivizes slow work, punishes efficiency
- **Undermines dignity** - "Your time is only worth $X per hour"

## BFS Two-Tier Compensation Model

### Tier 1: Salaried Positions

**Definition:** Fixed monthly salary for ongoing roles with stable responsibilities.

**Who:**
- Service Officers (top leadership)
- Section Chiefs (middle management)
- Unit Leaders (team leads)
- Core service staff (permanent positions)
- Committee members
- College instructors

**Compensation:**
- Fixed monthly salary based on role level and experience
- Paid regardless of exact hours worked each week
- Typical expectation: ~40 hours/week, but flexible
- No tracking of individual hours

**Benefits:**
- **Security** - Predictable income allows budgeting, planning life
- **Flexibility** - Slow week? Take time off. Busy week? Get it done.
- **Trust** - No micromanagement, focus on outcomes
- **Professional autonomy** - Adults managing their own time

**Example Pay Scales:**
- Level 1 (Service Officer): 3,000 Franks/month
- Level 2 (Section Chief): 2,200 Franks/month
- Level 3 (Unit Leader): 1,800 Franks/month
- Level 4 (Core Staff): 1,500 Franks/month

*Note: Actual scales set by each service based on responsibilities and cost of living.*

### Tier 2: Daily Positions

**Definition:** Fixed daily rate for temporary, seasonal, or project-based work.

**Who:**
- Seasonal agricultural workers (harvest crews)
- Construction project workers
- Event staff
- Surge capacity for services
- Short-term volunteers
- Trial periods for new service members

**Compensation:**
- Fixed rate per day worked (e.g., 100 Franks/day)
- Paid for the day, not the hours
- If you show up and work is done, you get paid

**Benefits:**
- **Dignity** - Full day commitment recognized
- **Simplicity** - No time tracking, no disputes
- **Fair** - Recognizes commute, preparation, full day availability
- **Flexibility** - Pick up days when you need income
- **Entry point** - Way to try service work before committing

**How It Works:**
1. Service posts need: "Agricultural Service needs 10 crop pickers Wednesday"
2. Members sign up (first-come-first-served or lottery if oversubscribed)
3. Show up Wednesday, work until task is done
4. Get paid 100 Franks (or whatever the day rate is)

**Important principles:**
- Work finishes at 2pm? Still get full day pay.
- Work runs till 7pm? Still one day pay (prevents dawdling).
- Bad weather ruins plans? If you showed up, you get paid.
- Can't complete task? Pay based on good faith effort.

## Why This Works

### Recognizes Full Cost of Work

When someone works for you, they bear costs beyond their time on-site:
- **Commute time and expense**
- **Childcare arrangements** (can't watch kids while working)
- **Opportunity cost** (can't take other work that day)
- **Physical preparation** (getting ready, packing meals)
- **Mental commitment** (planning around work day)
- **Recovery** (work is exhausting)

Hourly wages ignore all of this. Daily rates acknowledge: **you gave us your day, we compensate fairly.**

### Eliminates Exploitation Vectors

**Hourly model enables:**
- "You worked 7.5 hours so we don't pay lunch break"
- "We need you on-call but only pay when we call you in"
- "You're scheduled 15-32 hours per week" (impossible to budget)

**BFS model prevents:**
- ✅ No schedule games - salaried roles are stable, daily roles are opt-in
- ✅ No hour-shaving - you worked the day, you get paid
- ✅ No poverty wages - minimum daily rate ensures dignity
- ✅ No surveillance - trust and outcomes, not time tracking

### Supports Multiple Work Styles

**Full-time commitment:** Salaried position provides security

**Flexible income:** Pick up daily positions when you need money or want to try new work

**Semi-retirement:** Former officer can still pick up occasional daily work

**Skill building:** Try different services via daily positions before committing to salaried role

**Economic downturns:** Services can expand daily positions (easier than creating new salaried roles)

## Implementation Details

### Position Classification

Every service role must be classified as either:
- **Salaried** - Ongoing position with monthly pay
- **Daily** - As-needed position with daily rate

Services cannot create "hourly" positions. If work is ongoing, it should be salaried. If it's temporary, it should be daily.

### Salary Determination

Each service sets salary scales for its positions based on:
1. **Role level** (Officer > Chief > Leader > Staff)
2. **Responsibility** (budget size, people managed, criticality)
3. **Experience required** (years of service needed for eligibility)
4. **Cost of living** (adjusted by General Assembly as needed)

Salary scales must be:
- Published publicly
- Approved by service association
- Ratified by General Assembly
- Limited in differential (max 3:1 between highest and lowest in service)

### Daily Rate Determination

Daily rates set by each service for different types of work:
- **Standard labor** (general work): 100 Franks/day
- **Skilled labor** (requires training): 150 Franks/day
- **Specialized** (certifications, rare skills): 200 Franks/day

Services may adjust rates but must:
- Apply consistently (same rate for same work)
- Justify higher rates to General Assembly
- Maintain reasonableness (daily rate ≈ 1/20th of monthly salaried equivalent)

### Transition Between Tiers

**Daily → Salaried:**
- Work daily positions to gain experience
- Apply for salaried opening
- Selected via sortition from qualified pool
- Begin receiving monthly salary

**Salaried → Daily:**
- Term ends, don't want to continue
- Can still pick up daily work in same or different service
- Maintains income flexibility
- Keeps skills sharp

## Connection to Other BFS Principles

### Employment Guarantee
All members can always find work (daily or salaried) in services. No involuntary unemployment.

### Demurrage Currency
Stable income from salaries or regular daily work encourages spending rather than hoarding. Workers earn and spend, keeping money circulating.

### Sortition
Salaried leadership positions filled by sortition from qualified pools, not applications and interviews. Reduces careerism and politicking.

### Social Provisioning
Stable employment allows members to access housing, food, healthcare without financial anxiety. Employment is foundation for other services.

### Democratic Workplaces
Flat salary differentials (3:1 max within services) and rotation between roles reduce hierarchy and promote equality.

## Historical Precedents

### Medieval Guild Wages
Craftsmen paid by the job or day, not hour. A pair of shoes, a day's masonry—fair rate for completed work.

### Agricultural Labor Traditions
Harvest crews historically paid by day or by season, recognizing the totality of the commitment and the variation in daily hours.

### Salaried Professional Class
Doctors, lawyers, teachers traditionally salaried—recognition that their work doesn't fit neat hourly boxes and requires autonomy.

### New Deal Programs
CCC and WPA workers paid monthly wages for public works, not hourly. Provided stability during Great Depression.

## Addressing Common Questions

### "What if someone on salary doesn't work hard?"
Focus on outcomes, not hours. If work isn't getting done, that's a performance issue regardless of hours. Services have accountability mechanisms (recall, term limits, peer review).

### "What if someone takes advantage of daily work?"
Daily positions are posted and filled by sign-up. If someone signs up and doesn't show, they don't get paid and may be barred from future daily work. Social pressure and reputation matter in small communities.

### "Don't some jobs genuinely vary in hours?"
Yes—that's what daily positions are for. Seasonal harvest? Daily rates. Big construction project? Daily rates. Don't create permanent hourly positions to handle variable work.

### "What about very short tasks?"
Minimum unit is a day. If a service needs someone for 2 hours, they either:
- Include it in someone's salaried responsibilities
- Find 6 hours of other useful work to make a full day
- Decide the task isn't worth a day's pay and handle differently

This prevents the erosion back toward hourly thinking.

## Conclusion

The elimination of hourly wages is not merely a policy preference—it's a recognition that **human beings are not machines to be rented by the hour**. When someone gives their day to work, they bear costs far beyond the hours spent on task. BFS compensation acknowledges this reality through stable salaries for ongoing roles and fair daily rates for flexible work.

This model:
- Provides security without rigidity
- Offers flexibility without exploitation  
- Recognizes dignity while maintaining accountability
- Builds community rather than commodifying labor

Work in BFS is a contribution to the common good, compensated fairly and structured to support human flourishing—not a desperate scramble to accumulate enough hours to survive.
