# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Constabulary Service
*Article XV of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

The burden of safety falls on every member of the Ben Franklin Society. This is not a metaphor. When a member is in danger, the community responds — not a specialized class acting on the community's behalf, but the community itself, coordinated and led by those who have accepted the charge of safety as their particular obligation. The Constabulary Service exists to organize and lead that community response. It does not replace the community's responsibility for its own safety. It expresses it. Constables are neighbors who have accepted a burden, not officers who have been granted authority. The distinction is everything.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Community Safety as Shared Burden.** The safety of the community is the responsibility of every member. No member may decline this responsibility entirely. The Constabulary Service coordinates and leads the community's collective safety response — it does not monopolize it. When the community needs to respond to a safety situation, every member may be called upon.

**§2. Mandate.** The Constabulary Service's mandate is narrow and defined. It responds to immediate threats to the physical safety of members. It de-escalates dangerous situations. It calls for community backup when needed. It refers matters to the College of Conciliation or the Assembly as appropriate. It does not investigate. It does not prosecute. It does not adjudicate. It does not detain except as an immediate safety necessity with mandatory immediate referral. It does not govern.

**§3. De-escalation Orientation.** The Constabulary Service is de-escalation oriented at all times. The goal of any constabulary response is the resolution of a safety situation with the least possible harm to all involved. Force is a last resort, not a tool of convenience. Every constable shall be trained in de-escalation as a primary competency and shall exhaust de-escalation options before considering any use of force.

**§4. Character Over Authority.** The Constabulary Service explicitly does not select for people attracted to authority over others. The role of constable is a burden accepted in service to the community, not a position of power over it. The College of the Charge shall actively select against authoritarian personality in credentialing and shall maintain a culture of service and accountability rather than authority and enforcement.

**§5. Oversight.** The Constabulary Service is overseen by the College of the Charge. The College sets standards of practice, credentials constables, and maintains the knowledge base on which effective community safety depends. The College of Conciliation participates in character assessment for constable credentialing. The Permanent Review Committee exercises independent binding oversight of the Constabulary Service as established in Article VII.

---

## Article II — The College of the Charge

**§6. Nature.** The College of the Charge is a select body of peers who have accepted the community's physical safety as their particular burden and obligation. Members of the College have demonstrated the qualities of character, judgment, calm, and genuine commitment to community service that the constabulary role requires. Membership in the College is burdensome, not prestigious — it is a weight accepted, not a privilege granted.

**§7. Selection.** The College of the Charge is among the most selective Colleges in the Society. Credentialing requires demonstrated competence in safety response and de-escalation, assessed by the College of the Charge, and demonstrated character, assessed jointly with the College of Conciliation. The character assessment is as important as the capability assessment. People drawn to this role for the wrong reasons — for authority over others, for the exercise of power, for the status of the position — shall not be admitted regardless of their capability.

**§8. Joint Character Assessment.** The College of Conciliation shall participate in character assessment for all College of the Charge candidates. Conciliators are skilled at reading people — at distinguishing genuine care for others from performed care, genuine service orientation from desire for authority. No candidate shall be admitted to the College of the Charge without the College of Conciliation's endorsement of their character.

**§9. Ongoing Assessment.** Character is not fixed. People change. The College of the Charge shall maintain ongoing assessment of its members — not as surveillance but as the natural attention that a community of practice pays to its own. A member whose conduct raises concerns shall be reviewed. The College shall not protect members from accountability out of institutional loyalty.

**§10. Institutional Culture.** The College of the Charge shall actively maintain a culture of service, humility, and accountability. It shall resist the institutional drift toward insularity, self-protection, and the normalization of conduct that would be unacceptable to the community. The history of safety institutions is largely a history of this drift. The College shall treat its resistance as a continuous active obligation, not a founding principle that can be assumed to persist without effort.

## Article III — Constable Selection and Terms

**§11. Composition.** The Constabulary Service comprises a trained core drawn from the College of the Charge and a rotating civic complement drawn from the general eligible membership. The trained core provides continuity, expertise, and leadership. The rotating complement expresses the community's shared burden of safety and prevents the trained core from becoming a self-contained professional class.

**§12. Trained Core.** Members of the College of the Charge serving in the Constabulary constitute the trained core. They are available continuously, maintain current training, and provide leadership during constabulary responses. Core members serve defined terms established in the standing rules and rotate regularly. No member serves in the trained core indefinitely. *[Placeholder: core term lengths and rotation schedule to be established in standing rules.]*

**§13. Civic Complement.** The rotating civic complement is drawn from the general eligible membership of the Society by sortition. Service in the civic complement is a civic obligation of membership — the expression of the community's shared burden of safety. Complement members receive basic training from the College of the Charge before service. *[Placeholder: complement size, term length, training requirements, and grounds for deferral to be established in standing rules.]*

**§14. Training.** All constables — core and complement — shall receive training appropriate to their role. Training shall emphasize de-escalation above all other competencies, followed by community safety response, first aid, and communication. Training shall be developed and delivered by the College of the Charge. Training is not a one-time event — it is continuous for core members and refreshed for returning complement members.

**§15. Strict Rotation.** Rotation is among the most important structural protections in the Constabulary's design. No one builds a personal power base through a role they hold temporarily. Rotation shall be strictly enforced. The College of the Charge shall not extend terms or grant exceptions to rotation requirements except in genuine operational emergencies of defined maximum duration requiring Assembly notification.

## Article IV — Peacetime Operations

**§16. Peacetime Mandate.** In normal operating conditions the Constabulary Service's mandate is narrow. It responds to immediate threats to physical safety. It de-escalates dangerous situations. It calls for community backup when needed. It refers all non-safety matters to the College of Conciliation or the Assembly. It does nothing else. A Constabulary that is genuinely bored most of the time is a Constabulary that is doing its job — because the community's other institutions are handling what the Constabulary would otherwise be called to manage.

**§17. Response Protocol.** When responding to a situation the Constabulary shall: assess for immediate physical safety threats; attempt de-escalation as the primary response; call for community backup if needed; provide first aid if required; refer the matter to the College of Conciliation for disputes, conflicts, or conduct matters; refer to the Health Service for medical and mental health situations; and report the response to the College of the Charge and Assembly.

**§18. Referral Obligation.** The Constabulary shall refer situations to the appropriate service or body as its standard operating practice. Mental health crises go to the Health Service. Disputes go to the College of Conciliation. Conduct matters go to the accountability function. Community care situations go to the Community Care Service. The Constabulary is not the answer to every difficult situation — it is the first responder that stabilizes and refers.

**§19. Detention.** The Constabulary may detain a member only as an immediate safety necessity — when a member poses an imminent physical threat to themselves or others that cannot be managed through de-escalation alone. Detention is not arrest. It is temporary containment of an immediate safety threat. Any detention must be immediately reported to the College of the Charge, the Assembly, and the College of Conciliation. No detention shall last longer than is necessary to address the immediate safety threat.

**§20. Prohibited Functions.** The Constabulary Service is explicitly prohibited from: investigating crimes or conduct matters; prosecuting or adjudicating members; enforcing Assembly or Committee ordinances through coercion except where immediate physical safety is at stake; conducting surveillance of members; maintaining intelligence files on members; being deployed in political disputes or governance matters; and acting as an instrument of any College, Committee, or individual's authority over other members.

## Article V — Weapons Policy

**§21. Community Authorization.** The Constabulary Service's weapons policy is determined by the community through the Assembly, not by the Constabulary itself or the College of the Charge. What tools constables may carry, under what circumstances they may be deployed, and what use of force is authorized are community decisions. The Constabulary carries what the community sanctions and nothing more.

**§22. Default.** In peacetime operations the default constable is unarmed or minimally armed as the Assembly determines appropriate for normal community safety response. The community shall not arm constables beyond what genuine peacetime safety needs require.

**§23. Community Armory.** Any weapons held by or available to the Constabulary Service are community property held in community trust. They are not personal property of constables. They are stored, maintained, and accounted for by the Constabulary Service under College of the Charge oversight and subject to review committee inspection. *[Placeholder: armory standards and access protocols to be established by Assembly resolution.]*

**§24. Use of Force.** Any use of force by a constable shall be reported immediately to the College of the Charge, the Assembly, and the Permanent Review Committee. Use of force shall be reviewed as a matter of course — not only when complaints are filed. The default presumption is that force requires justification. The constable bears the burden of demonstrating that de-escalation was attempted and exhausted before force was used.

## Article VI — Contingency Operations

**§25. Planning for Upheaval.** The Constabulary Service explicitly plans for circumstances in which external law enforcement is unavailable, unreliable, or actively hostile to the community's interests. This is not an extreme scenario to be dismissed — it is a realistic contingency that the community's history and current trajectory make necessary to address directly. A community that plans for this contingency before it arrives is a community that can navigate it. A community that does not plan is at the mercy of improvisation under pressure.

**§26. Contingency Declaration.** The contingency tier of Constabulary operations is activated by Assembly declaration that external law enforcement is unavailable, unreliable, or presenting a threat to community safety. The Assembly — not the Constabulary, not the College of the Charge — holds this declaration authority. The Constabulary does not self-activate expanded authority.

**§27. Expanded Mandate.** Under contingency declaration the Constabulary's mandate expands to include: protection of community assets including food reserves, energy infrastructure, medical stockpiles, and financial infrastructure; coordination of community response to external threats; maintenance of community order during acute crisis conditions; and inter-society safety coordination with other Ben Franklin Societies. The expanded mandate does not include any of the functions prohibited under peacetime operations — those prohibitions apply in contingency conditions with equal force.

**§28. Stricter Accountability Under Contingency.** Expanded authority requires stricter accountability, not looser accountability. Under contingency declaration the Constabulary shall report every significant action to the Assembly within a defined period. The Permanent Review Committee shall increase its review frequency during contingency operations. The Assembly shall review the contingency declaration regularly and shall lift it as soon as conditions permit.

**§29. Sunset.** Contingency authority automatically lapses after a period established in the standing rules unless the Assembly affirmatively renews it. The Assembly shall not allow contingency authority to persist by inertia. Each renewal requires a deliberate Assembly decision. *[Placeholder: contingency sunset period to be established in standing rules.]*

**§30. Absolute Prohibition.** Under no circumstances — peacetime or contingency — may the Constabulary Service be deployed as an instrument of political enforcement within the community. It may not be used to silence dissent, suppress opposition to Assembly decisions, protect incumbents from accountability, or enforce political conformity. This prohibition admits no exceptions. Any attempt to deploy the Constabulary for political purposes is a fundamental breach of community trust and shall be treated as such by the Permanent Review Committee and the Assembly.

**§31. Vulnerable Member Protection.** Contingency conditions historically create elevated risk for vulnerable members — women, elderly, disabled, and those without social connections. The Constabulary shall treat protection of vulnerable members as a priority function during contingency operations. The College of Conciliation and Community Care Service shall be actively involved in contingency planning to ensure vulnerable member protection is built into operations from the start.

**§32. Inter-Society Coordination.** The Constabulary Service shall maintain relationships with the constabulary services of other Ben Franklin Societies for mutual support during contingency conditions. A federation of communities that can coordinate safety responses is vastly more resilient than isolated communities each facing threats alone. Inter-society safety coordination shall be developed in ordinary times so that it functions reliably in extraordinary ones.

## Article VII — The Permanent Review Committee

**§33. Establishment.** The Permanent Review Committee is established as an independent oversight body for both the Constabulary Service and the College of Conciliation. It is permanent — it does not convene only when problems arise. It operates continuously, maintaining active oversight of the community's most sensitive institutions.

**§34. Composition.** The Permanent Review Committee is drawn by pure sortition from the general eligible membership of the Society. Members of the College of the Charge and the College of Conciliation are excluded from serving on the Committee. This exclusion is absolute — the institutions under review shall have no representation on the body reviewing them. No other exclusions apply.

**§35. Mandate.** The Permanent Review Committee's mandate is wide-reaching. It reviews the conduct of constables and conciliators. It investigates complaints from members. It conducts proactive reviews of constabulary and conciliation activity regardless of whether complaints have been filed. It examines patterns of conduct over time. It assesses whether the institutions under its oversight are fulfilling their mandates with integrity. It reports its findings to the Assembly.

**§36. Binding Authority.** The Permanent Review Committee's determinations regarding individual conduct and practice are binding. It may remove a constable from service. It may recommend the removal of a College member to the College concerned. It may mandate changes to constabulary or conciliation practice. Its findings regarding individual conduct require no Assembly ratification — they are binding on their own authority. Its recommendations regarding institutional structure are referred to the Assembly for action.

**§37. Access.** The Permanent Review Committee shall have full access to all Constabulary Service records, activity logs, use of force reports, and College of the Charge proceedings relevant to its oversight function. It shall have full access to College of Conciliation proceedings relevant to its oversight function subject to appropriate privacy protections for parties involved in specific matters. No records shall be withheld from the Committee.

**§38. Transparency.** The Permanent Review Committee's findings and determinations shall be made available to the membership. Proceedings involving specific individuals shall protect individual privacy to the degree consistent with public accountability — the Committee's overall findings and patterns of conduct are public even when specific case details are protected.

**§39. Community Override.** The community retains the right to review any Permanent Review Committee determination. A member aggrieved by a Committee determination may petition the community directly. The community's authority is supreme over all institutions including the Review Committee.

## Article VIII — Reporting and Accountability

**§40. Activity Reporting.** Every significant constabulary action shall be reported to the College of the Charge and the Assembly. Significant actions include all responses to safety situations, all uses of force, all detentions, all requests for community backup, and all referrals to other services or bodies. The Constabulary does not operate in opacity.

**§41. Annual Report.** The Constabulary Service shall publish an annual report covering: response activity by type and outcome; use of force incidents and review outcomes; detentions and their resolution; referrals to other services; training activity; civic complement participation; contingency planning activity; and inter-society coordination. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and the Permanent Review Committee and made available to all members.

**§42. Member Complaints.** Any member may bring a complaint about constabulary conduct to the Permanent Review Committee. The complaint process shall be accessible, safe, and free from retaliation. A member who files a good faith complaint shall face no adverse consequences from the Constabulary Service or the College of the Charge. Retaliation against complainants is a serious violation subject to the Committee's binding authority.

**§43. Assembly Oversight.** The Assembly retains full oversight authority over the Constabulary Service. It may direct, restructure, or dissolve the Service. It authorizes weapons policy, contingency declarations, and resource allocation. The College of the Charge manages the Service day to day but never beyond the bounds of Assembly authorization.
