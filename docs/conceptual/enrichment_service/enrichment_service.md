# The Ben Franklin Society
## Of [County Name]

### Foundational Document on the Enrichment Service
*Article XXI of the Bylaws*

---

## Preamble

A community that only survives together has not yet become a community. People need to enjoy being together — to laugh and celebrate and grieve and wonder in each other's company. Shared culture, shared celebration, shared art and music and story are what transform a collection of people with mutual obligations into something worth belonging to. The Enrichment Service exists because the Ben Franklin Society understands that joy is not a luxury and culture is not frivolous. In a time of stress and uncertainty, the occasions that bring people together in pleasure and meaning are among the most important things a community can create. Benjamin Franklin knew this. His junto was a place people wanted to be. So is this community.

---

## Article I — Nature and Mandate

**§1. Purpose.** The Enrichment Service exists to create, coordinate, and facilitate the cultural and social events that make community life worth living. It is a facilitator and coordinator, not a producer in the narrow sense — the creativity, performance, and expression come from the community's members; the Service provides the infrastructure, planning, and coordination that makes it possible for that creativity to reach the whole community.

**§2. Community Cohesion.** The Enrichment Service serves community cohesion as directly as any other service. Shared events create shared experience. Shared experience creates relationship. Relationship is the substrate of mutual aid. The Enrichment Service is not separate from the community's practical functions — it is one of the most important conditions for those functions to work.

**§3. Available Venues.** The Enrichment Service uses whatever venues exist within the community — community halls, churches, fairgrounds, school gymnasiums, open fields, parking lots, marketplaces, and such other spaces as can be made available. It does not require dedicated performance infrastructure. It makes events happen with what the community has.

**§4. Scope.** The Enrichment Service coordinates events across the full range of community cultural life — film screenings, theatrical performances, music, festivals, seasonal celebrations, sports, art exhibitions, lectures, storytelling, community meals, and such other events as bring members together in shared experience. No form of community cultural life is outside the Service's scope if it serves the community's enjoyment and cohesion.

**§5. Oversight.** The Enrichment Service is overseen by the Arts College — a peer body of practitioners in the arts, performance, event planning, and cultural production. The Arts College credentials event planners, performers, and cultural practitioners, maintains standards for community events, and ensures that the Enrichment Service reflects the community's cultural values and artistic vitality.

---

## Article II — Facilitation and Coordination

**§6. Facilitation Model.** The Enrichment Service's primary role is facilitation. When a group of members wants to put on a play, the Service helps them find a venue, promotes the performance, and handles logistics. When a musician wants to give a concert, the Service creates the occasion. When the community needs a harvest festival, the Service organizes it. The creativity comes from members; the Service makes it accessible to the whole community.

**§7. Member-Led Events.** The Enrichment Service shall actively support member-led events — performances, exhibitions, screenings, gatherings, and cultural expressions that originate with members rather than with the Service itself. Members are the source of the community's cultural life. The Service amplifies and supports what members create rather than substituting for it.

**§8. Service-Organized Events.** The Enrichment Service shall also organize events on its own initiative — particularly seasonal celebrations, community-wide festivals, and events that serve cohesion functions beyond what individual member initiative produces. The Service maintains a calendar of recurring community events that anchor the community's shared cultural life across the year.

**§9. Venue Coordination.** The Enrichment Service shall maintain knowledge of all available venues in the community and relationships with venue owners and managers that make rapid venue access possible. The Service shall coordinate venue use across events — managing schedules, ensuring access, and developing new venue relationships as the community's event calendar grows.

**§10. Promotion.** Events only serve community cohesion if members know about them and attend. The Enrichment Service shall actively promote community events — through the virtual marketplace, community announcements, direct outreach, and such other communication channels as reach the community's membership effectively. Promotion is as important as planning.

---

## Article III — Events and Programming

**§11. Film and Screening.** The Enrichment Service shall organize regular film screenings for the community — using available projection equipment and suitable venues. Film screenings are among the most accessible and widely enjoyed forms of community entertainment. They require minimal infrastructure and can be held almost anywhere.

**§12. Performing Arts.** The Enrichment Service shall support and coordinate theatrical performances, musical events, dance, spoken word, and such other performing arts as the community's members create and wish to share. Performing arts bring audiences and performers into live relationship with each other in ways no recorded medium replicates.

**§13. Festivals and Celebrations.** The Enrichment Service shall organize seasonal festivals and community celebrations — harvest festivals, seasonal markers, community anniversaries, and such other occasions as the community chooses to celebrate together. Festivals are the most powerful cohesion events the Service organizes — they draw the whole community together across its usual divisions and create shared memory that persists long after the event.

**§14. Sports and Games.** The Enrichment Service shall support community sports, games, and physical recreation — leagues, tournaments, pickup games, and such other organized physical activity as members enjoy together. Sport builds relationship, friendly competition, and community pride. The Service shall create occasions for it.

**§15. Visual Arts and Exhibitions.** The Enrichment Service shall support exhibitions of members' visual art — painting, sculpture, photography, craft, and such other visual expression as members create. Community art exhibitions make visible the creative life that exists within the community and give artists an audience among their neighbors.

**§16. Lectures and Learning.** The Enrichment Service shall organize public lectures, discussions, and learning events — drawing on the knowledge of College members, visiting speakers, and such other intellectual resources as are available. Intellectual life is part of community enrichment. The junto Franklin founded was as much about shared inquiry as shared entertainment.

**§17. Community Meals.** The Enrichment Service shall organize community meals — potlucks, shared feasts, celebrations around food — in coordination with the Food Service where appropriate. Eating together is among the oldest and most effective community bonding activities. The Enrichment Service shall create regular occasions for it.

**§18. Inclusivity.** Events shall be accessible to all members. The Enrichment Service shall plan events with the full range of the community in mind — events appropriate for families with children, for elderly members, for members with disabilities, and for members across the full spectrum of cultural backgrounds and interests. No member shall feel that the community's cultural life is not for them.

---

## Article IV — Artists, Performers, and Creative Workers

**§19. Compensation.** Artists, performers, musicians, event workers, and other creative contributors to the Enrichment Service's programming may be compensated in Franks through the Service budget. Creative work is work. The community's creative life depends on people who have developed skills and knowledge in the arts — compensating them makes creative work economically sustainable within the community and gives the Frank economy a creative dimension it would otherwise lack.

**§20. Arts College Credentialing.** The Arts College credentials practitioners in event planning, performance, production, and cultural curation. Credentialing is not a barrier to community artistic participation — any member can participate in and contribute to community cultural life. It is a recognition of those who have developed particular expertise in making community events happen well.

**§21. Local Artists First.** The Enrichment Service shall prioritize local artists and performers in its programming — members whose creative work deserves an audience among their own community. Local artistic talent is community cultural wealth. The Service shall make it visible and support it actively.

**§22. External Artists.** The Enrichment Service may bring external artists and performers to the community where this enriches community cultural life and where local capacity alone cannot provide sufficient variety. External cultural engagement connects the community to the broader world of human creativity. The Service shall pursue it as a complement to local talent, not a substitute for it.

---

## Article V — The Arts College

**§23. Nature.** The Arts College is a peer body of practitioners in the arts, performance, cultural production, and event planning. Its members have developed knowledge and skill in creating the occasions and expressions that make community life culturally rich. The College maintains standards, credentials practitioners, and provides the knowledge base that the Enrichment Service draws upon.

**§24. Breadth.** The Arts College encompasses the full range of artistic and cultural practice — visual arts, performing arts, music, film, craft, culinary arts, storytelling, event production, and such other creative disciplines as the community's cultural life includes. The arts are not a narrow category. The College reflects that breadth.

**§25. Cultural Stewardship.** The Arts College is a steward of the community's cultural life — not its controller. It does not determine what art is made or what culture is practiced. It supports, credentials, and maintains the conditions for cultural life to flourish. The community's culture belongs to its members. The Arts College serves it.

**§26. Local Tradition.** The Arts College shall actively preserve and develop the community's local cultural traditions — music, craft, story, food, and such other cultural expressions as have particular roots in the community's specific place and history. Local tradition is irreplaceable once lost. The College treats its transmission as a responsibility alongside its support for new creative work.

---

## Article VI — Coordination with Other Services

**§27. Food Service.** Community meals, harvest festivals, and food-centered events involve both the Enrichment Service and the Food Service. The two services shall coordinate on food provision for community events — the Enrichment Service plans and promotes, the Food Service provides food. Community events that celebrate the harvest are among the most powerful occasions the two services can create together.

**§28. Education Service.** The Enrichment Service and Education Service share interests in community intellectual and cultural life. Lectures, exhibitions, film screenings, and performances that serve educational as well as enrichment purposes shall be coordinated between the two services. The community school's performances and exhibitions are natural Enrichment Service events.

**§29. Commerce Service.** Festivals and community events create natural commercial occasions — craft markets, food vendors, local producers. The Enrichment Service shall coordinate with the Commerce Service on the commercial dimensions of community events, ensuring that events support the local Frank economy as well as community cohesion.

**§30. Health Service.** Community events and shared cultural life are protective factors for mental health and community wellbeing. The Enrichment Service shall coordinate with the Health Service's mental health function to ensure that community programming serves members who may be isolated, grieving, or struggling. Joy is therapeutic. The Enrichment Service provides it.

---

## Article VII — Inter-Society Cultural Exchange

**§31. Federation Culture.** The Ben Franklin Society federation is a community of communities — each with its own cultural life, its own artists, its own traditions. The Enrichment Service shall actively pursue cultural exchange with other societies in the federation — sharing performers, co-organizing festivals, exchanging cultural programs, and creating occasions that bring members of different societies together in shared celebration.

**§32. Federation Festivals.** The federation of Ben Franklin Societies may organize festivals and cultural events that draw members from multiple societies together. These inter-society events are among the most powerful expressions of federation solidarity available — shared celebration across community boundaries builds the relationships that make federation function in harder times.

---

## Article VIII — Transparency and Reporting

**§33. Annual Report.** The Enrichment Service shall publish an annual report covering: events organized and facilitated by type; attendance in aggregate; member-led events supported; artists and performers compensated; venue relationships developed; inter-society cultural exchange activity; and any significant developments in community cultural programming. The report shall be presented to the Assembly and made available to all members.

**§34. Community Input.** The Enrichment Service shall actively seek member input on what events and programming they want — what would bring them to a community event, what cultural life they wish the community had. Programming that reflects what members actually want to attend is programming that serves community cohesion. The Service shall not plan events in isolation from the community it serves.
